# What the Moons Keep — Master Outline

*Updated to reflect all changes through Chapter 15*

---

## PREMISE

Ten thousand years ago, four colony ships arrived at Gaius. Three made it into stable orbit. The fourth crashed in the eastern foothills. The colonists who survived the crash encountered the Chrysalis — a native Gaian organism that infected most of them, producing mutations that compounded across generations into what the people of Gaius now call "the gift." A small number of colonists carried a genetic immunity. Their descendants, the bloodline, have maintained a monopoly on the pre-crash technology ever since, framing their immune status as sacred service and hunting any uninfected people born outside their line.

The three ships still in orbit are the three moons. No one alive knows this. The religion that replaced the truth has been maintained for ten thousand years — partly through institutional suppression, partly through the simple erosion of memory, partly because the truth, once lost, is very hard to find again.

The fourth ship is still there. Buried under centuries of overgrowth in the eastern foothills. The disc Maren's father left her is a crew ID chip. It opens something at the crash site. The bloodline knows this, or suspects it, which is why they want her.

## THE WORLD

**Gaius** — Earth-like world, fully colonized by the Chrysalis organism over ten millennia. Three visible moons orbit (the ships). The eastern foothills contain the crash site of the fourth ship, which locals call "the hill that moves in the light" — its metallic hull catches sunlight at certain angles and appears to shift. No one goes there. Old songs say not to.

**The Chrysalis** — Native Gaian organism, not carried by the colonists. It entered them after landing, through soil, air, and water. The weak died. The rest changed. Changes compound across generations into extraordinary mutations. The organism also responds to belief and self-concept to some degree — what you believe you are shapes what the Chrysalis makes you. This is the source of the theology: the colonists' children needed a story for what happened to them, and the story and the organism reinforced each other.

**Mutations (sample):**
- Davan: bioluminescent skin (color-coded emotion: amber=fear, blue=calm, green-gold=recognition/happiness), developing empathic sense — currently reads rooms and people as emotional residue; becoming more acute
- Vane: enhanced smell (primary sensory data); bark-skin patches spreading from wrists up arms
- Sella: membrane-thin translucent skin, bones fully visible, past the threshold where the change simply is her
- Lera: translucent luminous arms, longer fingers, dark iridescent nails, gray-gold eyes — still in motion toward something
- Various: copper skin, reflective silver eyes, hooked fingers, enhanced tracking vision, additional joint in each finger

**The Bloodline** — Descendants of the immune crew. Genetically resistant to the Chrysalis. Maintain technology monopoly: the Wound-maker (energy weapon, used once at the Stilling of the Brace three hundred years ago, now mostly legend), orbital access, communications, and — crucially — the ability to interact with pre-crash objects. They believe only the bloodline can operate Founding-era technology. They are wrong about this, but have had ten thousand years to make the belief look like fact. They hunt uninfected people born outside their line not from cruelty but from genuine fear: a hollow person with a crew ID chip is a key they cannot control.

**The Restricted Collection** — New Plymouth archive, bloodline-access-only. Contains material about the pre-Chrysalis period that contradicts the standard account. The church and bloodline have cooperated on its management for longer than their official relationship would indicate. Renner spent twenty years mapping its edges from the outside.

**Bloom** — Refined from secretions of heavily mutated captive humans during sleep. Wafer form, almost no smell (unsettling absence). Effects: sensory expansion, dissolution of perceptual boundaries, communion with the immediate world, pattern recognition beyond normal capacity. Costs: bark-skin spreading, psychic contamination from the Bloom-giver (involuntary visions, ceiling fragment, copper-flower smell), progressive dependency. Vane uses it professionally for pheromonal note-reading — her files carry chemical records of the moment they were written, and Bloom lets her read the whole picture. She's been telling herself it's a tool for four years.

## CHARACTERS

### Maren
*17. Uninfected. From Ashwick hamlet.*

Three years of performing the Ritual of the Second Skin with a coal-dark palm to fake the Chrysalis mark. Her father left when she was young — she now understands he ran for the same reason she ran. Her mother knew about the deception and chose not to address it; Maren reframed this in the woodcutter's shelter from abandonment to a particular kind of protective love. She's a skilled herbalist trained by Delia, pragmatic, dry, with a simmering fury under the surface that runs quieter when she's depleted. She catalogues available resources. She has been calling herself hollow for three years and recently concluded that hollow is wrong — it's a word that belongs to someone else's story about her, not to her actual experience of herself.

She carries: her father's disc (crew ID chip, symbol on the edge, recess that receives something specific), her mother's comb, her knife, Ulla's letter from her father (he went east to the hill, the disc will open it, told this once and kept moving). She's been watching the moons for three years without knowing why it matters.

*Voice: compressed, watchful, dry edge, privately furious. Catalogues resources. Analytical about her own performance. Catches herself in old habits.*

### Davan
*20. Bioluminescent skin. Empathic mutation developing. From Amber Valley.*

Left home after the Chrysalis Rite because he needed to understand something he couldn't name yet. He carves when he's working something out — figures, symbols, shapes that arrive from the wood rather than being imposed on it. He carved a face on the road north while thinking about the girl the bloodline was searching for, without knowing why his hands made what they made. He's been watching the moons the way Maren has, finding the inner orbit symbol in everything, unable to stop carving it. His empathic sense reads emotional residue in rooms and spikes in proximity to strong feeling; it fired when he held Corvel's container. He knows it's there but hasn't named it or examined it directly.

He thinks outward with curiosity, connects through noticing, processes slowly and thoroughly. He has been in New Plymouth for approximately five weeks. He has Pip, a developing relationship with Edvar, and his first meeting with the cooperage group behind him. He sent the pine carving to Maren via Pip.

*Voice: open-grained, observational, processes by circling. Connects to people and places through noticing. Half-thoughts he doesn't force to conclusion.*

### Aldric
*53. Keeper at the Temple of Confluence.*

Thirty years of institutional life, thirty years of careful honesty within it. He found the sealed chamber with the face (a flat dark screen that responds to touch and shows an orbital display — four shapes around a blue-green world, one irregular and damaged). He has been lying to everyone about it for two months and finding the lying increasingly difficult to reconcile with his self-concept. Brennan's letter confirmed the astronomical interpretation of the margin notation (four to three — something changed in the sky) and warned him he's being watched. Prioress Crane has been conducting a doctrinal review at his temple; she asked about the eastern wing's architecture and pointedly did not ask about the undercroft, which Aldric finds more unsettling than a direct question. He is now riding east to Greenford to meet Brennan and a surveyor who walked the eastern highlands forty years ago and has not spoken about what he found.

Compulsive lamp-pinching tic — checks and extinguishes lamps even when they're already cold, callused fingertips. In the Greenford chapter, this tic will begin to change.

*Voice: formal precision masking parallel inner life. Long careful clauses. Institutional language deployed with increasing awareness of its inadequacy. Self-aware about his own lying.*

### Vane
*35. Enhanced smell. Bark-skin patches to mid-bicep. Bloom user.*

Professional bounty hunter for ten years. Walked away from a bloodline contract for reasons she has not fully articulated to herself, though her nose has been telling her something was wrong with this one since before she found Maren's kitchen trace. She is now traveling with Maren toward New Plymouth and beyond, which is not a thing she planned. She delivered people to Noble Quarter addresses before and collected her fees and moved on; the fact that she can no longer do this is a problem she has not finished working out. Her Bloom dependency is progressing: involuntary visions (the Bloom-giver's underground ceiling, copper-flower smell), bark spreading. She knows the Bloom supply chain involves captive humans and has been filing this for years.

She reads the world primarily through smell — fear is cold-metal, protectiveness is warm-particular, the Chrysalis baseline warmth is an unconscious filter she didn't know she was using until it was absent. Maren has no baseline. The absence keeps drawing her nose back.

*Voice: clipped noir register. Short sentences. World as cost/yield/information. Interiority rationed. Professional categories keep things clean — or used to.*

### Supporting Characters

**Pip** — 23, docks district, New Plymouth. Mutation: finds things through accurate intuition about where they're likely to be. Knows the city as layered text. Connected to Edvar and the cooperage group. Vouched for Davan. Now acts as intermediary to reach Maren.

**Edvar** — 60s, mapmaker and collector, Crafting Quarter. Has the face (a second one, found in a collapsed building twenty years ago). Has two maps with the margin notation (three and four). Has been working the question for twenty years alone. Takes three Thursdays to decide Davan is worth showing the maps. Has not yet shown Davan the face.

**Renner** — ~50, former temple archivist. Runs the cooperage group. Aggressive precision, stops apologizing for knowing more than everyone else. Has been arguing the astronomical interpretation of the notation for four years. Running disagreement with Asha about whether the Restricted Collection's account of bloodline capacity can be used as evidence at all.

**Asha** — ~30, glassblower. Quieter than Renner but not softer. Has the craftsperson's directness of assessment. Argues that the bloodline's account of their own abilities is circular evidence. Her position: the gap in the record is real and the question of who else might interact with Founding-era objects has never been tested because no one had reason to test it.

**Corvel** — tanner, square-built, measures words in public. Has a sealed pre-crash container — separate crash debris (resolved Ch. 19: found in marshland south of the ridge, half-buried in clay, during Quiet Century geological surveys). Same material as the face. Brought it to the group eight years ago. Nobody has been able to open it. Davan felt something from it.

**Brennan** — Aldric's old seminary friend, now Greenford scholar of old languages and material culture. Sent eight-page letter: Fathers' Boats tradition older than the Chrysalis theology, four boats with the fourth's people as apparent source of the Chrysalis (this is the sophisticated-but-wrong scholarly conclusion — it inverts the true causation but points in the right direction). Warning: someone has been watching him ask questions. Wants Aldric to come in person.

**Prioress Crane** — doctrinal reviewer from New Plymouth office. Methodical, expressionless, asks architectural questions about the eastern wing and pointedly doesn't ask about the undercroft. Her agenda remains unclear — threat or bureaucrat or both.

**Horst** — Vane's former handler. Now in New Plymouth personally, Merchant Quarter, two men, looking for Maren. His presence means the contract is considered high-priority at the bloodline's domestic office level.

**Lera** — met Davan on the barge to New Plymouth. Translucent luminous arms, longer fingers, gray-gold eyes. Offered the map-is-wrong-but-useful framing of Chrysalis theology. Said: "your mutation will become what you believe it is."

**Betta** — farm wife north of the crossroads village. Helped Maren. Told the bloodline's agents nothing. Told Vane north and east.

## THE TRUTH (what no character yet fully knows)

The Chrysalis is native to Gaius. It was here before the colonists arrived. It entered them through the world — soil, air, water — and changed most of them. The immune survivors (the bloodline's ancestors) could still operate the pre-crash technology because their bodies hadn't been restructured by the Chrysalis. This gave them a functional monopoly. Over ten thousand years, that functional monopoly became a claim of sacred right, the claim became doctrine, and the doctrine was maintained through the Restricted Collection and the hunting of uninfected people born outside the bloodline.

The fourth ship is partially intact underground. Its systems are dormant but not dead. The crew ID chip (Maren's disc) was designed to be recognized by the ship's systems — not by bloodline genetics. The bloodline has been operating on the assumption that only they can interact with pre-crash technology; they've never tried a hollow person because they've never had reason to. They've been wrong for ten thousand years.

The three ships in orbit are still crewed by the long-dead descendants of those who stayed. The ships are waiting, as they have always waited. What they're waiting for is either a signal from the crashed fourth ship, or confirmation that the colony survived, or both. The counting rhyme's *"the moons are waiting"* is literally true.

Brennan's sophisticated-but-wrong conclusion (that the Chrysalis came from the fourth ship's people) points in the right direction while inverting the causation. The correction — that the world entered the people, not the other way around — is the central revelation of Act Three.

## ACT STRUCTURE

### ACT ONE — Chapters 1–10
*Each character in motion, alone. The net tightening. First convergence.*

The act establishes four separate worlds and sets each character moving toward the same destination by different roads. It ends with Maren and Vane meeting and the act of joining being chosen rather than forced.

### ACT TWO — Chapters 11–22
*Convergence in New Plymouth. The pieces finding each other. The hill becoming necessary.*

Characters arrive in New Plymouth carrying separate fragments of the same question. The city is large enough to move in but the net is closing. By the end of the act, the four characters are knowingly pointed at the same destination: the hill east of the city, the crash site, the thing the disc opens.

### ACT THREE — Chapters 23–30
*The hill. What's inside it. The truth and what it costs.*

The journey east. The opening. The revelation. The confrontation with what the bloodline has been protecting — and what it means for everyone who believed a story built on ten thousand years of managed forgetting.

## CHAPTER OUTLINE

### WRITTEN — ACT ONE

**Chapter 1 — Maren — "The Ritual of the Second Skin"**
Ashwick hamlet, Autumnal Rite. Maren's three-year coal-pouch deception is exposed when the pouch shifts during the ceremony. Deacon Ferris watches. That night she overhears "bloodless" and "bloodline has asked to be told." Memory of father: lamp oil and metal scent, hands turning small bright mechanism by firelight. She packs and leaves north under the three moons — transitioning from hollow to wanting to know why.

**Chapter 2 — Davan — "The Color of Hunger"**
Three weeks on the road north from Amber Valley. Nearly walks into armed men searching for a young woman, dark hair, no visible change. His fear-light flares amber under his shirt. Waypoint inn: merchants discuss the bloodline's "quiet word." He reflects on Chrysalis theology. Carves unconsciously — the face arrives turned as if listening. Thinks about the loneliest thing: walking through the world with nothing showing.

**Chapter 3 — Aldric — "What Lies Under the Stone"**
Dying pilgrim Sella's last words: "The moons are not moons. They are boats. Boats with the dead still inside." Workers find sealed chamber in the undercroft — five objects. The face responds to touch: cold light, star charts, orbital arrangements. Blue-green world with FOUR shapes orbiting. One irregular, damaged. Archive note: Restricted Collection, bloodline approval required. Lamp-pinching tic established throughout.

**Chapter 4 — Vane — "The Flavor of the Contract"**
Vane closes a skip, takes Bloom, experiences the communion of it. Bark-skin spreading to mid-bicep. Contract arrives from Horst: young woman, no visible change, enormous fee, Noble Quarter address. Pheromonal shorthand system introduced. Overhears supplier Cade: "three months before new one's producing at volume, if she survives the first month." Takes the contract.

**Chapter 5 — Maren — "North Is the Safest Direction"**
Four days on the road. Two mistakes, one good decision. She catches herself offering the burn-story lie to Betta and stops: "It's not a burn. There's nothing wrong with the arm." First honest exchange in three years. Examines the disc for the first time as a question rather than an inheritance. Sees bloodline agents questioning a farmer, goes wide. Boundary stone: "all who carry the gift are under protection." The hollow is not in her — it's in someone's plan for her.

**Chapter 6 — Davan — "Confluence"**
Arrives at Confluence city. Temple: the symbol above the door looks different — orbital, not spiral. Can't articulate what he's seeing. River barge north to New Plymouth. Meets Sable. "What if the Founders were just people?" Sable: the map is wrong but useful — question is whether it's wrong enough to send you off a cliff. Night on barge: his skin-light pulses blue-calm in sleep. Half-asleep thought: three is a number that implies a missing element. Wound-maker story introduced as layered oral tradition.

**Chapter 7 — Aldric — "The Investigator"**
Three weeks of lying. Prioress Crane arrives for doctrinal review. The face shows more detail: burning point at center, blue-green world third from it, FOUR shapes in orbit, one irregular. He thinks: damaged. Writes to Brennan in scholarly obliqueness. Hears the archive door late at night. Lamp tic throughout — at the crucial moment of counting the fourth shape, he pinches out his own lamp.

**Chapter 8 — Vane — "Trail"**
Twelve days behind Maren. Finds the kitchen — no fear-smell, just alert/watchful. Revises model: the girl is capable. Meets Corvin, reads his protectiveness, works around it rather than through it. Betta holds silence longer than most, eventually confirms north and east. The ankle injury trace. Involuntary Bloom vision: ceiling, stone, underground, stationary. KEY DISCOVERY: the girl has no Chrysalis baseline warmth. Absence like a tongue finding a gap where a tooth was. "Two days behind, maybe less."

**Chapter 9 — Maren — "The Hollow"**
Woodcutter's shelter, ankle worsening, food gone, second day. Defenses worn down by depletion. Thinks about her mother — the silence as protective love rather than abandonment. Thinks about her father as someone who ran so they'd follow him and not stay. Examines the disc with proper attention for the first time: markings not decorative but structural, recess designed to receive something. Pressing her thumb changes nothing. The hollowness is not emptiness — it's a fullness called the wrong thing. She wants to know who decided it and why. Walks north into the long sunset.

**Chapter 10 — Maren/Vane — "The Wrong House"**
Maren finds Vane having a full Bloom contamination episode in the ditch-grass. Uses Delia's grounding technique: a rope thrown into water. Gives Vane Corvin's carving to hold. Vane comes back. Looks at Maren and something moves in her face — recognition that is quickly managed. "You could have walked past me." "I could have." Vane names herself and the contract. Maren: "Was." Vane gives her the ford crossing and the east gate warning, walks north. Maren follows within thirty seconds. Vane does not let this show.

### WRITTEN — ACT TWO (opening)

**Chapter 11 — Vane — "Uncontracted"**
Vane turns around a mile north — the walking-away was theater. Returns, falls into step with Maren. Sets the ankle at a stream. Admits she's closed contracts to Noble Quarter addresses before and collected the fees. Maren: "What happened to those people?" Vane says nothing. Stillwater: the uncle is dead two years, Ulla gives Maren a coat and a letter from the uncle. Letter is from Maren's father — went east to the hill ten years ago, the disc will open it, told this once and left. Letter was already opened when it arrived. Vane names the threat clearly: they know about the disc specifically. Two absences — the wafer, and the girl with no baseline. She walks north and doesn't wait.

**Chapter 12 — Davan — "New Plymouth"**
Davan finds dock work. Meets Pip. Carves the symbol obsessively. Tells Pip about it. Pip names Edvar. First visit to Edvar: brings his carving, Edvar tests him, shows one map margin (the number four), says "come back when you've been here longer." Davan accepts this as correct. The dock meeting: he's carving in driftwood when Maren sits nearby. Clocks her. Something wrong in the way of a word he knows but can't reach. She shows him the disc edge — the symbol. He says his name. She says hers. Neither says much else. He becomes aware of the pine carving's weight in his pocket and does not take it out.

**Chapter 13 — Aldric — "What the Restricted Collection Restricts"**
Brennan's letter: eight pages. First half — Fathers' Boats tradition older than Chrysalis theology, four boats, fourth's residue apparently spreading as the Chrysalis (sophisticated-but-wrong scholarly conclusion; inverts the true causation but points the right direction). Second half breaks scholarly register entirely: someone with bloodline domestic office connections has been watching Brennan ask questions. Come to Greenford. Don't write. The old maps' margin notation: three, four, or one — a count of something fixed relative to the world. Aldric doesn't state the interpretation aloud. Thinks it. Crane asked about the eastern wing's architecture and did not ask about the undercroft — more unsettling than a direct question. He writes to Cael, seals the letter in his coat, rides east at first light. Crane watches him leave. He doesn't look back.

**Chapter 14 — Maren — "Stillness as a Skill"**
New Plymouth, eight days in. Vane's paranoia is real and present — the room, the window, the negotiated perimeter. Maren tests it on day four, comes back by a different route, earns expanded range by accounting for every choice. Vane tells Maren what she saw at the dock — she was the woman at the mooring post. She read Davan: not from the city, came north without a plan, not bloodline, has the symbol. Day six: Horst is in the city, Merchant Quarter, two men. His presence means high-priority. Vane has already found the cooperage yard with the gate on the far side. Two days before Maren can meet Davan again. Maren accepts this. They spend the evening on the eastern road maps.

**Chapter 15 — Davan — "The Cooperage Basement"**
Third Edvar visit — two maps shown, three and four, a thing changed in the sky. Edvar takes him to the cooperage group that evening. Renner: aggressive precision, challenges the cartographer before the meeting settles. Running argument about the Irongate inscription (pre-Chrysalis vs. contemporary; this determines whether the symbol was absorbed or invented). Asha (glassblower): quieter, harder to move. Argument about whether the bloodline's account of what they can operate constitutes circular evidence. Corvel's container comes out. Davan feels the proximity-warmth when he holds it; says nothing. Renner: "We cannot build on a gap." Asha: "The gap is there." Davan's skin-light flares amber once and he presses it down. Afterward: Pip tells him a woman went to Edvar's shop — dark hair, road-worn, Stillwater coin, knew what she was asking. Davan sends the pine carving as his message. Sets Thursday at the cooperage yard.

### TO BE WRITTEN — ACT TWO (continued)

**Chapter 16 — Maren — "Thursday"**
The pine carving arrives via Pip's network. Maren holds it and doesn't say anything for a long time. Vane watches her face. Maren's decision to go to the cooperage yard is made without drama — she recognizes the gesture and what it means about who sent it. Vane insists on the yard's sight lines and the exit gate and a prearranged signal. The meeting: Davan and Maren in the cooperage yard. The conversation they haven't been able to have yet. The disc on the table, the symbol, the hill. Davan's four-and-three. They establish what each of them knows. Vane stays outside, near the gate. When Davan looks up at the end, she's there, and that first look between them is its own quiet thing.

**Chapter 17 — Aldric — "The Surveyor"**
Arrives in Greenford. Brennan's house: cluttered, collegial, the warmth of two men who have corresponded for twenty years and are meeting in person under bad circumstances. Brennan has more material than he put in the letter — visual documentation of the margin notation across twelve different maps, a timeline of the number's change. The surveyor: old, has said nothing about the eastern highlands for forty years, agrees to meet Aldric because a Keeper's rank carries weight he respects. He walked the hills east of New Plymouth four decades ago and found something wrong about a particular formation. Metallic. Old. The way the light caught it at dusk. He didn't go back. He's been not-going-back ever since. Aldric rides west toward New Plymouth with a felt distance in his chest and Brennan's warning still running.

**Chapter 18 — Vane — "The Bloom-Giver"**
Vane's Bloom supply is running low. She knows a contact in the docks district. The purchase requires going to a specific address — and what she finds there is what she's been filing for years: the room, low stone ceiling, the source. What Vane does with this information is the chapter's question. She doesn't free the person. She doesn't purchase the wafer. She stands in the street outside for a long time and then goes back to the room and tells Maren what's in her pack, what it's cost, and what she's decided. The wafer tin stays in her pack for now. The decision has been made but not acted on.

**Chapter 19 — Davan — "What Edvar Keeps"**
Edvar shows Davan the face. It responds to touch — the orbital display, four shapes around a blue-green world, the fourth smaller, as if seen from further away or as if it had always been the most distant. (INTENTIONAL DISTINCTION from Aldric's face in Ch. 3 / Ch. 7 where the fourth shape reads as irregular/damaged — Edvar's and Aldric's faces are the same data from different viewing positions in the debris field. Ch. 25 reconciles them. Drafters of Ch. 24-25 must preserve this distinction.) Davan's skin-light goes green-gold, sustained, the recognition-register. He counts four. "It came down." Edvar has sat alone with this implication for twenty years; he has never let himself complete the thought. Davan completes it for him. They sit with what it implies for the rest of the evening. Davan asks about the container — whether it could be from the same source. Edvar looks at him with the expression of someone who has been waiting for someone to ask that question.

**Chapter 20 — Maren — "The Restricted Collection"**
Renner. Maren is brought to the group by Davan — properly this time, vouched for, with the disc. The argument about the bloodline's capacity to operate Founding-era objects reaches Maren directly: Renner's position that no hollow person has ever been tested. Maren puts the disc on the table. The room goes quiet. This is not an argument anymore. This is a test with someone in it. The chapter ends with the disc on the table and everyone looking at it, and Maren understanding for the first time the full weight of what she's been carrying.

**Chapter 21 — Aldric — "Convergence"**
Aldric arrives in New Plymouth. He goes to the temple district for lodging and finds, through a chain of his own careful connections, that a Keeper traveling east on a scholarly consultation has been noted by someone in the New Plymouth office. Crane's reach extends further than the Confluence. He goes to the docks district instead. Through Brennan's letter reference to the hill and the surveyor's description, he finds the cooperage — or finds Edvar first, who recognizes the quality of his questions and brings him in. The group's first meeting with all four principals: Maren, Davan, Aldric, and Vane at the perimeter.

**Chapter 22 — Vane — "What the Contract Was For"**
Horst closes in. Vane's professional read on his search pattern tells her they have days, possibly less. She makes the decision she's been working toward since Chapter 18: she goes back to the Bloom-giver's location and does what she didn't do the first time. This is the chapter where the bark-skin begins to recede — not because freeing the Bloom-giver magically heals her, but because she's stopped feeding the dependency. The withdrawal begins. She tells Maren: we leave tomorrow. The hill is five days east. They know what they're going toward. Act Two closes with all four characters pointed at the same destination.

### TO BE WRITTEN — ACT THREE

**Chapter 23 — Maren — "East"**
Departure from New Plymouth: Maren, Davan, Aldric, Vane. The ford crossing, not the east gate. The first day of travel together — four people who have been moving toward the same question from different directions, now moving together and finding out what that costs. Vane is managing the beginning of withdrawal and managing it privately. Aldric is the eldest and the most institutionally formed and has to find his footing in a group that operates on different principles. Maren and Davan have established their working rhythm; the others are new variables. The chapter is about the texture of the group forming.

**Chapter 24 — Davan — "The Country Changes"**
The landscape east of New Plymouth. The eastern highlands, the contour lines thickening toward Irongate country. Davan's empathic sense reads the country differently from the city — less human noise, more of something older underneath, a quality he doesn't have words for. He shows Aldric the disc. Aldric looks at it for a long time and says: the inner orbit symbol. I've been counting what it describes for months. He tells them about the face at the Confluence. Davan tells him about Edvar's face. Two faces. Aldric's face was in the undercroft of a temple built on what appears to be, from Brennan's surveyor's account, a secondary debris field from the same crash.

**Chapter 25 — Aldric — "The Shape of Old Things"**
Camp at the edge of Irongate country. Aldric and Davan compare what they've each seen on the two faces. The orbital display: same data, slightly different perspective, as if the two units were positioned differently when they recorded. Aldric's face showed the fourth moon as irregular; Davan's showed it as simply smaller, as if seen from further away. The implication: the face at the Confluence was from a different part of the debris field. There is more down there. This is the chapter where Aldric begins to understand that his thirty years of institutional honesty is going to require him to become a specific kind of disruptive presence rather than a steward of questions. The lamp tic is almost gone.

**Chapter 26 — Vane — "Withdrawal"**
The third day east. Vane's withdrawal is no longer private — she's managing a physiological process that has symptoms, and the group is too close for symptoms to be invisible. Maren handles it the way she handled the ditch: practically, herbally, present without making it about herself. Davan's empathic sense reads the withdrawal as a distinct quality and has to manage his own response to it. Vane at her most stripped-down: without the Bloom, she still has her nose, and her nose is telling her they're being followed. Someone from Horst's team. One person. A professional. She tells the group. They adjust.

**Chapter 27 — Maren — "The Hill"**
First sight of the hill. The metallic catch of old light through centuries of overgrowth. The specific wrongness of a shape that is not geological — too regular, too deliberate, even under vines and root systems and sediment. The disc in Maren's hand. The symbol on its edge, and the same symbol carved somewhere in the hillside — not carved, she realizes. Part of the surface. The approach: Vane tracking the follower, Aldric reading the structure with the knowledge of someone who has been looking at two separate pieces of it for months, Davan's proximity-sense firing at a sustained level that he can no longer contain. When Maren presses the disc into the recess in the hillside's surface, something happens. The chapter ends there.

**Chapter 28 — Davan — "What Was Inside"**
The ship. Partially accessible — one section of hull breached by the crash and subsequently sealed by the hillside's growth, now reopened. What they find inside: not treasure, not weapons, not the bloodline's feared key-to-power. Documentation. Records. The ship's log. Ten thousand years of waiting, sealed in storage that the Chrysalis could not reach because the hull material resisted it. The records tell the truth: a native organism, a crash landing, an immune minority who saw what they had and built a story around it. Davan's empathic sense reads something from the intact storage systems — not human emotion but its oldest ancestor, the long patience of something that has been waiting to be found. His skin-light goes to a color he's never produced before.

**Chapter 29 — Aldric — "The Record"**
Aldric reads. He is, of the four of them, the one most equipped to read what the ship's records contain and understand what it means institutionally, theologically, historically. This is what thirty years of careful scholarship was for. The chapter is slow and large: what the record says, what it means for the Chrysalis theology, what it means for the bloodline's claim, what it means for ten thousand years of people who built their lives on a story that was wrong. He is not angry. He is very quiet. He thinks about the face in the undercroft at the Confluence, and the lamp he keeps pinching out of habit, and the thirty years of institutional honesty, and what honesty requires of him now.

The follower from Horst's team has found them. The chapter ends with them becoming aware of this.

**Chapter 30 — All — "What the Moons Keep"**
The confrontation. Not the bloodline's full force — one agent, one choice, the moment where the four characters have to decide what they're going to do with what they've found. The records are not something that can be un-found. Vane's professional assessment of the tactical situation, Maren's decision about the disc, Aldric's decision about the institutional implications, Davan's decision about what he's willing to do with what his body has been trying to tell him for the entire novel.

The ending does not resolve everything. The truth exists now; it has been found; it cannot be made un-found. Three moons are in orbit. They are still waiting. The question of what they're waiting for has an answer now, and the answer is: the signal from the fourth ship, which Maren's disc has just sent. What happens next is not this book's story.

The last image: the three moons, full-bright together over the eastern hills, and the quality that the Tidemark harbor-master described — the old patient grief that doesn't belong to you personally but is yours anyway because it's been handed down so many times it's worn itself into the shape of something you were born knowing. Maren looks up at them for the first time in the novel. She has been avoiding them for three years. She looks up.

## THEMATIC THREADS

**Truth vs. useful lies** — The map is wrong but useful; the question is whether it's wrong enough to send you off a cliff. Every character is working with an incomplete account of something that matters. The novel is about what happens when the map turns out to be wrong enough.

**What you're told you are vs. what you actually are** — Hollow is wrong. The bloodline's account of what the unchanged can and cannot do is wrong. The Chrysalis theology is wrong. All three are wrong in the same direction: they restrict people to categories that serve someone else's interests.

**Institutions and the truths they manage** — The church's Restricted Collection, the bloodline's succession rites, Aldric's thirty years of careful honesty within a structure that turns out to contain a structural dishonesty. What you owe an institution and what the institution owes you.

**Mutations as self-made-real** — Lera's map: the story is the envelope, what's inside matters. The Chrysalis responds to belief. This is not magic — it's biological — but it means that ten thousand years of people believing they were gifts and gifts only has shaped what the Chrysalis has produced.

**The cost of seeing clearly** — Aldric's institutional crisis, Vane's contamination, Maren's exposure, Davan's empathic bleed. Each character is paying for the clarity they're arriving at. The novel does not suggest the cost was too high.

**Four moons, three moons** — The missing fourth is the crash, the truth, the starting point of everything. Counting to four is the novel's recurring gesture: Aldric counting on the face, Davan counting the margin notation, the children's rhyme (*four fires in the sky, one came down to die*), the prologue fragments disagreeing about what arrived and what remained.

**Absence as information** — Maren has no Chrysalis baseline. The wafer has almost no smell. The Restricted Collection's gaps are shaped. Vane's professional read on the world depends on recognizing what isn't there. The truth is partly a story about what was removed.

## WORLD NOTES AND CONSISTENCY FLAGS

- **Chrysalis origin**: Native to Gaius. Entered the colonists after landing. Not carried by the colonists. Brennan's letter has this inverted (sophisticated-but-wrong scholarship) — this is intentional and should not be corrected in the text. The correction is Act Three.
- **"Solar" / Earth-specific language**: Avoid. Use "burning point at center," "star," "the sun of this system."
- **The face**: Named consistently as "the face" (surface that looks back, produces light from within). Not "the panel."
- **Three objects in the sealed chamber**: The face was one of five objects found. Four smooth containers and one flat dark object. The four containers' contents and fate require confirmation before later chapters.
- **Corvel's container**: RESOLVED — separate crash debris from the marshland south of the ridge, NOT the fifth Confluence chamber object. Same material as the face. Debris field is wider than the cooperage group knows, which is itself a discovery.
- **"Careful"**: Overused across all chapters. Flag for manuscript pass.
- **"Not quite a smile" / "corner of Vane's mouth"**: Used three times across Chapters 10 and 14. Flag for manuscript pass.
- **Vane's bark-skin**: Spreading to mid-bicep at Chapter 4. Should not regress during withdrawal; the regression is gradual and post-withdrawal.
- **Davan's carving of Maren's arm**: The pine figure has one arm held close to the body in the guarded posture. He carved it on the road north before meeting her. His hands knew the shape of her gesture. This thread must pay off when she sees the carving in Chapter 16.
- **The disc's recess**: Designed to receive something specific. A finger doesn't work. The ship's surface has a corresponding recess. When Maren presses the disc into it, the signal sends. This is the mechanism — not a key in the conventional sense but a paired authentication object.
- **Aldric's lamp tic**: Established in Chapter 3, present throughout his chapters, beginning to change in Chapter 25 (nearly gone), effectively absent by Chapter 29. Track this.
