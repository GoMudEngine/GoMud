## 1. Geographic & Travel-Time Audit

**Five-day eastern journey — consistent and internally coherent.**
Ch. 22 (Vane) establishes "the hill is five days east." The
day-count across Chs. 23–27 tracks cleanly: Ch. 23 = Day 1 (ford
crossing, first camp at sheep-fold); Ch. 24 = Day 2 (ash-stand
camp); Ch. 25 = Day 3 (through Irongate, eastern heath camp);
Ch. 26 = second watch on Day 3 / first light Day 4 with the
ash-lee camp again — wait. This is a minor seam worth flagging.

**Day-count seam at Chs. 25–26.** The Irongate passage (Ch. 25) ends
at the shepherd's fold "an hour past the eastern edge of Irongate
country" — Day 3 confirmed by Vane's note "day three symptom per
Cade's old chart" (Ch. 26). But Ch. 25 frames the camp as being
"at the ash-stand" while Ch. 26 opens at "the ash-lee" on second
watch, consistent. The party then breaks camp and travels through
Irongate before reaching the shepherd's fold — this is still Day 3.
Ch. 27 opens on Day 5 ("Day five opened in a light that did not
match the day"). Day 4 is not assigned a chapter-heading POV. This
is structurally intentional but the chapter-event table in Section 6
of the bible maps Ch. 26 to "Day 3 east" and Ch. 27 to "The hill" —
no Day 4 chapter is listed. **No contradiction; Day 4 is elided
within Ch. 26's compressed timeline. Audit clean.**

**Cart geography — "ford" not "ridge."** Ch. 22 closing reads: "the
ford crossing, not the east gate" (line ~5430). Ch. 23 delivers the
ford crossing in full. The bible specified Ch. 22 should now say
"ridge" not "ford" in the cart geography reference. **The text says
"ford" throughout Ch. 22 and Ch. 23.** The bible's note says: *"cart
geography (Ch. 22 should now say 'ridge' not 'ford')"*. Looking at
the actual text, the ford is a river ford (plank walkway, pilings,
gravel tongues, woman washing), and the ridge is a separate feature
the road climbs after crossing. These are two distinct geographic
elements. "Ford" is the river crossing; "ridge" is the highland
approach. The bible's note appears to reference an earlier draft
in which these were conflated. The current text correctly
distinguishes them. **No contradiction in the manuscript; however,
if the bible's correction directive was meant to change "ford" to
"ridge" in some specific sentence, that sentence has not been
located. Flag for author clarification — not marked as error.**

**Convergence at the crash site.** The group's approach in Ch. 27 is
coherent: cart left at the stone shoulder with Edvar, four
characters approach on foot, half-hour climb, recess located at
the southeastern end of the hill as Reth described. Geometry
consistent with Section 3 bible canon.

**Vane's deception plan.** Ch. 30 Vane section: the contract man is
given the falsified report *"signature did not register"* per bible
canon. The agent accounts (5 total: 1 captured at hill, 1 on
valley road, 2 with Horst in New Plymouth, 1 in Stillwater) match
exactly. **Clean.**

**"Year of her scarring" — Vane Ch. 30.**

adversarial sample review from V1 flagged this exact phrase:

event in her own timeline. The V1 sample flag was about Maren's

**Withdrawal timeline — Vane says "three days to four" before flat

any clinical chart. **Severity: Minor.** The manuscript has softened
"flat" to mean "low-functional" rather than non-functional, which
is arguably a character-consistent recalibration given Vane's
professional discipline. Flag as intent-check for the author: does
Vane's D5 performance exceed the stated withdrawal ceiling?

## 4. Lore / Canon Contradictions

**D4 bio-lock passage — "unchanged" vs. "native-baseline"
distinction. Ch. 29 key passage.**
Bible D4 (Maren's case, locked): "Passive environmental Chrysalis
presence is universal on Gaius — even a true Hollow (genetically
uninfected, never underwent the Rite) accumulates cellular
Chrysalis through seventeen years of breathing Gaian air...
Immunity (bloodline: organism present but inactive) and native-
baseline (Maren: organism never hosted by her genome) are
different axes — but both result in passive cellular integration
over a lifetime on Gaius. The bio-lock catches Maren for the same
reason it catches the bloodline."

Ch. 29 (Aldric reads the footnote, lines ~10163–10183):
"The holds could not be opened by any living person on Gaius,
because every living person on Gaius carried the Chrysalis
integrated into their cell-structure, even those — the bloodline
by direct lineage, **Maren by the long quiet of seventeen years
on a world saturated with the organism** — whose immune systems
had kept the active organism out. The organism had been present
across the population for ten thousand years; the bloodline's
descendants were not the same pre-organism baseline their
ancestors had been, even if those descendants were immune."

**The clarifying clause about Maren's mechanism is present.**
The text says "Maren by the long quiet of seventeen years on a
world saturated with the organism" — correctly distinguishing her
from the bloodline ("by direct lineage") while grouping both
under the bio-lock. However, there is a subtle lore tension: the
passage says the bloodline's "immune systems had kept the active
organism out" — this conflates Maren's mechanism (never hosted by
genome = native-baseline) with the bloodline's mechanism (immune
= organism present but inactive). The parenthetical reads as if
both groups have immune systems keeping the active organism out,
which is accurate for the bloodline but imprecise for Maren,
whose distinction per D4 is that the organism was "never hosted by
her genome." The passage collapses the two axes ("immunity" and
"native-baseline") into a single immune-system framing.

**Severity: Minor.** The clause about Maren is present and
correctly routes her under the bio-lock. The imprecision is in
the shared parenthetical framing that could be read as "same
mechanism" when D4 is explicit they are "different axes." This
is a nuance error, not a flat contradiction. The bio-lock
conclusion (both caught) is correct. The mechanistic distinction
is fudged.

**Chrysalis biology — "native-resistant baseline" terminology.**
Ch. 29 uses "native-resistant baseline" (lines ~9973–9976):
"The office had a word for them. The word was on the right version
of the cell: *native-resistant baseline*." Bible Section 3 /

**The bloodline capacity inversion.** Ch. 29 establishes that

## 6. Logic & Adversarial Stress Tests

**Vane's nose-bridge bark — see Section 3.** The most significant

spread. Vane is inside the ship for neither chapter (she stays on

**The agent report — falsified account credibility.**
Vane offers the agent a falsified report: "signature did not
register." The agent's brief was: "Authenticate signature. Secure
the disc. Prefer the holder alive. Report before close." He was
mid-report when Vane stopped him. He did not authenticate (she
interrupted). His falsified report ("signature did not register")
is plausibly compatible with his position at the time of capture —
he had confirmed location but not yet transmitted authentication.
The threat ("I find you first... alone... at an hour of my
choosing") is credible given Vane's established competence. **No
logic gap.** The professional reading of the scene is clean.

**Four positions disagreement — does the text deliver four distinct
positions?**
Bible Section 3 Ch. 30 locked: Vane ("leave as found"), Aldric
("build the table"), Davan ("never open"), Maren ("open eventually,
for company"). Ch. 30 only partially delivers this. Maren's
closing interior (lines ~11081–11129) indicates: "The four of them
were going to disagree" and "The disagreement was going to be the
thing they made, together, instead of the decision." But the four
positions are not spoken or dramatized in Ch. 30 as distinct
utterances — the disagreement is *anticipated* rather than enacted.

**Severity: Minor.** The bible says "the book ends with the
disagreement held open." Ch. 30 does hold it open, but the four
distinct positions from the bible (Vane's "leave as found"; Davan's
"I am not going to ask you to be opened" — this one is explicit in
Ch. 30 Davan's interior) are not all externally rendered in the
final chapter. Davan's position is stated directly. Aldric's
Ch. 30 interior describes his "three things" and the channel-
building project — consistent with "build the table." Maren's
position ("open eventually, for company") is not stated in Ch. 30;
what appears is the captain's words that "the children will be a
people worth the coming" arriving in her, which is the motivational
source for her position, but the position itself is implied rather
than stated. Vane's position is not stated in Ch. 30 at all — only
her economy-of-motion carriage and "you stood angles for languages
that were not yours." **The four-position architecture is present
at the structural level but understated in delivery. The bible says
the closing image is "the disagreement held open" — the manuscript
delivers the anticipation of disagreement but not the full four-
voice statement. Whether this is a flaw or an artistic choice
(ending before the arguing begins) is a drafting question, not a
continuity error.**

**The disc stays seated — final image.**

11108): "She lifted her thumb. The disc stayed seated. The symbol

## 8. Verdict

This manuscript has been substantially improved from earlier
passes. The critical architecture is intact: activation sequence,
captain's message, bio-lock mechanism, Davan's color callback, the
slide-step anchor, the Cael acknowledgment, Maren's memory-through-
childhood rather than through the book. The number of genuine
continuity errors is significantly lower than would have been
flagged in a V1 or V2 pass on this same span.

Two issues require author attention: the bark-skin at the nose-
bridge in Ch. 30 lacks any setup in Chs. 26–29 and represents an
undocumented escalation beyond the wrists-to-shoulder arc
established in the bible; and the bio-lock footnote collapses
Maren's "native-baseline" distinction and the bloodline's
"immune" axis into shared parenthetical framing that is
mechanistically imprecise relative to D4 canon. Everything else
flagged is either a minor question for the author or the
deliberate structural openness of the ending.
