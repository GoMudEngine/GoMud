## Davan Voice Signature Match

**Assessment: Strong. The chapter is a direct continuation of the Ch.
24 mode, transposed to an interior setting without losing the register.**

The open-grained, observational architecture is present from the first
page. "The hillside was doing something" — short declarative as
threshold sentence, no announcement of interiority, physical event
first. "He did not name the thing inside his ribs. He kept his hand
where his hand was." — this is canonical Davan: suppressing in body
rather than in cognition, the suppression described through the body's
action, not through an interior announcement.

The circling approach is structurally embedded throughout. The coals
image for the hillside light (introduced, dropped, then the "coals were
saying yes" landing) enacts the same approach-and-arrive rhythm as the
Ch. 24 empathic-country passages. "A piece of his mind, far back, said:
*all of one thing*. He did not chase the thought. He circled it and let
it settle and walked on." — this is Davan being Davan shown, not
narrated. The meta-commentary is inside the character's motion, not
above it.

The specific-noticing mode is at full operation: the vine anchor-points
going slack, the bracken sagging sideways, the thorn branch *released*
rather than pushed or torn. Davan reads the hillside through material
behavior, not through interpretation. He does not say "the hillside was
opening." He describes what each element does at its join.

**Carving-hands intelligence in non-carving form:** The fingerpad
proximity-read in the storage room is the correct equivalent. "He put
his palm at the level of the object, close but not touching, and he let
his proximity-sense do its work." The palm as reading instrument, the
extended-forearm sense as the carving-finger equivalent, the choice
of the center of the row because "the center of a row was where a
row's attention was most evenly distributed" — this is carver's
reasoning applied to shelving. The chapter explicitly names the carving
finger: "He extended one finger of his right hand — his carving finger,
the one that had known the inner-orbit shape before his mind had."
The continuity from the pine carving (Ch. 19) through the hawthorn
piece (Ch. 24) to the fingerpad contact here is earned.

**Ship-interior metaphors:** The chapter draws from held-air, barn,
jar, cellar, grain, seed, chapel recess, winter byre, and vault. No
drift toward abstract lyricism — every metaphor is material and
functional. "The air of an indoors that had been indoors since before
anyone in this country had a word for indoors" is the chapter's best
single sentence in this register: the metaphor contains its own
historical depth without leaving the material domain. The storage
analogies (barn/grain, jar/seed, cellar/roots) are exactly the right
register for Davan — craft and preservation, not transcendence.

**One drift zone:** "He was inside a room made of the material the
debris field had been scattering pieces of across sixty miles of
country for long enough that no song still knew what the pieces were."
This is a longer reach than Davan usually makes — it assembles a
historical inference that spans geography and cultural memory. It is
borderline: Davan can make this inference (he has the pieces), but the
sentence feels slightly more essayistic than observational. Not a
failure — more an accumulation that the chapter doesn't need. Optional
trim: cut to "He was inside a room made of the material."

## "He understood" — Strict-Voice Call

**Two instances. Verdict: both are within voice, but the second is
tighter than the first.**

**Instance 1 (line 116-117):** "He understood — and understood was the
wrong word; his body knew — that the disc's stillness and the
hillside's opening were the same motion."

The agent's self-undercut is present and noted. The strict-voice
question: is "understood" a filter here even self-undercut?

**Call: Yellow, not red.** The self-undercut is not a fig leaf — it
actively replaces the cognitive verb with the body-read, which is
Davan's correct mode. The clause "understood was the wrong word; his
body knew" does what the Ch. 27 validator accepted for analytical-
inference understood: it marks that this is body-intelligence, not
cognition. The risk is that the sentence performs the correction rather
than enacting it — "his body knew" followed by a largely cognitive
inference (shoulders-posture reading as directional signal). The body-
to-analogy move is characteristic of Davan, but the analogy here
(a person speaking to someone over their shoulder) is slightly more
constructed than his usual sensory-grounded reaches.

*Suggested fix if revision warranted:* "His body read it as an outgoing
direction —" and cut the "understood" clause entirely, opening directly
at the boot-sole resonance. The boot-sole paragraph that follows does
the work more cleanly. This is a yellow flag, not a red-line.

**Instance 2 (line 297):** "This was, he understood slowly and without
saying it, not a weapon room."

**Call: Clean.** The self-annotation ("slowly and without saying it")
is Davan's circling mode narrated in its verb. "He understood slowly"
is structurally the same as "It came down" (Ch. 19) arriving after
circling: the understanding is not announced in advance, it arrives at
the end of a physical and sensory process (the shoulders-open passage
immediately precedes it). The "slowly and without saying it" qualifies
the cognitive event in a way that honors the open-grained register.
This is not a filter; it is the moment of landing after circling, which
the Ch. 19 benchmark authorizes.

**Total filter count:**

- **"He understood" (instance 1):** Yellow (self-undercut; acceptable
  but the body-analogy creates mild cognitive-filter echo)
- **"He understood" (instance 2):** Clean
- **All mandatory filter forms ("he felt," "he noticed," "he realized,"
  "he saw," "he heard," "he was aware"):** 0 confirmed instances

Confirmed firm filters: 0. Borderline: 1 (the first "understood"
clause). Clean by the Ch. 24 standard (2 firm, 1 borderline). Passes.

**Verdict: within tolerance.** The flag is Aldric's dialogue tag:

## New Color Handling

**Triple-approximation verdict: the approach works, but one
approximation is stronger than the other two.**

The agent chose three uncommitted approximations rather than naming:

1. "the color of honey held up to a lamp, only cooler"
2. "the color of the inside of a candle-flame where the flame went
   from orange to something past orange, in the cold blue center that
   was not blue"
3. "the color of the edge of dawn a morning nobody had seen, a morning
   the country was having for the first time"

Approximation 3 is the strongest and most distinctly Davan: "a morning
nobody had seen" is country-register language, and "the country was
having for the first time" applies Davan's established mode (landscape
as agent, landscape as language) to the color itself. It also correctly
refuses to land — a morning nobody has seen is not a named color.

Approximation 1 is solid (material, specific, within the
craft-vocabulary domain).

Approximation 2 is the weakest: "the cold blue center that was not
blue" approaches the kind of paradox-compression that drifts toward
abstract lyricism. It does more work trying to define what the color
isn't than the other two combined, which edges past Davan's circling
mode toward intellectual wrestling.

**On the triple vs. single question:** Three approximations is slightly
over-explained for Davan's circling register. The circling mind is
shown reaching for three approximations, which is faithful to his mode,
but "his circling mind was circling, and the circling wanted a shape"
annotates the process. The annotation risks being Davan-being-Davan
narrated rather than shown. Compare to Ch. 24: "He let the thought come
without forcing it to a shape" (flagged yellow in the Ch. 24
validation). This passage is doing the same thing in longer form.

**Recommended fix:** Cut approximation 2. Retain 1 and 3, in that order
— material (honey/lamp), then country-register (edge of dawn, first
morning). The two sit at different scales and together define a range
without over-explaining it. Cut "his circling mind was circling, and
the circling wanted a shape" to "if he had to reach for the word —
and he did not have to —." The meta-commentary on the circling can go.

## Empathic-Patience Rendering

**Assessment: Lands in Davan's half-thought register with one
over-articulation.**

The outline called for "not human emotion but its oldest ancestor, the
long patience of something that has been waiting to be found." The
chapter renders this via three moves:

1. "It was not loneliness." — The word tried, found wrong, discarded.
   The explanation ("Loneliness was a thing bodies felt when they were
   built for company and were not in company. These objects had never
   been built for company. They had been built for *being there*.") is
   slightly long but earns its length — the distinction is doing real
   work.

2. "It was not patience, exactly, though patience was close." — The
   correct word approached and declined. "Patience was what a person
   did when they were waiting and knew they were waiting. The objects
   did not know they were waiting." — clean negative definition.

3. "simply doing the tick — only without the clock's conceit of a
   person checking it." — The well-set-clock figure. This is the
   chapter's single best empathic-patience sentence: the clock is
   not proud, not humble, simply correct — and the only modification
   needed is the removal of the implicit checker. The conceit of a
   checker is what makes patience patience. Without it, what remains
   is the outline's "oldest ancestor" — function without waiting.

**"*Attending without attender*"** — this is the chapter's strongest
formulation. It arrives as a fragment from "a piece of his mind,"
correctly placed, and is then explicitly set down: "He did not push
the thought. He set it down at the back of the shelf of his mind where
the unfinished sentences lived." This is canonically Davan: the half-
thought received, not concluded, placed in the working collection.

**The over-articulation:** "The waiting was now the material, and the
material was now the waiting" (lines 528-531). This is the one passage
that crosses from Davan's approaching/naming-the-limit mode into a
resolved paradox. "A waiting that had gone on for so long that the
waiting had taken the shape of the material" is the correct level —
the clock-figure extended to architecture. The next sentence ("and the
material was now the waiting, and the waiting was now the material")
completes the paradox, which is slightly more arrived-at than Davan's
open-grained register allows. In Ch. 19, "It came down" works because
it is the shortest possible arrival. This sentence doubles back on
itself.

*Suggested fix:* Cut the second inversion. End at "the waiting had
taken the shape of the material." The sentence already contains the
paradox; the reversal over-explains it.

## Aldric's Liturgical Moment

**Assessment: The kneeling and the word ring correctly in Aldric's
register. The dialogue lands in his established voice.**

The kneeling is rendered externally (Davan observing) and is framed
with full behavioral specificity: hands flat on thighs, back straight,
lips moving once. The liturgical word is not caught by Davan — correct
technique. Davan infers category, not content.

Aldric's declarative sequence on the records is the chapter's strongest
dialogue passage:

> "They're records. All of them. Nothing else. No instruments. No —"
> his hand moved and stopped and came back to his thigh. "No *control*
> of anything. No lever. No dial. No — weapon, though I do not know
> why I expected a weapon."

The interrupted list (No — / No — weapon) is the bible's two-registers-
in-conflict audible in syntax: the scholarly inventory reaching for
technical vocabulary and finding that the expected category (weapon,
instrument, lever, dial) is absent, and the interruption is Aldric's
body arriving at the absence before his sentence finishes. "I do not
know why I expected a weapon" is the self-awareness the bible calls
for — he names his own prior expectation and finds it inadequate.
The liturgical register arrives in the third declaration: "They are
records" said a third time as a vessel. This is the scholar finding
that the word he has been applying as a scholarly category has become
a liturgical utterance. The mode-shift is enacted, not announced.

Davan's observation of the third iteration — "the way a man said a
thing when he was making the saying itself a vessel, carrying the
saying from the place the saying had landed in his body to the place
the saying needed to go in order to become a thing he had said to
another person" — is long but correct. This is Davan watching Aldric
perform a liturgical action through a secular body, which is the bible's
confirmed voice signature for Aldric rendered from outside.

"Oh," Aldric said. / "It was, Davan thought, the smallest and most
accurate sound a priest had made in a long while." — This is the
chapter's best closing-of-a-passage. Clean, compressed, the Aldric
register heard accurately by Davan.

**One note:** Aldric's lamp-tic is registered ("His hand had gone
inside his robe, had found the cord of the lamp he was not carrying,
had not been there, had come back out without the tic finishing") and
explicitly not spoken by Davan. This is the correct technique — Davan
observes the aborted gesture, the bible notes the tic beginning to
change around Ch. 25. Ch. 28 has it starting-and-not-finishing, which
is consistent with the arc. Davan registering it and not naming it is
canonical empathic restraint.

## Landing Image Integrity

**Assessment: Firmly in Davan's register. Distinct from prior chapter
closers. Restraint-at-a-held-point correctly enacted.**

The final image: Davan's carving finger on one object, not pressing,
not lifting, not turning, not carrying, not claiming — held at the
exact register at which two things can be at once two things.

"He did not lift it. He did not press harder. He kept the contact at
exactly the register at which two things could be, at once, two things
— the object still the object, his finger still his finger, the
material the material, the body the body."

This is the carving-hands intelligence refusing to carve: the same
restraint that governs Davan's knowledge-building (approaching without
claiming, circling without concluding) applied to physical contact.
The final enumerated negation ("did not take it off, and did not turn
it over, and did not look to see if there was a marking on the
underside, and did not carry it back to the passage for Aldric, and
did not claim it, and did not pick it up") performs the restraint
in the syntactic act of listing what is not done. This is earned: the
list is not evasion, it is the full catalog of the actions the hands
know how to perform and are choosing not to.

**Rhyme check:**

- Ch. 19 (Davan): "three moons hung in a sky that had once held four"
  — upward close, sky-register. Ch. 28 is an interior close, material-
  register, no sky. Not repeated.
- Ch. 24 (Davan): "the country was under all of it and did not ask
  anything of him at all" — downward-open close, landscape scale. Ch.
  28 is at palm-scale, single object, a held point. The scale is
  inverted — from country-under-everything to one kept thing under one
  fingertip. Structurally rhymes (both are Davan at a held point with
  something he has not claimed) without using the same image.
- Ch. 27 (Maren): disc pressed into the recess — the activation image.
  Ch. 28's close deliberately echoes the posture (finger on object)
  without the activation. Maren's finger *activates*. Davan's finger
  *attends*. The echo is controlled and thematic: the two characters'
  relationship to the material is being distinguished in parallel
  closing images across adjacent chapters.
- Prior Davan closers (Ch. 22, 23, 25, 26): not Davan POV chapters.
  Ch. 24's closer (hawthorn against ribs) is the relevant prior Davan
  closer. Ch. 28 does not borrow warmth, ribs, or the country-as-
  receiver figure. **Distinct.**

**The "waiting for Aldric to be ready to begin"** — the close does not
summarize the chapter's discovery; it holds at the threshold of the
next action. This is the correct register for Davan's open-grained
mode. The chapter's discoveries are left in the room, not wrapped.

## Adversarial Read — Ch. 24 vs. Ch. 28

**Assessment: The chapters are distinguishable. No generic awe-struck-
interior drift. One area where the distinction blurs.**

**Rhythm:** Ch. 24 uses longer observational sentences that circle and
accumulate; Ch. 28 uses shorter declaratives within those sequences and
fewer subordinate clauses. The interior is more compact in Ch. 28,
appropriate to the enclosed space. "The coals were saying yes" — three
words. "He inhaled." — Two. The chapter compresses where Ch. 24
expands, and the compression is setting-appropriate (vault versus road).

**Diction:** Ch. 24's dominant vocabulary is grain, warmth, country,
buried, carry, circling. Ch. 28's is holding, storage, kept, material,
register, correct. The material-function vocabulary replaces the
landscape-craft vocabulary — appropriate to the shift from open country
to interior made-thing. Both are recognizably Davan (craft-register,
non-abstract) but use different nodes of that register.

**Interiority mode:** Ch. 24 processes across a full day of walking;
Ch. 28 processes in a single room over compressed time. The circling
is tighter in Ch. 28, which is accurate — the empathic reading is
stronger and faster in close proximity to concentrated material. The
mode is the same (approach, circle, partial name, set down) but the
speed changes. This is character-consistent, not drift.

**Skin-light as second narrator:** In Ch. 24, the green-gold is at
the collarbones and is named by its emotional meaning (recognition,
green-gold sustained). In Ch. 28, the new color is described only by
its effect on the walls — Davan does not read his own color, he reads
what the material gives back. This is a correct evolution: the second
narrator (skin-light visible to reader, not narrated from interior) is
more strictly enforced in Ch. 28 than in Ch. 24.

**Blur zone:** Both chapters involve Davan standing near an object and
letting his empathic-sense read it. The proximity-sense passages risk
reading as similar if isolated from context. The mitigation is in the
objects themselves: in Ch. 24 the empathic reading is of geological-
country scale (a hillside, a country, a buried thing); in Ch. 28 it
is of a discrete manufactured object at palm-range. The scale
difference is legible in the prose. But a reader moving quickly through
both chapters might not register the scale shift. This is not a
failure; it is a structural characteristic of the Davan chapters across
Act 3. Monitor Ch. 29 for the same proximity-sense repetition.

## Closing Image

**Assessment: Among the strongest closing images in the draft sequence.
The restraint-at-a-held-point is enacted rather than described.**

"He stayed at the shelf, with his finger on the one kept thing his
finger had chosen, and waited for Aldric to be ready to begin."

The chapter ends not at a conclusion but at a threshold — the same
structural move as Ch. 24 ("He did not finish the thought") but at a
different register. This is not unfinished thought; it is deliberate
waiting at a held point. The new color is in his wrist and is not
fading. The room is holding. The finger is on the object. The chapter
stops here, not because it has nothing left to say but because what
comes next belongs to Aldric.

The formal logic of restraint is the chapter's argument. The close
enacts it.
