## Contradictions (must fix)

### C-1: Corvel's container tenure — "two years" in Ch. 20 vs. "eight years" in Ch. 19 and bible

- **Issue:** Ch. 20 states the container has been with the cooperage group for
  two years; Ch. 19 and the bible lock "eight years" as canon.
- **Where:** Ch. 20, Asha's dialogue — "Two years. Nobody can open it. Nobody
  can find a seam, a latch, a mechanism. Fourteen people have handled it."
- **Canon says:** Bible Section 5 (Corvel's Container): "Edvar says 'eight years
  ago' in Ch. 19 — discrepancy resolved. Author has confirmed: **8 years** is
  canon." Bible Section 7 also lists it as a resolved contradiction.
- **Chapter says (Ch. 20):** "Two years. Nobody can open it."
- **Fix options:**
  1. **Edit Ch. 20:** Change "Two years" to "Eight years" in Asha's line.
  2. **Reconcile in-world:** Asha could be referring specifically to how long
     *she personally* has been part of the group's attempts — not the container's
     full tenure — but this reading requires added dialogue clarification and
     is the weaker option. Option 1 is cleaner.

### C-2: Reth's survey — "forty-one years ago" vs. "forty years" inconsistency
within Ch. 17 itself, plus the "retired the following year" arithmetic

- **Issue:** Reth says he walked the highlands "forty-one years ago" and then
  "completed the rest of the survey and retired the following year." Later in
  the same chapter Aldric reflects on "a surveyor who had carried his report
  for forty years." The arithmetic is internally consistent (41 years ago,
  retired 40 years ago), but it creates an inconsistency with the bible, which
  describes the wait as "forty years" throughout, including in its character
  entry for Reth ("Confirmed: metallic formation in eastern highlands...").
  The bible's Reth entry says "Forty years and no one followed up" in Brennan's
  dialogue account. This is actually consistent with Reth's own statement — the
  gap is one year between survey and retirement, which doesn't contradict
  anything. **RESOLUTION: This is not a true contradiction.** The 41/40 spread
  is accounted for by the survey-to-retirement gap. Logging here for the record.
  Mark CLEAN.

### C-3: Edvar's face — "face" terminology slip ("the display")

- **Issue:** Ch. 19 uses "the display" repeatedly to describe Edvar's face
  object. The bible (Section 8, Style Notes) is explicit: the object is ALWAYS
  "the face" — never "the panel," "the screen," or "the device." "The display"
  is not on the forbidden list but it is the closest synonym to "screen," and
  its repeated use in Ch. 19 risks drift from the manuscript-wide convention.
- **Where:** Ch. 19, multiple paragraphs — "The display assembled itself," "the
  display widened to include them," "the cold light of the display," "the four
  shapes continued their orbits" (this instance uses no noun, which is fine),
  "the display slowly fade as the touch-activation ran its course," "while the
  face lay wrapped in its canvas."
- **Canon says:** Bible Section 8: "**The face**: always 'the face' — never
  'the panel,' 'the screen,' or 'the device.'" Also Section 5: "Named
  consistently 'the face' — NOT 'the panel.'"
- **Chapter says:** "the display" (used ~7 times in Ch. 19, plus one correct
  use of "the face" at the end of the chapter).
- **Fix options:**
  1. **Edit Ch. 19:** Replace each use of "the display" that refers to the
     object as a whole with "the face." Where "the display" refers to the
     *content being shown* (the orbital image), it may survive as a noun for
     the image rather than the object — but this distinction is ambiguous in
     several lines and should be reviewed phrase by phrase. Safe approach:
     use "the face" for the object, "the image" or "the orbital record" for
     the visual content.
  2. **Update bible style notes** to explicitly permit "the display" when
     referring to the visual content (not the object itself) — then enforce
     only for object references. This is the more nuanced fix and requires
     author confirmation.

### C-4: Maren's three years in New Plymouth — contradicts established timeline

- **Issue:** In Ch. 20, Maren tells Renner she has been "in New Plymouth for
  three years" and has been "unchanged" her whole life. But the established
  bible timeline (Section 6) has Maren in New Plymouth for only 8 days at
  the start of Act 2 (Ch. 14). Ch. 16-20 represent a short additional period.
  Three years in New Plymouth is impossible given the story's timeline.
- **Where:** Ch. 20, Maren's dialogue: "How long? / Three years. / And you've
  been in New Plymouth for — / Three years."
- **Canon says:** Bible Section 6 timeline: "Ch. 14: Maren in New Plymouth
  (8 days in)." Ch. 20 is only a few weeks after Ch. 1. The story opened at
  the Autumnal Rite.
- **Chapter says:** "Three years" in New Plymouth, Maren to Renner.
- **Fix options:**
  1. **Edit Ch. 20:** Renner's question "And you've been in New Plymouth for —"
     is likely a question about how long Maren has been *passing as Chrysalis-
     touched* (i.e., the fraud), not specifically New Plymouth tenure. The
     "three years" answer then refers to the duration of her coal-pouch
     deception (established as three years in the bible: "Three years of
     coal-pouch deception to fake the Chrysalis mark"). This reading makes the
     exchange consistent. Fix: add a clarifying beat or rephrase Renner's
     question to make clear she's asking about the deception duration, not
     New Plymouth specifically. A minimal edit: change Renner's line from
     "And you've been in New Plymouth for —" to "And you've been passing
     for —" or "And you've been concealing it for —"
  2. **If author intended three years in New Plymouth:** the timeline in the
     bible is wrong and a major restructuring of Act 1-2 placement is needed.
     This is extremely unlikely given the story's continuity elsewhere. Option 1
     is almost certainly the correct fix.

## Drift / Soft inconsistencies (should address)

### D-1: Maren's inner-pocket disc location in Ch. 20 vs. bible's established carry habits

- **Issue:** Ch. 20 describes the disc as carried "in her coat, in the inner
  pocket, where it had been since she'd left the highland town." The bible
  establishes the disc as a carried object from Ch. 1, but does not specify
  the "highland town" as the point of transfer to an inner coat pocket. The
  phrasing "since she'd left the highland town" is ambiguous — the story begins
  in Ashwick hamlet, which is rural/hamlet not a "highland town." Could be
  referring to Stillwater (crossroads town) or the road out of Ashwick.
- **Where:** Ch. 20, Maren's internal section after the argument scene.
- **Canon says:** Bible Section 2 (Maren): carries father's disc; no pocket
  specification. Bible Section 5: disc is warm from Ch. 1 packing.
- **Soft fix:** Confirm authorial intent for "highland town" — is this Stillwater,
  the journey through the eastern foothills, or a slip for "hamlet"? If Ashwick
  is meant, change to "hamlet" or simply "since she'd fled north."

### D-2: Vane's bark-skin description — "past the elbow" in Ch. 18 vs. "mid-bicep by Ch. 4"

- **Issue:** Bible establishes bark-skin patches at "mid-bicep by Ch. 4" as the
  baseline. Ch. 18 says "past the elbow now" and "the bark-skin on her right
  arm itched. Past the elbow now." These are the same location description —
  mid-bicep is above the elbow, so "past the elbow" means it has progressed
  downward toward the wrist? Or does "past the elbow" mean past the elbow
  upward? The direction is unclear.
- **Where:** Ch. 18, opening section.
- **Canon says:** Bible Section 2 (Vane): "Bark-skin patches spreading from
  wrists up arms, at mid-bicep by Ch. 4." The spreading direction is wrist
  to shoulder. Mid-bicep (above the elbow) = further progressed than elbow.
- **Chapter says:** "Past the elbow now" — if the spread is wrist-to-shoulder,
  this means it has not yet reached mid-bicep (elbow is below mid-bicep). This
  contradicts the Ch. 4 baseline, where it was already at mid-bicep.
- **Soft fix:** If bark-skin spreads wrist-to-shoulder, Ch. 18 should describe
  it as having reached or passed mid-bicep, not merely "past the elbow."
  Consider revising to "above the elbow now" (meaning it has continued past
  elbow toward shoulder) or "to the shoulder now" if significant time has
  passed and progression is expected. The current phrasing implies regression,
  which the bible explicitly flags as wrong (Section 7, item 5).
