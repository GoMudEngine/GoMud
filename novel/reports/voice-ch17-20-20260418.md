## Per-character findings

### Aldric (POV, Ch. 17)

- **Signature match:** Mixed — strong in places, but blurs with other
  analytical POVs more than the bible demands.

- **Specific issues:**

  - **The liturgical register is present but thin.** The bible (Sec. 2 and
    Sec. 8) calls for "a man of the cloth realizing his god is a shipwreck"
    — formal precision masking parallel inner life, with ecclesiastical
    vocabulary deployed against an increasingly material reality. What we
    get in Ch. 17 is closer to a thoughtful scholar's interiority than a
    Keeper's. The word "institution" appears correctly, but there is almost
    no theological language in his thoughts. Compare: when Aldric looks at
    the face in the undercroft in Ch. 3, does that chapter use liturgical
    framing? If so, Ch. 17 is noticeably lighter. The lamp tic is the
    single most distinctly *Aldric* element in the chapter, and it carries
    more signature weight than anything in the internal prose.

    *Suggested revision:* At the moment Reth confirms the recess and the
    symbol (p. 7, the coin-circle gesture), Aldric's internal processing is
    told entirely in scholar's language: "He had been counting to four...
    exactly what Reth had seen." This is the scene's climax. It should ring
    with Aldric's specific register — guilt, theological vertigo, the
    experience of a man whose institutional vocabulary is shattering against
    a literal fact. Something like: "Thirty years of pastoral training held
    his breath in and held his face still. Beneath that training: the word
    *wreckage* arriving in a place where he had always used *providence*."

  - **Long analytical clauses without the simple-sentence counterpoint.**
    The bible specifies the pattern: long careful clause → stripped simple
    sentence (what he actually thinks). Ch. 17 does this occasionally ("It
    was real," "I am tired") but not consistently enough. Many paragraphs
    are unbroken long-clause chains that read as competent literary prose
    but are not distinctly Aldric's. Example: the paragraph beginning "He
    thought about Reth's hands on the table..." (p. 9 closing ride section)
    — five long sentences in a row, no landing-beat simple sentence.

    *Suggested revision:* Break that paragraph's close with something short
    and stripped: "He was the next piece. He sat with this and did not
    reach for the candle at the waypoint, which was the closest he had come
    to breaking the habit in thirty years."

  - **Lamp tic arc is handled correctly.** Caught twice on the road west,
    resisted both times, with growing self-awareness about the tic's
    psychology. The paragraph explaining "the habit was not about lamps"
    is the chapter's best Aldric-voice moment — exactly the formal-to-frank
    movement the bible calls for. Protect this.

  - **"Carefully"** — appears once (p. 3: Brennan says "Carefully" when
    Aldric reaches for the map). This is Brennan speaking, so technically
    fine. But note the global pass flag: watch for authorial habit bleeding
    into any POV's narration.

  - **No filter words flagged in POV narration** in this chapter. Clean.

### Vane (POV, Ch. 18)

- **Signature match:** Strong — this is the best-executed voice in the
  four-chapter run. The compressed style is doing exactly what Vane's
  voice should do.

- **The compression question:** The bible notes Ch. 18 is "markedly
  compressed... much shorter paragraphs than the main manuscript" and asks
  whether this is intentional voice-under-pressure or draft compression.
  The verdict here is: **intentional, and it is working.** The short
  paragraphs and staccato line breaks are not draft laziness — they are the
  prose enacting Vane's psychology. The chapter is about a person whose
  professional compartmentalization is failing. The form (stripped, refusing
  to expand into full paragraphs, cutting before sentiment can arrive)
  mirrors the content (a mind that cannot let itself linger on what it has
  seen). The basement scene, the street scene, and the return to Maren are
  all compressed in a way that feels like Vane actively preventing herself
  from dwelling, which is exactly her character. **Do not expand this
  chapter for length. Expand it only if specific moments need more
  resistance on the page.**

- **Specific issues:**

  - **The cost-benefit ledger scene is Vane's clearest interiority
    moment and it mostly lands.** The passage beginning "A cost-benefit
    analysis" is the most explicit the narration gets about her inner
    state, and it earns that explicitness because the form — ledger,
    columns, acknowledged unknowns — is itself a character tell. She is
    using her professional cognitive tools on a moral problem and the
    tools are breaking. This is good. One small concern: "She was
    consuming a substance made from a captive person's body to track
    another person she was supposed to be protecting" (p. 6) — this is
    the FB-5 irony (harvesting irony) made explicit. The bible notes it
    "should be made explicit in Vane's realization." It is made explicit
    here, and it works. No fix needed; this closes FB-5 for Ch. 18.

  - **"Not quite a smile" / "corner of Vane's mouth" — CHECK NEEDED.**
    This chapter does not use these exact phrases. The passage "The
    faintest movement at the corner of Reth's mouth" appears in Ch. 17
    (Reth reacting to being called Surveyor). That is Reth's mouth, not
    Vane's, but the phrase-form is the same. The global flag (Sec. 8,
    Sec. 7 item 4) says the phrase is used 3+ times in Chs. 10 and 14.
    It does not appear in Vane's Ch. 18 narration or in Maren's Ch. 20.
    Ch. 17 contributes one instance (Reth's mouth). Recommend the author
    run a search across all chapters for "corner of" and "not quite a
    smile" to confirm the full inventory before the manuscript pass.

  - **"She catalogued" — used three times in Ch. 18.** Once is voice
    ("She catalogued. She'd always been good at cataloguing."), which is
    correct. But the verb recurs: "Vane catalogued this too" (re: Maren
    reading the situation) and implicitly in the collection apparatus
    paragraph ("She catalogued. She'd always been good at cataloguing").
    Three instances of the same distinctive verb in one chapter is the
    beginning of a tic. Vary one.

    *Suggested revision:* "She'd always been good at cataloguing" →
    "She'd always been good at sorting" or "at inventory." Keeps the
    professional-categories voice without repeating the specific word.

  - **"She stood there for a long time" (p. 4).** This phrase also
    appears in the bible's own summary of this chapter: "stands in the
    street a long time." When prose and summary share exact phrasing, it
    sometimes means the draft has not found its own words for a beat.
    Consider whether this is the right way to render the street-standing
    — or whether the external detail of the lamplighter doubling back,
    the shadow migrating, is already doing the work and the explicit
    "long time" is redundant.

  - **Maren's "All right" lands exactly correctly.** The bible flags
    this as a key voice tell (Sec. 2, Sec. 8: "when she says 'All right'
    it means something has settled at depth"). The narrator confirms it
    immediately: "This was the part that could have become something
    else... She didn't." Two words, correct weight, handled with the
    restraint the character requires.

  - **No filter words flagged in Vane's POV narration.** The chapter is
    clean on this axis.

### Davan (POV, Ch. 19)

- **Signature match:** Strong. The open-grained, circling quality is
  well-sustained. His characteristic movement — approaching a conclusion,
  leaving it, returning at a different angle — is structurally embedded
  in the chapter's pacing.

- **Specific issues:**

  - **The green-gold skin-light is used correctly and effectively.** It
    fires at contact with the face, holds sustained through the "It came
    down" revelation, pulses once at the container-connection moment, and
    fades to baseline at the chapter's close. The bible specifies this is
    "the recognition register... the most settled color he's produced"
    (first time sustained in Ch. 19). The chapter honors that. Skin-light
    is doing the work the bible assigned it — functioning as a second
    narrator visible to the reader.

  - **"It came down" is correctly under-dramatized.** Two words, after
    pages of circling. This is Davan's signature — the moment he does
    land, it lands with weight precisely because he's been orbiting it.
    Protect this.

  - **Half-thoughts he doesn't force to conclusion:** the chapter is
    generally faithful to this. The strongest exception is the passage
    "He let the thought arrive at its own pace" (p. 3) — this is the
    narration telling us Davan is being Davan, rather than showing us
    Davan being Davan. The thought that follows (the fourth shape, no
    longer in orbit) arrives cleanly; the announcement before it is
    redundant.

    *Suggested revision:* Cut "He let the thought arrive at its own
    pace." Start directly with "The fourth shape. Smaller, as if further
    away." The absence of the meta-comment is itself Davan's voice.

  - **"The words came in from the edges" / display assembling itself
    (p. 2):** "The display assembled itself" and the staged arrival of
    orbital shapes is a strong sensory sequence. The prose is doing
    Davan's observational work — registering things in the order they
    arrive at perception. This is correct for his POV.

  - **One filter word:** Ch. 19, paragraph beginning "He felt the
    thought arrive before he had words for it" — this is borderline.
    "Felt the thought arrive" is technically a filter construction, though
    it avoids the most mechanical forms ("he felt that," "he realized
    that"). The phrase is distinctive enough to survive, but flag for the
    line editor's pass.

  - **Edvar's characterization from Davan's POV:** Edvar's expressions
    are read and interpreted by Davan throughout — "the look of a man
    opening a door he had kept locked," "the expression of a man who had
    been sitting alone with a question for twenty years." These
    interpretations are presented as Davan's empathic readings, not
    omniscient narration. Appropriate for his developing empathic sense.
    No POV bleed here.

### Maren (POV, Ch. 20)

- **Signature match:** Strong. The compressed, watchful, exit-cataloguing
  quality is consistent throughout.

- **Specific issues:**

  - **Catalogue-of-the-room opening:** The chapter opens with Maren
    mentally sorting every person in the basement before she engages any
    of them. This is the bible's signature — "catalogues the room for
    exits and available resources before engaging people." The execution
    is clean. She maps Renner, Asha, Corvel, Edvar, the cartographer,
    Pip in sequence, reading each by physical tells. This is the chapter's
    strongest voice-consistency moment.

  - **The disc revelation and Maren's internal shift (pp. 7-8):** "She
    had carried it for years thinking she was carrying grief. She was
    carrying a test." This is the chapter's emotional climax. The prose
    here is clear and correct in register — compressed declarative, the
    settlement of a thing that has been uncertain. However, this passage
    risks the "analytical about her own performance" note in the bible
    slipping into straightforward interiority that any POV could deliver.
    What makes it distinctly Maren's is the framing as *assessment*, not
    *feeling*. "She was carrying a test" hits correctly. The preceding
    sentences — "Not sentimentally... She thought about him practically"
    — explicitly enforce her register. This is well-handled.

  - **Vane present in Maren's narration (p. 1):** "Vane was outside,
    near the gate, doing whatever Vane did when she waited — which was
    stand very still in a place where she could see a long way and think
    about angles." This is Maren's perception of Vane from prior
    observation — not POV bleed. It is also a strong character-voice
    moment: Maren describing Vane in Vane's own operational terms
    (sightlines, angles) shows that she has been paying attention to how
    Vane moves through space. Keep.

  - **"She catalogued it automatically"** (p. 1, re: exits in the
    stairwell). One instance; correct usage. Not the three-instances
    problem Vane has in Ch. 18.

  - **Three filter word instances found:**

    1. Ch. 20, p. 2: "she could see her doing it — the slight narrowing
       of focus that happened when someone was fitting a new piece..."
       — "she could see her doing it" is a filter construction.
       *Suggested:* "The slight narrowing of focus — someone fitting a
       new piece into an existing framework..."

    2. Ch. 20, p. 4: "Maren could see it register differently with each
       of them."
       *Suggested:* "It registered differently with each of them."

    3. Ch. 20, p. 5: "she could feel it against her ribs" (re: the disc).
       — Less problematic than the others; "feel" here is literal
       physical sensation, not perceptual filtering. Keep or adjust at
       author's discretion.

  - **Renner, Asha, Corvel characterization from Maren's POV:** Each is
    rendered through physical observation and behavioral inference — the
    glassblower's arms, the chemical stains in the knuckle-creases,
    Renner's precision-mouth. This is correct for Maren's POV; she reads
    rooms by physical evidence. No POV bleed.

  - **Davan's brief appearance (p. 7):** "She noted it. Filed it under
    *competent*." Clean Maren voice — professional category, no
    sentiment.

## Voice-blur warnings

### Aldric (Ch. 17) and Davan (Ch. 19): Low-level blur risk

This is less severe than the Aldric/Vane blur the feedback flags, but
worth noting. Both Aldric and Davan in these chapters process discovery
through sustained analytical circling with a moment of landing. Both
chapters use the architecture: extended observation → resist conclusion →
simple statement of what is true ("Something changed." / "It came down.").

This is not yet a confusion problem — the contexts are entirely different,
the sensory surfaces (Aldric's formal clauses vs. Davan's texture-and-
smell grounding) are distinct, and the chapters are not adjacent. But if
Act Three places them in the same scene, watch for their internal
monologue styles bleeding together. The fix is already in the bible:
Aldric needs more liturgical vocabulary in his processing; Davan needs
skin-light and tactile-sensory grounding. Both are present in these
chapters but can be pushed harder.

### Aldric (Ch. 17) and Vane (Ch. 18): The flagged blur — VERDICT: PASS

The big concern: can you tell Aldric from Vane without the chapter
header? Read adversarially, the answer is **yes, with one caveat.**

Vane's chapter is visually distinguishable — the short paragraphs, the
line breaks, the compressed structure are a formal signal. But formal
signals are layout, not voice. Setting layout aside:

**What makes them distinct:**
- Aldric uses long careful clauses with subordinate phrases nested inside
  subordinate phrases. His paragraph rhythm is slow and architecturally
  deliberate. Vane uses declarative sentences that stop. She does not
  subordinate.
- Aldric's metaphors draw from institutional/scholarly life (the strata of
  inquiry, coordinates, a patient sequence). Vane's metaphors draw from
  operational and commercial life (cost structure, production source,
  supply chain, fieldwork).
- Aldric's self-awareness is narrated — he catches himself in the lying,
  he analyzes the lamp tic's psychology in complete paragraphs. Vane's
  self-awareness is rationed and expressed through what she chooses NOT
  to think ("she could still taste the last wafer... she would probably
  taste it for a while" — and then nothing more on that subject).

**The one caveat:**
Aldric's road-meditation sections (the final two pages of Ch. 17) are the
closest the two chapters come to blur. The passage about Reth's patience,
about institutional silences, about the managed suppression of knowledge —
this is analytical, detached, and cool. It could, stripped of context, sit
in Vane's register. What saves it is diction: Aldric uses "institution,"
"managed," "permitted to be known," "pastoral care." These are Keeper
words. Vane's vocabulary does not include "pastoral." Keep Aldric's
Keeper-specific vocabulary working in these passages and the blur
risk stays manageable.

**Recommendation:** No rewrite required for blur. But when revising
Ch. 17, push the liturgical register specifically in the road-meditation
passages. Even one or two ecclesiastical terms in Aldric's self-analysis
will anchor his voice against Vane's worldly-operational register.

## Additional flags

### "Careful" — global overuse watch
One appearance in Ch. 17 (Brennan: "Carefully" when Aldric handles the
map). This is dialogue attribution rather than narration, and is
contextually appropriate. It does not worsen the global-overuse problem.
No fix needed here; maintain awareness for the manuscript pass.

### "Not quite a smile" / "corner of [X]'s mouth"
One instance in Ch. 17: "The faintest movement at the corner of Reth's
mouth." This is Reth's mouth, not Vane's, and appears in Aldric's POV.
The phrase-form matches the flagged tic even if the subject differs.
Recommend the author run a search for "corner of" across all chapters
to confirm full count. Current Ch. 17-20 contribution: 1 instance.

### Ch. 18 compression: author decision recommended
The question of whether Ch. 18's compressed format is intentional
voice-under-pressure or draft compression is resolved here in favor
of intentional. The form enacts Vane's psychology of non-dwelling.
If the author agrees, no expansion is needed. If the author feels
key moments need more resistance on the page — particularly Vane
standing in the street, which currently moves through the cost-benefit
analysis quickly — that is the place to add material, not the
basement scene or the return to Maren.

### Disc behavior consistency with bible
Ch. 20 describes the disc pulsing "several times a day" with increasing
frequency since arriving in New Plymouth. Bible Sec. 5 confirms disc
behavior is escalating from Ch. 1 forward. Ch. 20's reported frequency
("several times a day") is consistent with escalation from Ch. 14's
"increasing frequency noted." No contradiction.

### Edvar's face: display discrepancy maintained
Ch. 19 correctly renders the fourth shape as "smaller, as if further
away" — not irregular or damaged (Aldric's face, Ch. 3). Bible Sec. 5
specifies these must remain distinct through Act 3. The two chapters
are faithful to this.
