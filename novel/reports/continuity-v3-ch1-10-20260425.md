## 1. Geographic & Travel-Time Audit

**CLEAN with one note.**

The section is internally consistent on distances. Maren leaves Ashwick the
night of Ch. 1 and reaches Betta's farm by day 4 or 5 (Ch. 5); Vane
establishes she is "twelve days behind" when she leaves New Plymouth (Ch. 8),
places Maren's kitchen trace "three days" cold, and closes to "two days behind"
by Ch. 8's end. Davan is "three weeks on the road" when Ch. 2 opens and
arrives at the Confluence in Ch. 6. The barge leg from Confluence to New
Plymouth is one day plus a night, consistent with river geography.

**Minor note — Vane's day-count arithmetic (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: Ch. 8 opens: "She was twelve days behind when she left New
  Plymouth." The kitchen trace is "three days since the girl had been here."
  But Vane then finds the ditch-embankment trace and a barn for the night,
  and by the chapter's close says she is "two days behind, probably less"
  and estimates she will be "ahead" by morning.
- **The stress test**: Twelve days behind → three-day-old kitchen trace →
  "two days behind" in a single chapter. That collapse is plausible only if
  Vane traveled extremely fast from New Plymouth before Ch. 8's opening frame
  and the "twelve days" refers to the gap at departure, not at chapter-start.
  The text does not specify exactly when Ch. 8 begins relative to departure.
  The arithmetic holds *if* Vane spent several days closing the gap off-page,
  but this is never stated, leaving the reader to do silent division. Not a
  fracture, but the seam is visible.
- **Severity**: Minor.

## 2. Temporal Inconsistencies

**One substantive issue; one minor flag.**

**Anchor problem — Vane's Bloom use in the woodcutter's shelter (Major)**

- **Evidence**: In Ch. 8 Vane takes a wafer "that night, in the woodcutter's
  shelter." She has been tracking Maren's trail and plans to press on. In
  Ch. 9, Maren is sheltering in *the same woodcutter's shelter* with a bad
  ankle, for two full days (she arrives when "the light was going," sleeps
  heavily, endures a second harder day, then leaves "in the afternoon" of day
  two). The two characters appear to occupy the same shelter at overlapping
  or back-to-back times.
- **The inconsistency**: Ch. 8 positions Vane as "ahead" of Maren by the
  chapter's end — she has overtaken and now leads. Ch. 8 also places Vane
  in the woodcutter's shelter the same night Maren arrives there (Ch. 9 opens
  "two hours north of Betta's farmstead" with the ankle). But if Vane is now
  *ahead* of Maren, and the shelter is on the road between them and Stillwater,
  Vane should have passed through and been gone before Maren arrived. This
  is plausible — it just requires the shelter encounter to be sequential
  (Vane overnight, Vane leaves at dawn, Maren arrives late afternoon). The
  text doesn't contradict this, but it also doesn't secure it. Vane "lay on
  her back in the shelter and looked at the dark ceiling" and the reader has
  no explicit timestamp for when she departed. Given that Ch. 9 establishes
  Maren's ankle was already bad "two hours north of Betta's farmstead" and
  she makes four hours before it defeats her, the shelter is approximately
  six road-hours north of Betta's. Vane was already north of the farmstead
  by the time Maren left it (Vane's trail reading confirms she was ahead).
  The overlap *can* be resolved but requires the reader to construct the
  sequence. A prior reviewer did not flag this — worth confirming the spacing
  is intentional and perhaps making the departure explicit.
- **Cross-reference**: Story Bible, Section 6 Timeline: Ch. 8 = "Trail" /
  Ch. 9 = "Maren, Woodcutter's shelter." The bible treats them as sequential
  chapters, not concurrent days, which implies Vane cleared the shelter before
  Maren arrived. But the narrative's own internal cues don't confirm this
  cleanly.
- **Severity**: Major. If confirmed sequential (Vane out before Maren in),
  this is a Minor structural ambiguity. If the nights genuinely overlap, it
  is a logic error — Maren would have found Vane there.

**Vane's withdrawal timeline — when does Bloom usage become unsustainable?
(Minor)**

- **Evidence**: In Ch. 4 Vane uses Bloom and checks her bark, noting it is at
  "mid-bicep on the right side" and comments she's been at this usage rate
  "for four years." In Ch. 8 she uses two wafers in a single day (the loose
  one while tracking, then the woodcutter's-shelter session). She has "four
  left in the tin" as of Ch. 10's opening of Ch. 11. The Bloom-giver
  overheard in Ch. 4 says "three months before a new one's producing at
  volume." No specific supply refill is shown between Ch. 4 and Ch. 10.
- **Minor flag**: The timeline of how many wafers Vane started with and how
  many she has consumed is never stated, so "four left" in Ch. 11 cannot be
  verified against consumption. Not a fracture, but any future chapter
  referencing the tin's count should proceed with caution.
- **Severity**: Minor.

## 3. Item & Object Permanence

**One clean contradiction found; one flag.**

**Corvin's whittled bird — double appearance (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: In Ch. 5, Corvin presses the whittled bird into Maren's hand
  at the farmstead door. She puts it "in her pocket alongside the disc." In
  Ch. 9, Maren takes the disc from her pocket and examines it; "in her other
  hand" she turns over Corvin's whittled bird. She puts them "away also."
  In Ch. 10, she grounds Vane by pressing "Corvin's whittled bird" into
  Vane's hand. Vane's fingers close around it. At the end of Ch. 10, Maren
  says "keep it" and Vane pockets it.
- **The issue**: The bird is transferred to Vane. This is intentional and
  tracked — no fracture there. The minor flag is that in Ch. 9, Maren holds
  the bird in her "other hand" while examining the disc, then puts them "away
  also." The word "also" implies both go back into pockets, which is clean
  before the Ch. 10 transfer.
- **Verdict**: Object permanence is correctly maintained. The bird travels
  Maren → Vane in Ch. 10 and is thereafter Vane's. No fracture.

**The coal pouch — disposal continuity (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: In Ch. 5, Maren discards the charcoal and wool-cloth into a
  stream at the first crossing: "The charcoal and the wool-cloth she scattered
  in the stream." She keeps the leather bag because it is useful. In Ch. 1
  she then picks up the pouch again at the kitchen shelf: "She picked up the
  coin purse and found, under it, the small worn pouch — charcoal and
  wool-cloth, still warm from the Rite. She put it in her pocket."
- **The issue**: This is sequence-correct: she takes the whole pouch in Ch. 1,
  then disposes of the charcoal/wool in Ch. 5, keeping the leather.
  The pouch is described as "charcoal and wool-cloth" when she picks it up
  in Ch. 1, and she "scattered in the stream" the charcoal and wool-cloth in
  Ch. 5, "the leather" kept. Consistent.
- **One wrinkle**: In Ch. 1, packing, she "put it in her pocket. It smelled
  like Delia's kitchen." In Ch. 5 she reflects: "It smelled like Delia's
  kitchen, like drying things, like fennel and coltsfoot." But she has then
  discarded the wool-cloth that held this smell — the leather pouch alone
  presumably carries less of the kitchen scent. A trivial sensory continuity
  note, not a fracture.
- **Severity**: Minor (trivial).

**The disc's behavior — escalation vs. stated Bible canon (Note, not fracture)**

- **Evidence**: Story Bible (Section 5) states: "Ch. 1 (packing): warm,
  vibrates faintly. Ch. 9 (woodcutter's shelter): produces a bluish ring of
  light, pulses." In the manuscript, Ch. 1 packing: the disc "was warm — not
  body-warm, warmer, with the faintest vibration under her thumb." On the
  road in Ch. 1, after she leaves: "the disc hummed once — a single brief
  vibration, there and gone, like a heartbeat that wasn't hers." In Ch. 9,
  the disc shows "a thin ring of color appeared at the disc's outer edge —
  faint, bluish" and "a low steady pulse she could feel in her fingertips."
- **Status**: Manuscript matches bible canon exactly. Escalation from
  warm/vibrate → blue ring/pulse is correctly staged. No fracture.

## 4. Lore / Canon Contradictions

**Two findings, one significant.**

**Inner orbit symbol — fourth arc description discrepancy (Major)**

- **Evidence**: Story Bible (Section 5, Inner Orbit Symbol): "four nested arcs
  with raised points along their paths; the fourth arc drifts off-true,
  breaking the nested-arc pattern at a tangent." Ch. 9 manuscript (Maren
  examining disc): "A set of nested arcs, four of them, struck from a single
  center — the innermost tight, the next two stepping outward at regular
  intervals, the fourth riding wider and slightly off-true, as though whatever
  had drawn them had let the last one drift. Along each arc, a small raised
  point."
- **Ch. 3 manuscript (Aldric, the face in the undercroft)**: When Aldric
  observes the face, Ch. 7 has him count "four small shapes in tight orbit"
  around the blue-green world, with "the one closest — the one that would be
  largest in the sky — had a quality... it was different from the others. Less
  regular in shape, as though something had happened to it." This is "damaged"
  per Ch. 7 and per the bible's display canon for Aldric's face.
- **The stress test**: The bible specifies that the *inner orbit symbol* (on
  the disc, on temple lintels, carved by Davan) shows four arcs with the
  fourth "drifts off-true" — this is the descent trajectory schematic. The
  *face* (Aldric's and Edvar's) shows four orbital bodies where the fourth is
  "irregular/damaged" (Aldric's angle) or "smaller/further away" (Edvar's
  angle). The disc symbol and the face display are two different
  representations of the same underlying cosmological reality but are NOT
  the same object. This distinction is maintained in the text.
- **The actual issue**: In Ch. 6, Davan observes the symbol above the
  Confluence temple door: "The inner spiral of the symbol — the oldest part
  ... was not quite a spiral. He had always read it as one. Up close, with
  fresh attention, it was a set of concentric arcs that described an orbit."
  This is correct. But Davan's Ch. 6 empathic response to the symbol is
  described as reading it as "lonely. The loneliness of a thing going around
  and around and around." Bible canon states Davan "is the first character in
  the book whose empathic sense reads the symbol accurately as orbital rather
  than spiral." The bible says his empathic sense reads it accurately — but
  the manuscript's empathic sense gives him an *emotional* register
  (loneliness, vigil), not the *accurate schematic* reading (mission-profile
  diagram). These are compatible: the emotional read and the schematic read
  can coexist. But the bible's phrasing ("reads the symbol accurately as
  orbital") is not what the manuscript delivers in Ch. 6. Davan reads it as
  *orbital in character* (not a spiral but a circling thing), not as
  *accurately identifying it as a mission-profile diagram*. This is
  probably intentional layering, but the bible language slightly over-claims
  what the manuscript does in Ch. 6.
- **Severity**: Minor. The underlying logic is sound. The bible's phrase
  "reads the symbol accurately" should probably read "reads the symbol as
  orbital rather than spiral" to match the manuscript's actual beat.

**Lera's age — bible vs. manuscript (Major)**

- **Evidence**: Story Bible (Section 2, Supporting Characters): "Lera —
  barge companion, met Davan Ch. 6 on river barge from Confluence to New
  Plymouth." No age given for Lera in the bible character entry. In the
  manuscript, Ch. 6: "She was perhaps sixty, or perhaps older in ways that
  made the number less useful — the Chrysalis had been working on her long
  enough that her arms, where they were visible below her rolled sleeves, had
  gone translucent at the wrists."
- **The stress test against internal canon**: Lera says she is going to New
  Plymouth for "the final Rite" — the ceremony for those in the last stages
  of transformation. She says "The first years hurt. When it's working against
  what you expect, what you've decided you should be. Then it stops hurting."
  She says "I've been thinking about it for forty years... you have the
  advantage of being earlier in the process." She also says "my daughter kept
  finding reasons to delay me." Davan has been on the road three weeks; his
  Blooming came "on slow, just a warmth at first... he'd mistaken for a fever"
  — his Chrysalis is active and maturing. Lera's Chrysalis has been working
  on her for "long enough" that her arms are translucent and her fingers have
  elongated by a joint. "Forty years" of thinking plus "first years" suggests
  she first changed in her twenties and has been changing for decades. At
  "perhaps sixty," this is coherent.
- **The bible character entry for Lera** lacks the age/decade framing but
  this is not a contradiction — the bible simply doesn't specify. No fracture.
  **Clean.**

**Chrysalis baseline — how hollow is hollow? (Note, not fracture)**

- **Evidence**: Story Bible (Section 5, bio-lock): "Passive environmental
  Chrysalis presence is universal on Gaius — even a true Hollow (genetically
  uninfected, never underwent the Rite) accumulates cellular Chrysalis through
  seventeen years of breathing Gaian air." This is the D4 canon distinction:
  Maren is genetically uninfected but environmentally exposed.
- **In the manuscript Ch. 8**: Vane finds in Maren's traces "No trace of the
  thing she'd learned to detect in infected people — a particular baseline
  warmth, present even in the non-mutated and the barely-changed, that came
  from whatever the Chrysalis did to the body's basic processes. It wasn't
  there." And: "the girl smelled like a person and nothing else."
- **The stress test**: If passive cellular Chrysalis is universal on Gaius
  (everyone accumulates it environmentally), then Vane — whose mutation is
  olfactory/sensory — detecting *zero* Chrysalis baseline on Maren contradicts
  the D4 canon. The D4 canon says Maren has passive environmental Chrysalis
  in her cells; Vane's nose says there is no Chrysalis baseline. One of these
  must be wrong, OR Vane's olfactory sense detects active/hosted Chrysalis
  (organism integrated into genetics/expression) but NOT passive environmental
  accumulation.
- **This distinction is not made explicit in the manuscript.** The reader
  cannot tell whether Vane's nose distinguishes between active Chrysalis
  (mutated/transforming) and passive cellular trace. If Vane detects only
  active/expressed Chrysalis, the D4 canon and the manuscript are compatible.
  If Vane's nose detects any Chrysalis presence including passive trace, they
  contradict.
- **Severity**: Major. The resolution depends on a distinction the manuscript
  does not currently establish. A single clarifying sentence in Ch. 8 would
  close this — e.g., that Vane reads the *active hosted* Chrysalis warmth,
  not trace accumulation. As written, a careful reader can construct the
  contradiction.

## 5. Character Knowledge & Continuity

**Two findings.**

**Vane's foreknowledge of Maren's appearance — gap and resolution (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: Vane's contract letter (Ch. 4) says "Female. Young,
  approximately seventeen. Dark hair. No visible change. Last confirmed
  location: Sanctum Basin region, moving north." When Vane finds Maren in
  Ch. 10 she says "You're the one they're looking for." Her recognition is
  based on the smell profile she has been building through Ch. 8 (no
  Chrysalis baseline, distinctive to a tracking-nose). This is established
  and clean.
- **The minor note**: In Ch. 2, the farmers' overheard conversation and the
  man on the horse both describe a girl "dark hair, no change visible." This
  information circulates via roadside rumor. Vane gathers trail evidence in
  Ch. 8 that confirms Maren's movement and smell profile. When Vane says in
  Ch. 10 "I've been following you for nine days" — the timeline is: twelve
  days behind at departure, three-day-old kitchen trace, so she's been on
  the active trail perhaps six to eight days, not quite nine. Again the gap
  arithmetic is tight but not broken.
- **Severity**: Minor.

**Maren's father — what she knows and when (Clean)**

- The manuscript carefully stages what Maren knows and when. Ch. 1: she
  knows he was hollow and left. She thinks of it as abandonment. The deeper
  question ("what did his leaving mean") is not asked until the coal pouch
  is gone. Ch. 5 boundary stone: she explicitly reframes from abandonment
  to "something the bloodline had wanted." Ch. 9: she explicitly revisits —
  "He ran so they'd follow him and not stay to look for me." This is a
  managed, staged revelation, correctly paced. No knowledge fracture.

**Davan's pine carving — temporal paradox acknowledged by bible, managed
in text (Clean)**

- Story Bible (Section 2, Davan): "Known contradictions: The pine carving
  was made before he met Maren." The manuscript handles this by having
  Davan's hands "know the shape" via his empathic sense — the guarded-arm
  posture the carving depicts is a posture his hands arrived at before he
  consciously knew it was a person. The text in Ch. 2 makes this a feature
  of his mutation, not a plot error. Bible confirms this must pay off in
  Ch. 16. Through Ch. 10, the carving is transferred to Vane correctly.
  No fracture in these chapters.

**Aldric's knowledge of the four containers — Ch. 3 vs. Ch. 7 (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: In Ch. 3, Aldric finds the sealed room and examines the five
  objects: "He picked up the nearest one and found it colder than the room
  had any right to make it, and heavier than its size predicted, and unwilling
  to be opened by any pressure his hands could bring to the ridge. He set it
  back. He did not try the others." In Ch. 7 (three weeks later): "He had
  tried the ridges on all of them over the second week — a thing he'd told
  himself he was only confirming."
- **No fracture**: Ch. 3 says he did not try the others on the *first night*.
  Ch. 7 says he tried all of them over the *second week* of his visits. The
  temporal gap resolves this cleanly.

## 6. Logic & Adversarial Stress Tests

**Three findings.**

**The deacon's procedural response to the exposure (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: Deacon Ferris sees the coal pouch slip at Maren's cuff during
  the Rite. He does not interrupt the ceremony — he completes it, moves on to
  the next congregant, and only acts later (the overheard conversation with
  Delia). The text accounts for this: "She understood this. You did not
  interrupt a Rite. You completed it and reported afterward."
- **The stress test**: A trained deacon from New Plymouth, presumably tasked
  with identifying hollows, sees clear evidence of deception during a sacred
  ceremony and... finishes the ceremony normally and reports to the village
  herb woman before presumably filing official reports? The in-text
  justification (ritual propriety) is offered, and it holds for the ceremony
  itself. But the subsequent reporting sequence — to *Delia*, not to a
  secular authority — implies the deacon views Delia as a responsible party,
  presumably because she is the girl's guardian-figure. This is
  culturally plausible and the text doesn't overexplain it. Acceptable.
- **Secondary stress test**: The deacon says "the bloodline has asked to be
  told." The implication is that the Rite is partly a surveillance mechanism
  and the bloodline has standing requests for hollows. The deacon's compliance
  with ritual propriety despite having a surveillance mandate is a mild tension
  the text lets sit without resolving. Not a fracture, but an invitation for
  a reader to ask: wouldn't a bloodline agent interrupt the Rite for this?
  The text implies the protocol has a hierarchy (Rite first, report after)
  that the deacon follows. Plausible.
- **Severity**: Minor.

**Betta's back-door visitor — ambiguity that never resolves (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: Ch. 5, barn: Maren hears a back-door conversation late at
  night. "The outside voice said something. Not a question. A statement made
  quietly... Then Betta. One sentence, also quiet. Then the door." Maren
  spends a paragraph deciding she is probably being paranoid. She leaves
  before dawn.
- In Ch. 8, Vane interviews Betta: "'North and east,' Betta said finally.
  'She was heading for the road.'" Betta is described as someone who "had
  decided something... before Vane had arrived" — she withholds the specific
  direction and circumstances.
- **The stress test**: In Ch. 8, bloodline agents had been "ahead of [Vane]
  in some places and behind her in others." Vane notes that "the girl was not
  predictable." The back-door visitor in Ch. 5 is never identified in these
  chapters. It remains ambiguous whether it was a bloodline agent (Betta then
  sending them north and east = away from the barn) or something else. This
  is clean narrative ambiguity — the text preserves it deliberately. No
  fracture, but the thread is left open through Ch. 10.
- **Severity**: Minor (intentional ambiguity, not error).

**Maren leaving the coin at Betta's door vs. the Corvin coin (Note)**

- **Evidence**: In Ch. 5, Maren tries to pay Corvin for the turnip; he
  refuses, pressing the coin back. She also tries to pay Corvin for the bird;
  he gives it freely. In Ch. 9, she recalls: "the coin refusal had been at
  the cart, the bird at the door, and in her memory they had collapsed into a
  single gesture." When she leaves Betta's barn, she explicitly decides NOT
  to leave coins or herbs: "leaving things behind was leaving a record of the
  direction she'd been going." This is stated security logic, clean.
- The detail is internally consistent. The collapsing of the two Corvin
  gestures in Maren's memory (Ch. 9) is a nice POV move that is not
  contradicted by any other passage. Clean.

**The Chrysalis symbol — "orbital not spiral" first observation (Minor)**

- **Evidence**: In Ch. 3, when Aldric sits in the chapel after evening
  prayers, he looks at the carved symbol: "The inner spiral of the symbol —
  the oldest part... was not quite a spiral. He had always read it as one.
  Up close, with fresh attention, it was a set of concentric arcs that
  described an orbit."
- In Ch. 6, Davan looks at the same type of symbol above the Confluence
  temple door and has his empathic response: "it was a set of concentric arcs
  that described an orbit."
- **The stress test**: Both Aldric (Ch. 3) and Davan (Ch. 6) have the
  "orbital not spiral" realization, but Ch. 3 comes *before* Ch. 6. The
  bible says Davan is "the first character in the book whose empathic sense
  reads the symbol accurately as orbital rather than spiral." But Aldric has
  the same visual realization in Ch. 3. The distinction seems to be that
  Aldric's is an intellectual observation (he's looking with scholar's eyes
  after three weeks of staring at a starmap) while Davan's is an *empathic
  sense* reading (the symbol "feels like" something). The bible's claim
  ("first character whose empathic sense reads it") is probably intended to
  distinguish Davan's intuitive register from Aldric's analytical one. But
  the manuscripts' actual language for Aldric's realization (Ch. 3) and
  Davan's realization (Ch. 6) use near-identical phrasing: "a set of
  concentric arcs that described an orbit." A reader who notices this
  repetition may ask why Davan's version is "first." The distinction needs
  to be clearer in either the ch. 3 passage (make Aldric's more tentative or
  more analytical) or the ch. 6 passage (make Davan's empathic register
  more distinct from a simple visual observation).
- **Severity**: Minor.

## 7. Verdict

The manuscript is substantially cleaner than a pre-revision draft would be.
The architecture holds: geography, object permanence, and character-knowledge
staging are all solid through Ch. 10. The most consequential unfixed issue is
the passive Chrysalis / Vane's nose contradiction (Section 4) — the D4 bible
canon (universal environmental Chrysalis accumulation) and Vane's detection of
*zero* baseline in Maren are incompatible unless the manuscript specifies that
Vane's sense reads only active/expressed Chrysalis, not passive trace. This
is a one-sentence fix with real load-bearing implications for Act 3. The
woodcutter's-shelter timing ambiguity (Section 2) is the second priority:
whether Vane and Maren occupy the shelter back-to-back or overlapping is
currently unresolved by the text, and an overlapping reading creates a logical
impossibility given Ch. 10's structure. This section has converged
significantly from prior passes — there are no Critical issues, and the number
of Majors is lower than the V1/V2 review would have produced; diminishing
returns is a real finding here.
