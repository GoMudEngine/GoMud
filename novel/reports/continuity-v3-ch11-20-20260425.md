# Continuity Audit V3 (Cass) — Ch 11-20

## 1. Geographic & Travel-Time Audit

**Stillwater detour fits but Maren's elapsed time unclear**

Ch. 11 establishes that Vane and Maren travel together from somewhere south of
Stillwater to Stillwater itself. Ch. 14 states Maren has been in New Plymouth
for 8 days. No explicit travel time between Stillwater and New Plymouth is given
in these chapters, which is acceptable; the implied sequence (Stillwater → New
Plymouth road → city arrival) tracks without contradiction.

**Vane's wafer count vs. supply-contact claim — Minor discrepancy**

Ch. 11 opens: Vane has four wafers left "in the tin." She calculates "nine days,
possibly ten" on her current rate of one wafer "every two and a half days,
sometimes two." Four wafers at one every 2.5 days = roughly 10 days — arithmetic
checks out. She also says she told Maren she is going to Stillwater because "I
have a supply contact there." She then resupplies general goods at Ori's general
goods house; there is no Bloom resupply at Stillwater. By Ch. 14, the tin is
described as being checked "twice a day" and near-empty. By Ch. 18, "one wafer
left. Maybe none" with the last wafer taken "three days ago."

This is internally consistent but raises a minor question: Vane told Maren she
had a supply contact in Stillwater, and nothing came of it — the pivot from
Stillwater to New Plymouth happens before any contact is made. Vane's stated
reason for the Stillwater detour was this contact. The reader understands the
pivot (she turns back to Maren, the contact was a pretext or was abandoned in
the decision), but Maren's line "You're coming to New Plymouth" and Vane's
response treat the detour as already over. If scrutinized, a reader could ask:
did Vane see her contact or not? The text never closes this loop.

**Severity: Minor** — No hard contradiction, but the Stillwater supply contact
disappears without acknowledgment.

## 2. Temporal Inconsistencies

**Bloom wafer arithmetic: Ch. 11 vs. Ch. 18**

This is the most traceable numeric thread and it mostly holds, with one wrinkle.

- Ch. 11 opening: four wafers remaining. Rate: one every ~2.5 days, sometimes
  two. Estimated supply: 9-10 days.
- Ch. 14: tin checked twice daily, checking frequency now reduced (going "from
  twice a day to not at all," which implies either nearly empty or she stopped
  looking). Maren says the tin is near-empty based on observation.
- Ch. 18: Vane checks tin with "a brief precise motion, thumb across the clasp.
  Confirmed what she already knew. One wafer left. Maybe none." And: "the last
  wafer she'd taken, three days ago."

The "three days ago" line in Ch. 18 is consistent with near-depletion (she had
one wafer and sat with it for three days rather than taking it). The tin
carrying one possible wafer is internally plausible. However, the 9-10 day
estimate from Ch. 11 and the journey to New Plymouth (days of travel + 8+ days
in the city by Ch. 14) puts the elapsed time between Ch. 11 and Ch. 18 at
roughly 2-3 weeks, meaning she should have been fully depleted well before Ch.
18. The arithmetic implies she was already past her estimated supply window by
Ch. 14 or shortly after.

This is not a hard contradiction — the estimate was "nine days, possibly ten"
and she may have rationed further than the estimate implied — but the counting
is loose enough that an adversarial reader will notice the gap.

**Severity: Minor** — The arithmetic is strained but not impossible. The text
never claims she stuck to the 2.5-day rate after the road.

**Father's letter timing: "five years ago" in Ch. 11, "ten years ago" in
Ch. 14**

Ch. 11: Maren reads Ulla's letter containing her father's words. She reports to
Vane: "My father came through Stillwater five years ago. Heading east, traveling
fast, didn't stop more than a day. He said he was going to the hill."

Ch. 14: Maren's internal narration references her father's going east and not
coming back. She thinks: "Her father had gone east with it five years ago."
This is consistent with Ch. 11.

However, the STORY_BIBLE (Section 7, Author Canon — Locked Decisions) states:
"Maren's father is dead. He went east **ten years ago** and did not return."
The bible also notes, in the Ch. 27 canon, that his remains are present at the
site.

The manuscript says **five years** in both Ch. 11 and Ch. 14. The bible says
**ten years**. This is a direct conflict between the manuscript and the locked
author canon.

**Severity: Critical** — The manuscript says "five years ago" in two chapters;
the locked author canon says "ten years ago." If Maren is 17 and her father
left when she was 7 (ten years ago), that shapes what she remembers of him.
If he left when she was 12 (five years ago), she has significantly more
conscious memory of him, which affects the emotional texture of the arc and
the Ch. 30 childhood-memory callback ("He had been making her look"). This
must be reconciled. The bible entry is marked as locked author canon; the
manuscript needs to be updated to "ten years" or the bible needs revision.

## 3. Item & Object Permanence

**Coal pouch: present in Ch. 11, not mentioned after**

Ch. 11 establishes the coal pouch as a habit Vane notes: "She had the disc in
her hand without remembering having reached for it" — the disc is tracked
continuously. The STORY_BIBLE notes Maren carries the coal pouch "habit even
after fleeing." The pouch itself is not explicitly mentioned in Chs. 12-20.
Its absence is not a contradiction (she no longer needs the deception) but the
bible flags it as an ongoing carried item. No problem here — the silence reads
naturally.

**Found nothing to flag on permanence.**

**Pine carving tracking: clean**

The carving is introduced in Ch. 15 (Davan deciding to send it), received in
Ch. 16 (Maren opening the bag), held and examined at the cooperage yard
meeting, pocketed by Maren at the end of Ch. 16 ("I'm keeping it"), and
confirmed in her pocket in Ch. 20 ("The disc was in her coat, in the inner
pocket..."). The pine figure is distinct from the disc and the two are tracked
separately without confusion. Clean.

**Davan's driftwood carving: slight inconsistency**

Ch. 12 describes Davan carving the inner orbit symbol into a piece of driftwood
on the dock. Maren sees it; the driftwood becomes the visual trigger for the
first meeting. The driftwood carving is not the same as the pine carving he
sends via Pip. The text is clear on this distinction, but in Ch. 15, Pip tells
Davan that Edvar described a woman "had something in her coat pocket she kept
touching" — which is the disc, established in Ch. 12. The driftwood is left on
the dock and not retrieved. No continuity error; tracking is clean.

**Disc on table at Ch. 20 cooperage meeting: confirmed**

Ch. 20: Maren sets the disc on the table. It pulses once. The container sits
beside it inert. This matches the STORY_BIBLE exactly: "Maren sets disc on
table; it pulses once, visible as a 'deepening' of the dark surface — everyone
in the room goes quiet." The container's inertness is also confirmed by the
bible. Clean.

**Hand-disc grounding: no issue found in Chs. 11-20**

The disc-as-grounding for Vane during Bloom episodes is established in prior
chapters. In Chs. 11-20 the disc is tracked as Maren's object; Vane does not
hold it. No continuity error.

## 4. Lore / Canon Contradictions

**Inner orbit symbol description: Ch. 20 vs. STORY_BIBLE (LOCKED)**

This is the most significant lore-level finding in this section.

The STORY_BIBLE, Section 5 (Inner Orbit Symbol), states the locked physical
description as: **"four nested arcs with raised points along their paths; the
fourth arc drifts off-true, breaking the nested-arc pattern at a tangent."**
This is marked as LOCKED (Ch. 9 manuscript source).

Ch. 20, when Maren describes the symbol to Renner's group:

> "Four arcs, nested. The outermost one runs off the line the others share —
> drifts away at the end. It's on the edge of the disc."

This matches the locked bible description. **No contradiction here.**

However: earlier in Ch. 15, the cooperage discussion frames the symbol
description differently. Ch. 12 (Davan carving the symbol repeatedly) and Ch.
15 (the cooperage meeting with Corvel's container) both reference the "inner
orbit" without a fresh physical description. This is fine; they reference it as
known to the characters who have been working with it.

The concern surfaced by the audit assignment — "especially the inner-orbit
symbol description in Ch. 20" — appears to be resolved. The description
Maren gives in Ch. 20 is consistent with the locked bible canon.

**Found nothing to flag here.** The inner orbit description is consistent.

**Edvar's face display: Ch. 19 vs. STORY_BIBLE**

The bible specifies Edvar's face shows the fourth shape as "smaller, as if
further away — NOT irregular or damaged from this perspective." This contrasts
deliberately with Aldric's face (fourth shape = "irregular, damaged"). Ch. 19
manuscript: "the fourth was reduced. A point that was almost a shape. Something
that had been there when this record was made, caught at the limits of whatever
instrument or eye had captured the arrangement." And: "The fourth shape. Smaller,
as if further away."

This matches the bible specification exactly. Clean.

**Corvel's container timeline: resolved in Ch. 19 and Ch. 15**

Ch. 15, Pip says: "Eight years. He brought it to the group eight years ago,
when he found it." Ch. 19, Edvar: "Corvel first brought it to the group, eight
years ago." Both consistent with the bible's locked resolution ("8 years is
canon"). Clean.

However: there is a small internal gap. Pip says "when he found it" — implying
Corvel brought it immediately to the group. Edvar says essentially the same. But
in Ch. 19, Edvar describes the container's clay-deposit origin as "what surveys
date to the Quiet Century window" — implying the geological context is known.
This is a minor loose end (how does Edvar know the clay surveys?) but it is
exactly the kind of background detail that can be inferred without contradiction.
Not flagging as an error.

**The bloodline's account of Chrysalis: Renner's cooperage argument**

Ch. 15 and Ch. 20 both have Renner argue that Founding-era objects respond to
"unchanged hands" — the absence of Chrysalis modification. The bible (Section
4) confirms this is correct as far as the cooperage group understands it, with
the important caveat that the full truth (passive cellular Chrysalis in everyone,
including Maren) is only revealed in Ch. 29. The cooperage group's position in
these chapters is intentionally incomplete but not wrong-enough to be a
continuity error within the story's information architecture. Clean.

## 5. Character Knowledge & Continuity

**Davan/Maren first-meeting beats: Ch. 12 vs. Ch. 16**

Ch. 12 (dock): Davan and Maren exchange names only. He does not show her the
driftwood carving proactively — she sees it from six feet away. She shows him
the disc edge (angled toward light, not offered). No conversation beyond names.
He does not take the pine carving out of his pocket. The interaction is a silent
recognition scene.

Ch. 16 (cooperage yard): This is described as their "proper meeting." Maren
receives the pine carving via Pip before this meeting. At the yard, she shows
Davan the carving: "You carved this before we met." He confirms it. They
exchange substantial information — the disc, her being hollow, the hill, four-
to-three count, the cooperage group. This is a first real conversation after
the dock's silence.

These two meetings are consistent with each other and with the STORY_BIBLE's
framing ("dock meeting Ch. 12; proper meeting Ch. 16"). The information
exchanged in Ch. 16 builds correctly on what Ch. 12 established.

**One potential soft issue:** In Ch. 14, Vane tells Maren that the young man
on the dock "has been carving [the symbol] repeatedly, from what I could see."
This implies Vane was observing multiple evenings of dock carving, not just the
one night of the first meeting. The text supports this — Vane's pattern
surveillance is established — but Ch. 12 only depicts one evening in detail.
The implication of Vane having "been watching" multiple evenings is plausible
given her character, but it is not shown on-page. No contradiction, mild gap.

**Severity: Minor** — Plausible, not contradicted.

**Vane's knowledge of Davan: Ch. 14**

Ch. 14, Vane describes Davan: "He came to New Plymouth the same way you did —
without a plan except north." She knows his arrival direction and docking-work
cover. Her nose and observation are the explained mechanism; this is consistent
with her established competence. The detail about him arriving from the south
"within a week of when you arrived" is plausible for a tracker of her skill
given she has been watching the dock area. No contradiction.

**Aldric's knowledge state: Ch. 13 → Ch. 17**

Ch. 13: Aldric reads Brennan's letter (eight pages, the Fathers' Boats
tradition, the Restricted Collection material, the "unchanged hands" annotation,
the warning about watchers). He decides to go to Greenford. Ch. 17: He arrives
in Greenford, meets Brennan and Reth. This is in correct sequence. The details
Aldric knows when he arrives at Reth's house (the face in the undercroft, the
four shapes) are exactly what he established in Ch. 3/7/13. His interior
narration in Ch. 17 does not over-extend — he does not know things he shouldn't.
Clean.

**Aldric leaving Cael a letter: Ch. 13**

He writes to Cael, walks it to the administrative block. When he returns, the
door is ajar. Crane is in his room. The sequence: he left for 8 minutes,
Crane was in his room, the pencil is wrong. He then decides to carry Brennan's
letter rather than leave it. The logical chain holds. One small note: the text
says "he went to his desk and opened the drawer where he kept the journal" —
he checks the journal but carries the letter. The journal stays. This is his
choice as written. No issue.

## 6. Logic & Adversarial Stress Tests

**Vane burns Cade's supply operation: timing logic**

Ch. 14: Vane reports she will make a constabulary report about Cade's supply
house. Her stated reason is tactical: generate official attention in the docks
district, making Horst's invisible grid-search expensive. The text acknowledges
this also burns her own future supply. This is internally coherent.

The adversarial question: if Vane knows Horst is in the city (she tells Maren
this on "the sixth day" in New Plymouth), and she burns Cade's operation on
roughly day 5-6, the constabulary attention would have been in place for perhaps
1-2 days before Ch. 18 (when she goes to Deren). During that same window, Deren
apparently receives and processes her contact request without difficulty.
This suggests the constabulary action disrupted Cade's network without touching
Deren's separate, deeper operation. The text treats Deren as a different
network; this is plausible given the description of him as operating "deeper
current" than Cade. No logical impossibility.

**Deren's room matches Vane's visions exactly: retroactive logic**

The STORY_BIBLE establishes that Vane's involuntary visions showed her the
ceiling, the corridor, the lamplight — the source of the Bloom. In Ch. 18, she
walks down the stairs and recognizes the corridor as the one she'd been seeing
in visions. The text executes this cleanly: "She knew this corridor. She'd been
seeing it for months." The mechanism (psychic bleed-through from the Bloom-giver
via the wafer) is established in the bible. Clean.

However, one logic stress: Vane's visions have been showing her "ceiling, stone,
underground, stationary" since Ch. 8 (per the bible). In Ch. 18, she finally
acts on the lead to see a new supplier. Why, over the months of visions, did she
not connect these to a source location earlier? The text addresses this: "She'd
catalogued the visions and never once followed the thread to its source because
following the thread would have required standing where she was standing now."
This is a character explanation (willful compartmentalization), not a logic
error, and the text makes it explicit. Passes the stress test.

**Ch. 20: Renner's framework collapse on disc pulse**

Ch. 20 ends with the disc pulsing on the table. The text notes Renner's
framework breaking: "Renner went still — not because it had pulsed, but because
the pulse was *specific*, and her framework did not account for specificity." The
specificity point is that the disc responded to Maren's presence, not to the
room at large.

Adversarial flag: the reader at this point doesn't have enough information about
how Renner's framework works to understand why specificity breaks it rather than
confirming it. Renner has been arguing "unchanged hands" = the trait, not the
person. A disc that pulses when an unchanged person is in the room could be read
as confirming her framework. The text says the disc "answered the person" rather
than "the room," but the disc was set down by Maren — the only unchanged person
present — so from a reader's vantage, it's ambiguous whether it's responding to
her proximity or to the material property. The text treats this as obvious to
Renner but it is not fully earned on the page.

**Severity: Minor** — Renner's emotional response is legible but the reasoning
behind it needs one more beat to be fully logical. This is a story-craft issue
rather than a continuity error.

**Davan's pine carving: temporal plausibility**

The carving was made "on the road north, weeks before the dock." Davan carves
Maren's guarded-arm posture before meeting her. The text explicitly frames this
as his empathic mutation reading something across a distance — not prescience,
but a physical sensitivity that anticipated a person's characteristic posture.
The STORY_BIBLE notes this as a known "contradiction" (the pine carving was made
before he met Maren; must pay off Ch. 16). The payoff in Ch. 16 is present and
executed — Maren recognizes the posture, holds it without comment for a long
time, keeps the carving. The in-world mechanism (his hands "find things before
he does") is established through his mutation. The reader must accept the
empathic-sense explanation; the text does not over-explain it.

No continuity error. The payoff executes as the bible specifies.

**Aldric's lamp tic: tracked through Chs. 13 and 17**

Ch. 13: "He pinched the desk lamp out of habit — still burning, had been burning
for an hour, he pinched it anyway and sat in the dimmer light before he caught
himself and relit it." Ch. 17 (road west, after Reth): "The lamp tic found him
twice on the road — once at a waypoint where he pinched a candle stub beside
the register, once reaching for a lantern at the stable where he changed horses.
Both times he caught himself." This is exactly the bible's specification: "caught
himself twice on the road to Greenford (Ch. 17)." The road in Ch. 17 is the
return road west to New Plymouth, not the outbound road to Greenford — the
sequence is (Ch. 13 leaves Confluence → Greenford → Ch. 17 returns west). The
bible says "road to Greenford" but the text catches him on the return road. This
is a very minor labeling inconsistency in the bible vs. manuscript (the bible
says westward road, the text says road to Greenford = outbound east). No
narrative harm.

**Severity: Minor** — Direction of travel where tic is caught differs slightly
between bible and manuscript.

## 7. Verdict

This section of the manuscript is substantially clean. The most significant
finding is the **father's absence duration** — "five years" in two manuscript
chapters (Ch. 11 and Ch. 14) versus the bible's locked "ten years" canon. This
is a Critical continuity issue with arc implications (how much of her father does
Maren consciously remember) and must be reconciled. Everything else found in
this pass is Minor or resolved: the Bloom wafer arithmetic is strained but not
broken, the Stillwater supply-contact loop is left open without contradiction,
and the inner orbit symbol description in Ch. 20 is consistent with the locked
bible canon. Compared to what would have been flagged in earlier passes —
character voice issues, cooperage scene structure, disc behavior introduction —
this pass finds significantly fewer issues. The revision work has been effective.
The manuscript in Chs. 11-20 is in good structural shape; the father-timeline
discrepancy is the one item requiring immediate correction.
