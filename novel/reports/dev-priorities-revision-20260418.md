## Priority 3c — Maren still misreading the disc

**Chose wrong theory (1): the disc as a locator for her father's remains.** Reasons:

- Most poignant. Maren has been reframing the father-abandoned-us story throughout Act I (Ch. 9 and Ch. 11 beats). A private belief that the disc will lead her to his grave continues that arc and gives her somewhere emotional, not just intellectual, to reverse from in Ch. 20.
- Drafter's note in the bible confirms Maren's father is dead (author canon), and the Ch. 27 candidate placement is "finding his remains." The misreading prepares the reader for a father-remains encounter; the Ch. 20 reversal is that the disc is not *for* the body — it is a *key.* Reversal from "marker for a grave I cannot stand at" to "test I have been carrying" lands on both registers (father + test).
- Option 2 (destination-marker) is too close to the truth — dev editor explicitly cautioned against. Option 3 (destroy-it-before-they-take-it) contradicts her established stubborn curiosity and breaks voice.

**Where:** Ch. 14, in the "she thought about the disc" passage, immediately after her reasoning about her father going east ten years ago and not returning.

**The addition:** Maren handles the disc without remembering she reached for it. She has been reading *The disc will open it* as a lock-instruction, picturing a door in a hillside. But her father is gone, nobody found him, and a man who walked into a hill and did not walk out is somewhere in the hill or at its foot. She reframes: she has been holding the disc "the way you held a marker for a grave you had not been allowed to stand at." The warmth in her palm becomes "her father's temperature, preserved" — something that would tell her where he had fallen. *The disc will open it* — open the ground, open the hill's silence, give her back the body no one had returned. "It was a reading. She tested it and it held. It was the first reading that had allowed her to want to keep carrying the disc instead of dreading it."

This gives her a specific wrong theory she can act under through the dock meeting (Ch. 12 retrospectively), the cooperage yard (Ch. 16), and up to the Ch. 20 v2 reversal. Ch. 20 v2's *not a keepsake, a test* now reverses from *marker for a grave*, which is a structurally deeper reversal than *keepsake* — grief-object to test-object instead of sentimental-object to functional-object.
