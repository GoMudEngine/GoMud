# Prose & Voice Audit V3 (Vee) — Ch 11-20

---

## 1. POV Voice Drift

### Vane (Chs. 11, 18)

Ch. 11 is the strongest Vane chapter in the manuscript so far. The
bloom-realization sequence — the wafer, the smell, "the compartment did not
hold" — earns its length and stays inside Vane's professional-analysis-as-
feeling voice signature. One wobble:

**Issue: Slight sentimentality creep**
Evidence: *"It smelled, she thought, like a job in which the agreement and
the actual work had stopped being the same thing."*
Severity: Low. The metaphor works, but it's a shade more lyrical than Vane's
register elsewhere — she tends toward cost-yield inventory, not metaphor. The
sentence is good enough to keep, but it's the one place where the voice slips
into the narrator's idiom rather than staying wholly inside Vane's.

Ch. 18 is the best single chapter in these ten. The prose-block format earns
the compression. The cost-benefit analysis in the street is exactly right:
Vane using professional tools on a problem they cannot contain. The tin
weighing "almost nothing" as a load-bearing image for the whole chapter. No
voice drift detected. The decision to stop buying without freeing the woman
is tonally perfect and consistent with the Story Bible's "Does NOT free the
person Ch. 18" note. This chapter is what the voice signature was built to do.

### Davan (Chs. 12, 15, 19)

Davan's open-grained, circling voice is mostly reliable. His best moments
are the dock scene (Ch. 12), where what he doesn't say carries more weight
than what he does, and the Edvar revelation in Ch. 19 ("I have never said any
of this to another person"). One recurring issue:

**Issue: Over-explaining what the green-gold means**
Evidence (Ch. 12): *"the blue was the blue that came when a thing was true
and he did not yet know what to do with the truth."*
Evidence (Ch. 19): *"The green-gold at his collarbones flared and held. The
recognition-register, his body's way of acknowledging what his mind was still
assembling."*
Evidence (Ch. 15): *"his skin-light stirred faintly under his coat sleeves,
responding to the proximity before he could suppress it."*
Severity: Medium. These explanatory glosses are editorial voice — the narrator
stepping in to make sure readers don't miss the signal. Davan's voice
processes by circling and half-thought; he would feel the amber or the green-
gold before he'd label it. The label flattens the register that is the entire
point of his POV. Trust readers to accumulate understanding across the three
chapters. Cut or heavily trim the explanatory clauses ("The recognition-
register," "his body's way of acknowledging") — let the color arrive without
the footnote.

**Issue: One reaching half-thought**
Evidence (Ch. 15): *"like a green board left to season — the kind that held
its tension in the grain, that you could feel before you touched it, that
would eventually move. He could feel it. It was warm."*
Severity: Low. The extended woodworker's metaphor is in-character but goes
one clause too far. "He could feel it. It was warm" restates what the image
already showed. Cut the last sentence.

### Aldric (Chs. 13, 17 — prior pass added stumbles in both)

Ch. 13 is the chapter most improved from prior audit notes. The liturgical-
vs-scholarly conflict is now audible: *"providence" where "procedure" belongs*
is explicitly placed on the page, and the lamp-pinch tic is used with craft
throughout. The Crane scene (pencil on wrong side, displaced chair) is the
tightest scene in the Aldric chapters and possibly in the manuscript.

One remaining inconsistency:

**Issue: Voice drift in the journal entry**
Evidence: *"I leave the room below as it is. I leave the light to face what
comes. I leave with less certainty than I arrived with, which is the condition
I was taught to seek and turns out to be considerably less comfortable in
practice than in theory."*
Severity: Low-Medium. This is the only moment in Ch. 13 where Aldric sounds
like a wise man rather than a frightened one. The journal entry is internally
consistent (private, shorthand), but the third clause — "which is the condition
I was taught to seek and turns out to be considerably less comfortable in
practice than in theory" — has a wry ease that doesn't match the earlier
register of a man whose pencil is on the wrong side of his journal and who
has just understood the institution is part of the situation. That discovery
should produce something less composed.

Ch. 17 is clean by the standards of prior passes. The lamp tic twice caught
and released on the road is exactly right. Brennan's house as a physical
externalization of Aldric's mental landscape is one of the better bits of
environmental work in these ten chapters. The Reth scene is strong —
particularly the moment where Reth stops mid-sentence and the silence is the
description. No significant drift found. The closing road-ride reflection
runs slightly long but stays in voice.

**Issue: One authorial intrusion (see section 3)**
The ending of Ch. 17 — *"three is a number that described a loss"* — see
section 3.

### Maren (Chs. 14, 16, 20 — Ch. 20 final interiority revised to remove
"in a world that had decided")

Ch. 14 is competent. Maren's compressed watchfulness is present throughout.
The knife lesson is the best scene — Vane teaching through information-not-
encouragement and Maren's kitchen knife framing are both character-true. The
extended disc-meditation in the bedroom (carrying grief / carrying a test) is
one of the two places where her interior gets long enough to risk becoming
self-explanatory.

Ch. 16 is excellent. The moment where Maren refuses to explain the tucked arm
to Vane is one of the most disciplined character decisions in the book — she
lets Vane miss the point and keeps it, because some recognitions belong to
specific people. The cooperage yard scene earns its two hours.

Ch. 20 final interiority was revised. The revision holds. The "carrying grief
/ carrying a test" revelation lands cleanly and without over-reach. The voice
is contained.

One issue in Ch. 14:

**Issue: Maren interior goes longer than the voice warrants**
Evidence: *"He hadn't come back. She didn't know if that meant he'd failed or
succeeded or been caught, and not knowing was the kind of gap she couldn't
fill by thinking harder, so she stopped trying to fill it."*
Severity: Low. The diagnosis ("not knowing was the kind of gap she couldn't
fill by thinking harder") is slightly long for Maren's compressed register;
she tends toward shorter declarative. A character who "catalogues the room for
exits before engaging people" would cut to "so she stopped" and move on. The
explanatory clause leaks the narrator's voice into hers.

## 2. Narrator Leak / Authorial Intrusion

**Issue: Explicit emotional labeling — Ch. 17 closing**
Evidence: *"three is a number that described a loss."*
Severity: Medium. Aldric has been counting the moons for chapters. The
symbol, Reth's account, the twelve maps — all of it has been building this
knowledge. The sentence states what the reader already knows, in the
narrator's language rather than Aldric's interior. Compare to how Davan
handles the same knowledge in Ch. 19: "It came down" — two words that contain
the same weight without explaining it. The Ch. 17 intrusion could be cut
entirely; the preceding image (Aldric looking at the sky, three moons, "the
count had not settled") is sufficient.

**Issue: Commentary on the Crane encounter — Ch. 13**
Evidence: *"The encounter was a doctrine: this is what I can do, and this is
how I will describe what I have done, and the description is the thing the
institution is built to receive."*
Severity: Low-Medium. This is mostly in-voice — Aldric would think this
clearly; he's a man of thirty years' institutional processing. But the
formulation "The encounter was a doctrine" is more aphoristic than Aldric's
register elsewhere, and "this is the thing the institution is built to
receive" tips into the narrator's perspective rather than the character's
experienced thought. Trim or recast in a less epigram-shaped form.

**Issue: Ch. 16, explaining Vane's nod**
Evidence: *"Vane nodded. Once. The smallest possible movement, an
acknowledgment that Maren couldn't fully read — not approval, not dismissal,
something more private than either, something that belonged to whatever Vane's
nose was telling her about the person standing in the doorway."*
Severity: Low. "Maren couldn't fully read" is correct and in-POV. What
follows — the triple not/not/something-more-private — is the narrator
offering alternative framings to ensure the reader feels the ambiguity. Let
the gesture be unread. "An acknowledgment Maren couldn't fully read" is the
full sentence.

## 3. Sensory Filter Verbs

These chapters are substantially cleaner than V1/V2 reports would have
flagged. Most sensory work is direct. Two holdouts:

**Issue: "She could feel" and "she could hear" constructions**
Evidence (Ch. 11): *"she heard him ask a question"*
Evidence (Ch. 14): *"She could feel it against her ribs — the warmth, the
faint rhythm, the pulse."*
Evidence (Ch. 20): *"she felt it against her ribs."*
Severity: Low. Minor — the locutions are few enough to be acceptable. In the
Ch. 14 instance, "she could feel it" is immediately followed by content that
earns the setup; the filter verb is redundant but not deadening. Worth a quick
pass on the whole range but not a significant issue at this stage.

## 4. Telling Where Showing Was Available

Only one clear instance across ten chapters — a meaningful improvement from
earlier-pass patterns.

**Issue: Pip's introduction**
Evidence (Ch. 12): *"He knew New Plymouth the way a good river pilot knew a
stretch of water — not by map but by attention, by having been wrong enough
times to understand where the current actually ran."*
Severity: Low. This is good telling in Davan's observational voice. The
analogy is character-appropriate. But "by having been wrong enough times to
understand where the current actually ran" is the narrator explaining what the
analogy already showed. Cut after "by attention."

**Issue: Explaining Asha's directness**
Evidence (Ch. 15): *"She had a quality Davan associated with people who worked
with their hands — a directness of gaze, an assessment moving from the face
to the hands and back."*
Severity: Minimal. She shows the directness immediately after. The explanation
is unnecessary — the quality lands on its own.

## 5. Repetition & Echo Phrases

**Issue: "the way" construction overuse — Davan chapters**
The phrase "the way X" appears frequently in Davan's chapters as a
subordinate connector:
*"the way he'd turned the driftwood at the dock"*
*"the way a long walk home settled in your legs"*
*"the way you build anything worth having"*
*"the way water worked its way through stone"*
*"the way Edvar must have looked at the formation"*
Severity: Low-Medium. Each individual instance is effective. The cumulative
frequency tips into mannerism, particularly across Ch. 15 and Ch. 19 in
sequence. Thin by one or two in each chapter — keep the strongest.

**Issue: "Carrying grief" / "carrying a test" echo**
Evidence: *"She had carried it for years thinking she was carrying grief. She
was carrying a test."* (Ch. 20 close)
The "carrying grief" image also appears in Ch. 14: *"She went to sleep with
the warmth of it against her ribs, and it felt like a hand she could not quite
reach."*
Severity: Low — this is intentional callback structure. The echo is earned.
Note for authors: the Ch. 14 version ("a hand she could not quite reach") is
the more specific and emotionally precise rendering; the Ch. 20 version is the
explicit reframe. Both should stay.

**Issue: "In its way" construction**
Evidence (Ch. 11): *"It was, in its way, the most accurate possible assessment
of their situation."*
Evidence (Ch. 16): *"This was, in its way, familiar now."*
Severity: Minimal. Both are in voice. The construction is a Vane marker (self-
aware qualification). The repetition is thin enough to note but not repair.

## 6. Cliché, Adverb Cruft, Beat-Sheet Sentences

**Issue: "Something eased in his face"**
Evidence (Ch. 16): *"Something eased in his face — a tension she hadn't known
was there releasing, the way a held breath changes a face and only becomes
visible in the letting go."*
Severity: Low. "Something eased in his face" is a common move; the extension
redeems it. But "the way a held breath changes a face and only becomes visible
in the letting go" is doing the reader's work for them. The gesture — tension
released — is already delivered by "something eased." Cut from "the way" to
end.

**Issue: "the quality of the silence"**
Occurs four times in Chs. 14-20 across different POVs:
*"the quality of the entry"* (Ch. 14)
*"the quality of the quiet"* (Ch. 14, twice)
*"the silence had a different texture"* (Ch. 20)
Severity: Low. The "quality of" construction is a house style for this
manuscript and mostly works. Four instances across six chapters is manageable.
The slightly fresher Ch. 20 variant ("different texture") suggests the authors
are aware of the repetition and working around it.

**Issue: Beat-sheet sentence — Ch. 15 cooperage meeting close**
Evidence: *"You moved through it. The rest came."*
Severity: Low. This is the one moment in the Davan chapters where the wisdom-
epigram voice takes over from the open-grained observer. Davan doesn't land on
aphorisms; he circles and leaves things half-formed. This sentence has the
wrong architecture for his register.

## 7. Dialogue Ring

Ch. 15 cooperage debate (Renner vs. Asha on circular evidence) is the best
sustained dialogue in these chapters. It sounds like people who have been
having this argument for years — the interruptions, the "I was getting there
slowly" / "I was getting there thoroughly" exchange, Corvel's single measured
interjection. No dialogue ring.

One exception:

**Issue: Ch. 11 — "That's not a reason to release a contract. It's not a
reason to walk back."**
Evidence: Maren: *"That's a reason to release a contract. It's not a reason
to walk back."*
Severity: Low. The sentence is too well-shaped — too precisely balanced — for
a girl on a road asking a genuine question. Maren's voice under stress is
short declarative. This reads like a writing exercise in antithesis rather
than improvised dialogue. Consider: "You already knew that."/ "You could have
just — not come back." Rougher, less sculptured.

## 8. Paragraphs That Earn Their Page Space

**Strongest passage — Ch. 18, the cost-benefit analysis in the street:**

*Cost of continued use: the body on the pallet. Not abstractly — she'd had
the abstraction for months and it hadn't stopped her. Concretely. That
specific ceiling. That specific breathing. The cloth on the restraints changed
by someone who kept things clean. The small white scar at the corner of the
mouth.*

This earns every word. The switch from "abstractly" to "concretely" and then
the itemized specifics — ceiling, breathing, cloth changed by someone who
"kept things clean" (worse than neglect, not better), the scar — that last
detail turns the abstract moral problem into an irreducible person. The
fragment "Not abstractly — she'd had the abstraction for months and it hadn't
stopped her" is the best use of Vane's self-aware professional voice in the
manuscript. It does not excuse her; it makes her culpability specific and
therefore real.

**Second strongest — Ch. 13, Aldric reading the rhyme:**

*He had spoken the line ten thousand times and had always read "came down" as
blessing — light descending, the sacred gift. He read it now and the word
"down" had a different gravity to it. Things that came down did not always
come down on purpose. The liturgy had been telling him for thirty years. He
had been repeating it without hearing it.*

The move from "came down" as blessing to "down" as physical fact — and then
"the liturgy had been telling him for thirty years" — is elegantly compact. The
sentence "He had been repeating it without hearing it" is both character truth
and one of the novel's structural arguments delivered without announcing itself.

**Third — Ch. 16, Maren and the carving:**

*She knew the posture. She'd been holding her arm like that for three years —
the arm with no coal-dark palm, the arm she'd learned to keep close and
angled away, the arm that could end her if a stranger saw the wrong thing at
the wrong moment. She'd stopped thinking of it as a conscious choice somewhere
in the second year. It had become architecture.*

"It had become architecture" is the correct word for what three years of
hiding does to a body. The passage earns that word by being specific first
("coal-dark palm," "angled away") before landing on the abstraction.

## 9. Verdict

This is a manuscript that has substantially addressed its earlier-pass
problems. The two most-flagged issues from prior reviews — Aldric's missing
liturgical-scholarly conflict, and Vane's interiority being too withheld — are
now resolved in the direction the Story Bible prescribes. Ch. 18 is
publishable at this draft level. Ch. 13's Crane scene and Ch. 16's cooperage
yard are close behind.

What remains is fine-grain work rather than structural repair: the Davan skin-
light over-explanation pattern (Medium), the narrator intrusion at Ch. 17's
close and Ch. 13's "doctrine" sentence (Low-Medium), and the scattered beat-
sheet sentences in Davan's chapters. None of these require a revision pass of
any scope — they are single-line corrections. The manuscript is past the point
where a full prose pass is warranted; it needs a targeted surgical trim, not
another draft.

If earlier reviews would have flagged twenty significant issues across this
range, V3 finds four at Medium severity and nine at Low. Diminishing returns
has been reached. The prose is doing what it's supposed to do.
