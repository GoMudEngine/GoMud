## Aldric voice signature match

**Assessment: The chapter's strongest sustained Aldric voice in the draft
sequence. The two-languages-in-conflict operates throughout without
tip-over into self-parody.**

The opening watch passage establishes both Aldric registers simultaneously:

> "And so the watch had become, in its way, honest."

This is the formal institutional man granting theological weight to
a secular hour — the chapelmaster's vocabulary applied to a roadside
camp. The architecture of the sentence (two long qualifying clauses
landing on a short declarative) is the bible's confirmed signature.

The lamp-tic opening is the chapter's best deployment of the two-register
collision. The *pastoral trim* reaches up from the priestly body-memory
while the hand reaches for a cold wick; the scholarly and the ecclesiastical
are not named here, they are *enacted* — the hand moving on its own thirty-
year training. This is a stronger deployment than anything in Ch. 21 because
the collision arrives through physical action, not through the three-way
vocabulary argument the docks district passage in Ch. 21 required.

The institutional-reframe passage (mid-chapter, riding through Irongate
gap) is the chapter's central voice test. Assessment below.

## Two-languages-in-conflict: liturgical register

**Anchor count: 4 distinct anchors across three scenes. Not saturating.**

1. *Lauds / sanctuary lamp / pastoral trim* (opening watch, ll. 14-36):
   The chapel vocabulary is embedded in physical memory — the hand reaching
   for the pastoral trim in the dark. This is the bible's "involuntary
   liturgical language appears when confronted with the object" deployed at
   the tic level. Strongest of the four.

2. *The institution / receiver* (spring stop, ll. 159-161):
   "He was the receiver the institution had trained up. He would receive."
   The language of ecclesiastical formation applied to listening to Maren's
   disc-count. Not marked with italics or vocabulary flags — the
   institutional vocabulary does the work quietly. This is correctly
   calibrated: Ch. 25's lying is behind him, so the register surfaces as
   residue rather than performance.

3. *The liturgical word / different rooms* (face comparison, ll. 369-370):
   "the liturgical word was trying to come up to meet the scientific one,
   and he let them come together this time; he was tired of keeping them
   in different rooms." This is the most explicit naming of the two-register
   conflict in the chapter. It works because Aldric names it consciously —
   he has earned this naming by the chapter's position in the arc (the lying
   is over; he can be direct about his own registers). The bible's "self-
   aware about his own lying" quality is here transferred cleanly to self-
   awareness about his own vocabulary. The carry-through is accurate.

4. *Wayside-chapels / tallow dips* (closing fire passage, ll. 512-513):
   Ecclesiastical vocabulary arrives as inventory — not reflection, not
   performance. He lists the lamps he has tended; the list is what a
   Keeper's life produces when reviewed as a ledger. In Vane's register
   this would be professional stock-taking; in Aldric's, the same motion
   produces chapel inventory. The distinction is audible.

**No saturation. The register does not tip into self-parody.** The four
anchors are spaced across four distinct scenes. The institutional-reframe
passage (see below) uses the vocabulary structurally, not decoratively,
and its landing earns the weight it carries.

**One place the register goes thin:** The debris-field enumeration dialogue
(ll. 429-480: "At least four separate sites... Sixty miles") uses entirely
scholarly-expository vocabulary with no liturgical shadow. Given that
Aldric is reading himself here as the scholar the church trained him to
be, some thinning is appropriate — but one word from the ecclesiastical
register in that passage ("I can confirm" → "I can *witness*," for
instance, or the debris field being described as scattered rather than
ranged) would ground Aldric in his distinct voice at that moment. As
written, the enumeration reads as precise narrator-exposition with
Aldric's name attached.

## Long clauses → stripped simple sentence pattern

**Strongly present throughout. Multiple correct deployments.**

Best instances:

- Opening watch: four long clauses about the hour's honesty →
  "He had not caught himself." Three words. The stripped sentence
  does not explain the tic's change; it reports it. The reader
  must do the work of understanding that not-catching means something
  new. Correct.

- Irongate gap interior: two long paragraphs of self-examination →
  "They had built the temple to hide the face." The sentence arrives
  after the long circling and the bible's "vault-stone settling"
  figure is used explicitly ("the way a vault-stone settled into a
  vault"). The landing is clean.

- Face comparison: the extended subordinate-clause account of his
  undercroft display ("not closer — the same — as if from a nearer
  position — and — ") → "Same object." Two words from Davan;
  Aldric's "Same object" is the echo. The stripped exchange enacts
  both characters' characteristic modes: Davan's half-thought confirmed,
  Aldric's formal clause stripped by the weight of the confirmation.

- Closing: the full inventory of lamp-tending history → "He leaned
  down and blew." The simplest physical action following the longest
  reflection. Correct.

**One overrun:** The scholars-in-different-libraries figure (ll. 400-410)
is long and well-architected but contains one redundancy. "The faces
had not been the same face. / They had never been going to be the same
face." The second sentence restates the first with a temporal qualifier.
One of the two should go; the first lands harder on its own.

## Self-aware-about-lying quality: appropriate attenuation in Ch. 25

**Assessment: Correctly handled. The quality is present in residue, not
in active performance.**

The bible specifies self-awareness-about-lying as Aldric's confirmed
signature. Ch. 25's position is three days east of New Plymouth, the
group formed, the undercroft disclosed the previous night. The lying
is not active.

The quality survives in two forms appropriate to the chapter's position:

1. **Self-awareness about vocabulary:** The "tired of keeping them in
   different rooms" passage (l. 371) is the lying-quality's structural
   heir — Aldric noting in real time what he has been doing with his
   own cognitive tools, and choosing to stop. This is the same mechanism
   as the lying-aware narration, applied to a different concealment.

2. **The withholding in the spring exchange** (ll. 155-161): Aldric
   receives Maren's disc-count, has no answer, does not pretend otherwise.
   "He did not yet know the question well enough to answer it." This is
   not lying; it is the absence of lying, noted precisely. The
   self-monitoring is present in the clean form — he is aware of his
   own limits and does not paper over them.

Both are appropriate for Ch. 25. Forcing the active lying-awareness into
a chapter where lying has dissolved would be a register error. The chapter
reads correctly.

## Non-negotiables: complete citation

### Filter words

**Count: 2 instances. Both weak-grade.**

1. **"he saw its consequence"** (l. 137):
   > "the disc under her coat pulsed — he could not see the pulse,
   > of course, but he saw its consequence: her free hand went briefly
   > inside the coat..."
   The parenthetical "he could not see the pulse, of course" partially
   rescues this by naming the perceptual limit — Aldric is marking his
   POV boundary, which is in-voice for a self-monitoring scholar. But
   "he saw its consequence" is still a filter wrapper on what can be
   directly stated: "her free hand went inside the coat — the disc's
   pulse, he could not see, but the hand's motion was its evidence."
   Low priority; the self-correction partially earns the form.

2. **"She saw him watching"** (l. 140):
   > "She saw him watching. She did not pretend she had not."
   Double filter: Maren seeing, Aldric watching. In Aldric's POV, "She
   saw him watching" is Aldric inferring Maren's perception of his gaze
   — technically valid POV inference. The phrase lands because it moves
   quickly ("She did not pretend she had not") and both sentences are
   short. Borderline acceptable. Low priority.

**"He was aware" — confirmed ABSENT.** The Ch. 7 and Ch. 21 signature
Aldric filter does not appear in Ch. 25. The improvement is real and
should be noted: the four prior Aldric chapters averaged 5 instances
of this specific construction; Ch. 25 has zero. This is the chapter's
most significant technical improvement over its predecessors.

**"He sat with the not-reaching"** (l. 520):
This is in the territory of the "sit with this" tic (flagged 7x in
the manuscript-wide audit) but is doing meaningfully different work.
"The not-reaching" is a named object — the thing being sat with is
specific and novel (a thing-not-done, not an abstraction). The
sentence enacts the lamp-tic's transformation by naming the
transformation's precise texture. This is an earned use of the
sitting-metaphor, not the workshop cliché. Keep.

## Lamp-tic rendering

**Two beats. Both land.**

**Beat 1 — opening unreached-wick:**
> "Aldric's hand had reached once, in the first quarter hour, for a
> wick that was not there. The ember at the lamp had wanted tending
> in the old way — pinched between thumb and forefinger, the small
> pastoral trim — and his fingers had come up off his knee and had
> gone the short distance and had closed on nothing. / He had not
> caught himself."

The transformation is in "He had not caught himself." The previous
Aldric chapters show him catching the tic; here the hand moves and
the catching mechanism is simply absent. The explanation comes
afterward in two careful sentences ("His fingers had opened before
they closed. Not because he had decided. Because there had been nothing
there to close on") — and the explanation is in Aldric's characteristic
mode: causal precision, no sentiment. The tic's change is rendered
through mechanics, not announced.

**Beat 2 — closing breath-extinguish:**
> "He leaned down and blew. / The flame went. The cold came in at
> the space where the flame had been. The hand he had not used to
> put it out stayed on his knee, open, palm up, the pads of the
> thumb and forefinger resting on air."

**The "pads of thumb and forefinger resting on air" — verdict: lands.**

The concern would be whether this over-announces the tic's transference.
It does not. "The hand he had not used" is the sentence's syntactic
center — not "the hand that used to reach" or "the hand that had
changed," but the hand defined by what it did not do. The fingers
"resting on air" is the geometrically accurate description of the hand
that did not pinch the wick: the exact position the old motion would
have produced, held without the object. The image is precise rather
than symbolic. The precision is in Aldric's register. The narrator
does not explain what it means.

The two beats are calibrated correctly: the opening beat shows the
mechanism of change (the hand finding nothing and returning without
being told); the closing beat shows the aftermath of that change
(the hand open, the position of the absent wick held in air). Neither
explains the other.

## Institutional-reframe beat

**Assessment: Realization, not decision. The landing is correct.**

The passage in question:

> "The temple had been put over the face to keep the face under a
> floor. The next Keeper, if he was going to be honest, was going to
> be the one who took the floor up. / He did not yet know how a man
> took up a floor he had sworn to walk on. The *how* was tomorrow's,
> or the week's, or the year's. The *that* was this hour's, and this
> hour had given it to him, and he accepted it."

The realization arrives in two stages, correctly sequenced:

Stage 1 — structural understanding: "They had not hidden the face in
the temple. / They had built the temple to hide the face." This is the
vault-stone landing — the sentence arrives slowly, is held, and the
narrator marks the holding without explaining it ("He let the sentence
arrive... He held the reins and let the bay take her pace and let the
sentence stand").

Stage 2 — personal implication: "The next Keeper... was going to be
the one who took the floor up." This is not announced as a decision.
It arrives through the grammar of Aldric's self-description: "if he
was going to be honest" — a conditional, not a declaration. The frame
is honesty, not vocation or heroism. The word *honest* is the chapter's
deepest liturgical-secular collision: the Keeper's thirty-year career
of careful institutional honesty confronted with what honesty actually
requires here.

"He accepted it" is the closest the passage comes to decision-language,
and it is passive — he does not choose; he receives. The sequence is
realization → acceptance, not decision → commitment. This is correct
for Ch. 25's position in Aldric's arc. Ch. 25 is not where he takes
the floor up. It is where he understands that he is the one who must.

**No narrator announcement of the reframe.** The institutional-reframe
arrives entirely through Aldric's interior in his own vocabulary,
without the narrator stepping in to gloss it.

## Debris-field enumeration + "Sixty miles" exchange

**Assessment: The enumeration sits in Aldric's scholarly register.
The dialogue lands in everyone's voice. One exposure risk.**

The enumeration (ll. 461-472):

> "At least four separate sites. That is not a guess. That is four
> confirmed. The field is the distance between the furthest two sites
> we know about. Confluence to the highlands. That is — he did the
> arithmetic — sixty miles. A little more."

"He did the arithmetic" is the slight slip toward expository narrator.
The arithmetic is Aldric's, but "he did the arithmetic" as a parenthetical
stage direction announces the action rather than enacting it. Suggested
revision: embed the calculation in the speech without the frame: "The
field is the distance between the furthest two we can confirm. Confluence
to the highlands. Call it sixty miles."

The items listed (Brennan's signatures, Reth's formation, the marsh,
Edvar's face in the collapsed building) are all in Aldric's voice:
attributed to proper names, referenced by specific scholarly sourcing.
This is the scholar's mode — nothing appears without a citation. The
list does not slide into expository narrator because every piece is
anchored to a person and a scene Aldric has been in.

**"How wide" / "Sixty miles" / "There will be others" exchange:**

All three speakers are in voice.

- Maren: "How wide" (two words, no inflection — she is not asking
  for comfort; she is asking for a number). "How many others." (no
  question mark in the text — she does not rise at the end; she
  states the opening of a question). Maren at her most compressed.
- Aldric: "Sixty miles" as a stand-alone sentence, then "The places
  I can confirm. There will be others." The uncertainty is stated
  precisely: not "I don't know how many," but "I don't know" — the
  short form because the long form would be dishonest about the

- Davan: silent in this exchange. Correctly absent — this is Maren
  and Aldric's exchange; Davan has already done his work with the
  face.

The chapter's note that Maren "understood when a question had reached
the edge of its answer and did not pretend by asking again that the
edge could be moved" is a POV-clean observation: Aldric inferring her
intelligence from her behavior. He does not enter her interior; he
reads her stopping. **Clean.**

## Closing image

**Assessment: Firmly in Aldric's register. Stands distinct from all
prior chapter closers.**

> "The flame went. The cold came in at the space where the flame had
> been. The hand he had not used to put it out stayed on his knee,

**In Aldric's register:** The sentence is physical and precise in the
way Aldric's closing sentences are physical and precise. He names the
thermal event ("the cold came in at the space where the flame had been")
with the accuracy of a man who has sat next to many extinguished lamps
and knows exactly what they do. The hand position is described
anatomically ("pads of the thumb and forefinger") — not emotionally,
not symbolically. The symbol is there for the reader; the narrator
does not attach it.

**Rhymes check:**

- Ch. 17 (Aldric): moons over the road, three where there used to be
  four. Ch. 25's closer does not look up; it looks at a knee.
- Ch. 19 ("It came down") — Davan; different POV, different mode.
- Ch. 20 ("The test had not yet begun") — Maren; different POV.
- Ch. 22 ("doing what they had always done") — Vane; different POV.

**Verdict: Stands distinct. The Ch. 24 sibling relationship is a
controlled echo that serves the book's structural rhyme, not an
accidental repetition.**

## Adversarial read: Ch. 25 Aldric vs. Ch. 24 Davan

**Verdict: Clear, unambiguous distinction. The chapters are immediately
distinguishable without tags.**

**Syntax:** Aldric's sentences are architected — nested subordinate
clauses, the scholar's long reach. "He had been thinking instead —
the way he had been trained to think at a lectern, long architected
clauses, the scholarly way — and he had been thinking it on top of
another thing the scholarly way did not quite have a handle on."
This meta-commentary on his own syntax IS Aldric's syntax. Davan's
Ch. 24 sentences circle and break. "Maybe it was —" ends without
a predicate. Aldric does not break sentences; he extends them.

**Diction:** Aldric: Lauds, pastoral, sanctuary, vault-stone, debris
field, vantage, institutional, undercroft, scaffolding. Davan: register,
carrying, carving, grain, hawthorn, bark-flake, circling. The diction
sets are entirely non-overlapping.

**Interiority mode:** Aldric reaches conclusions and sits with them
("He let the sentence arrive"). Davan circles and arrives at half-
conclusions ("He did not finish the thought"). Both use the
long-clause-to-short-sentence landing pattern, but Aldric's short
sentences are declarative and completed ("They had built the temple
to hide the face"), while Davan's are provisional and open-ended
("He had carved a buried thing").

**Lamp-tic:** Aldric has a physical signature that has no equivalent in
any other POV. The body acting below the mind's awareness — and then the
body *not* acting — is Aldric's unique contribution to the book's
physical vocabulary. Davan has skin-light. Maren has the coal-pouch
arm. Vane has bark-spreading. All four are immediately POV-distinctive.

**Verdict: No blur risk. The adversarial read passes with clear margin.**
