# Prose & Voice Audit V3 (Vee) — Ch 21-30

---

## 1. POV Voice Drift

### Maren
Maren's voice has sharpened considerably since what earlier passes apparently flagged. The compressed watchfulness is consistent and earns its keep. Her cataloguing habit lands as character rather than tic. One residual concern:

**Issue: Slight register bleed in the father's-coat scene (Ch. 27)**
> "She did not say anything. She did not weep. The weeping was a country she could not afford today."

The phrase "the weeping was a country she could not afford today" is genuinely good — controlled, metaphorically disciplined. But the following passage accumulates a triple negative construction and then a summary of her own emotional state that tips into the narrator explaining Maren to us:

> "She took a breath and did not take another, and then she took another anyway because the body did what the body did, and she sat back on her heels in the grass beside what was left of her father and she put her hand inside her coat and she held the disc against her palm and let it hum. She had been carrying grief. The cooperage had taught her she was carrying a test. The hill taught her what she had not had the words for."

The last three sentences are thesis-sentences rather than experience-sentences. Maren does not think in theses. "She had been carrying grief. The cooperage had taught her..." reads like the narrator stepping in to close a loop for the reader. The register is Maren-adjacent but belongs to someone tidying up — which is the one moment in thirty pages where the prose retreats from full trust of its material. **Severity: Medium.**

### Davan — Ch. 28 expanded skin-light register, Ch. 30 callback
The skin-light work in Ch. 28 delivers. The two approximations — "the color of honey held up to a lamp, only cooler" and "the color of the edge of dawn a morning nobody had seen, a morning the country was having for the first time" — arrive as half-thoughts he doesn't force to conclusion, which is exactly the voice signature. The register is right. The Ch. 30 callback ("the color of the edge of dawn a morning nobody had seen, a morning the country was having for the first time") lands clean.

**Issue: One moment of over-conclusion in Ch. 28**
> "His hands had known her shape before he'd met her. The room had taught him why: hands kept things ahead of the rest of you, and asked nothing."

This is the one place in Ch. 28 where Davan lands a clean thesis. The problem is that "the room had taught him why" is an authorial convenience — a pinning-down of meaning that Davan himself would leave as a loose thread. By the Story Bible's own voice signature, Davan processes by circling rather than concluding. "It came down" (Ch. 19) works because it's four syllables that earn their weight. This is a twelve-word explanation. The sentence before it ("hands kept things ahead of the rest of you, and asked nothing") is the part Davan would have. The frame ("the room had taught him why") is the narrator offering the frame Davan wouldn't offer. **Severity: Low-medium.**

**Ch. 30 Davan register — no flag.** The internal monologue at the center-of-floor moment ("I am not going to ask you to be opened") reads correctly as Davan saying the thing he needed to say to the room before leaving, without any summary-sentence author intervention. That section is the passage's best Davan writing.

### Aldric — Ch. 29, the chapter where his voice should be at its strongest
This is the genuinely strong read. The dual-register conflict — institutional vocabulary with liturgical eruptions — is audible throughout and specifically in the exact moments the Story Bible prescribes. The lamp-tic fading to the ghost of a tic landing cleanly. The manifest reading, the voiced-wall voice, the cellular biology passage: all maintain the scholar-priest tension.

**Issue: Three-bullet internal manifest (Ch. 29 close)**
Near the end of Ch. 29, after Aldric has left the storage, we get the passage at the angle:

> *The records are not a find. The records are a floor. The floor is under my life's work. The life's work does not continue the way it was going.*
> *The floor does not belong to me...*
> *The channel is not the Temple's...*

This is three numbered realizations in italics, at full development, delivered as internal summary. Earlier V1/V2 passes apparently flagged an Aldric manifesto. The disruption appears to have converted the bullets into flowing paragraphs with ellipses, but the underlying structure is still a three-point thematic declaration. Three separate *this-is-what-I-now-know* statements in a row, each with an italicized hook sentence, each followed by elaboration. The italics make the structure visible.

What makes this a drift problem: Aldric's best earlier moments are marked by the realization arriving slowly and then the prose dwelling on it rather than the prose listing it. "They had not hidden the face in the temple. They had built the temple to hide the face." That works because it arrives once, as a two-sentence chiasm, and the next paragraph is silence on horseback. The three-bullet structure in Ch. 29's angle passage tells us what Aldric has decided rather than enacting the deciding. **Severity: Medium.**

**Issue: "tenderness" in the biology reading section**
> "What arrived in its place he could only name as *tenderness*."

The word is italicized, which means the prose is flagging it as important. Italicized emotional naming from inside a character's POV is the prose taking credit for the feeling rather than producing the feeling. Aldric has been established to distrust emotional vocabulary — he reaches for liturgical words and scholarly words before feeling-words. A man of his training, sitting on the floor of an alien archive, would not name his reaction in a single italicized word. He would circle it, or name it wrong, or note that the anger he expected was not there and leave the absence as the datum. This sentence does the opposite of his voice signature. **Severity: Low-medium.**

### Vane — Ch. 26 instrument inventory, withdrawal arc
The withdrawal is rendered with admirable restraint. The cataloguing-as-emotional-tell lands exactly right. The "nose, working. *Mine.*" ending of the inventory is the kind of understated beat that earns its blankness.

**Issue: "entry sat in its slot crooked" — minor idiom slippage**
> "She filed it. Not the way she had filed his warmth a week ago. Differently. The entry sat in its slot crooked."

"Crooked" is the right register — almost. The trouble is that Vane's inventory metaphor is always about what the ledger can and cannot book, never about physical filing error. An entry sat in its slot "crooked" is a spatial image; Vane's ledger is a cost-yield register, not a physical filing cabinet. The image briefly shifts her metaphor system from accounting to physical filing. A minor slip. **Severity: Low.**

**No flag on Ch. 30 Vane.** The action at the hill is the best Vane prose in thirty pages: the countdown spacing, the Threshing-Yard register, the cord over the wrist before the sword draws. "Not today" as a complete position statement. The closing image — "you stood angles for languages that were not yours" — is the exact note her arc needs to land on: not conversion, not catharsis. Economy meeting a new shape.

### Ch. 30 Multi-POV Register
The four-section structure echoes the prologue's multi-account architecture as the Story Bible prescribes. Each voice lands distinctly:
- Vane's section is action/inventory;
- Davan's section is proximity/empathy;
- Aldric's section is the three-bullet issue above plus the institutional pivot;
- Maren's section is the book's emotional terminus.

One register concern:

**Issue: Maren Ch. 30 moon-passage — slight elevation above her register**
> "She looked in the way he had taught her before she had had the words to know what looking was, and she let what was in the sky be in the sky, and what was in her be in her, and for a moment — the moment was short and the moment was also a moment she would carry for the rest of her life and would not, afterward, describe to anyone, not even to Davan — the two of them were at a register that did not have a name."

The phrase "the moment was also a moment she would carry for the rest of her life" is a proleptic narrator intrusion — telling us what happens to Maren after the book ends, in the moment of the book's close. "She would not, afterward, describe to anyone, not even to Davan" is the narrator speaking from outside the text's time. Maren's POV is present-tense compressed. She does not make predictions about herself. This is the narrator providing the reader with a guarantee that the moment is significant. The moment is already significant; the guarantee is redundant and breaks the register. **Severity: Medium.**

## 2. Narrator Leak / Authorial Intrusion

The climax is largely clean. The specific vulnerability the brief anticipated — author-explanation at the moment of revelation — is mostly held. The disc-seating scene in Ch. 27 trusts sensation over summary. The hill-opening in Ch. 28 trusts Davan's proximity-sense over narration.

**Issue: "It was not a hill" — three iterations**

> "It was the wrong shape for a hill."
> "It was not a hill."
> ...
> "The symbol was not on the hill. The symbol *was* the hill."

The first two instances of this insight arriving are effective escalation. The third — "The symbol *was* the hill" — lands correctly as reframe. But there is also a fourth beat before all of these:

> "She had known this from Reth's report, carried to her through Aldric on the third evening east."

This sentence arrives *after* the sensory revelation and before the emotional landing, and it is the narrator tidying continuity — reminding the reader why Maren knows what she knows. Maren's POV does not require this reminder. The sentence is scaffolding left in the manuscript. **Severity: Low-medium.**

**Issue: "the sentence became what it had not been in the basement: *architecture*"**

In Ch. 24, during Aldric's outdoor reading:
> "It did different work now. On a road east, under a lamp, over two faces laid on a blanket, the sentence became what it had not been in the basement: *architecture.*"

The italicized word is the narrator labeling the insight rather than the insight arriving. Davan's POV, not Aldric's — but this is in Davan's chapter, and Davan does not italicize his recognitions. He circles them and lets them settle. The italics are the prose pointing at itself. **Severity: Low.**

## 3. Sensory Filter Verbs

The manuscript has worked through most of the "she heard / she saw / she felt" filter-verb issue. The sensory content in Ch. 23-27 especially tends to arrive as flat declarative without the filter. However:

**Issue: Mild clustering in Ch. 21 cooperative scene**
> "She was aware, with the stripped clarity that had been following him since Greenford..."
> "He did not have, and needed, the people."

These are Aldric-POV, and Aldric's scholarly voice can absorb some filter verbs — "he was aware" fits a man who registers things with the precision of a Keeper. Not a flag so much as a note: Ch. 21 is the chapter most at risk for filter-verb accumulation because it is the most expository, and it does collect several "he had come to recognize" and "he understood" constructions in sequence. They don't rise to a formal problem, but the density is slightly higher than the rest of the section. **Severity: Low.**

## 4. Telling Where Showing Was Available

**Issue: Maren-on-Aldric summary, Ch. 23**
> "She had learned in the cooperage basement that Aldric's silences were working silences, and the working was not hers to interrupt. She walked."

The first sentence is a recap of a characterization the reader has already absorbed. The second sentence is the beat that matters. The first could be cut without loss. **Severity: Low.**

**Issue: Vane on herself, Ch. 26**
> "She had used to be able to pick up this much from a smell across a room. Tonight she was working from what was being said. The tool she had been for ten years, the instrument she had sharpened and overclocked with the Bloom, was going."

"Overclocked" is the right register and the right word. "The instrument she had sharpened... was going" is effective. But the sentence that follows undercuts it:

> *Not going,* she corrected. *Retrograding. Back to what it was before. My nose. Which still works. Just not for what I've been asking it to do.*

And then the prose tags the correction with:

> "The correction was a kindness she allowed herself. She did not fully believe it. It would become truer over weeks. She would have to wait."

"She did not fully believe it. It would become truer over weeks. She would have to wait." is the narrator telling us Vane is wrong about the correction in a way that reassures us that things will improve. Vane's ledger does not allow for reassurance. "She would have to wait" is a comfort note and it is out of register. **Severity: Low-medium.**

## 5. Repetition & Echo Phrases

**Issue: "the way a man/woman did X" construction — overused**

Across Ch. 21-30 this construction appears at high frequency:
- "the way Reth had laid his hands on the table forty years after filing his report"
- "the way a man who had made a professional practice of not probing what was offered"
- "the way the lamplight reached it and stopping there as though it had crossed a boundary"
- "the way a stone set in a stream"

The construction itself is not wrong — it is a legitimate means of grounding abstract perception in physical parallel. But it is the manuscript's most used comparator construction, and by Ch. 28-29 it has accumulated enough instances that the reader begins to feel the template rather than the individual comparison. In Ch. 29 specifically, within six pages:
> "the way a scribe marked, in the margin of a chronicle, the year"
> "the way an afterimage held in an eye"
> "the way a person of long patient inquiry arranged the body"

None of these are weak individually. Together they produce a sameness of rhythm. **Severity: Low-medium.**

**Issue: "had done" + "did not" paired construction — minor**

This is a micro-pattern rather than a problem, but it appears with enough regularity to note: "He had not caught himself. His fingers had opened before they closed. Not because he had decided." The alternation of positive-past and negated-present appears as a rhythmic tic in Aldric's sections specifically. Fourteen times in Ch. 25-29 in close proximity. A reader attuned to sentence rhythm will notice the pattern. **Severity: Low.**

## 6. Cliché, Adverb Cruft, Beat-Sheet Sentences

**Issue: "the way a vault-stone settles into a vault"**

> "He let the sentence arrive. It arrived slowly, with a settling, the way a vault-stone settled into a vault."

"The way a vault-stone settled into a vault" is a dead metaphor with a slight tautological edge — a vault-stone settling into a vault is just a stone doing what stones do. It is correct but not alive. The preceding sentence ("he held the reins and let the bay take her pace and let the sentence stand") is stronger because it is specific. The vault-stone simile is the kind of elevated comparison a careful author reaches for when the plain statement would do more work. **Severity: Low.**

**Issue: "with the economy she seemed to bring to every gesture" — authorial tag**
> "Maren lifted the disc off the table with the same quiet economy with which she had set it down three days ago..."

The phrase "quiet economy" in a different phrasing appears later:
> "with the economy she seemed to bring to every gesture, only the look of one person acknowledging another..."

This is the narrator labeling Maren's characteristic rather than producing it through action. "She seemed to bring" especially — "seemed" is the narrator hedging its own observation. **Severity: Low.**

## 7. Dialogue Ring

**No significant issue found.** The dialogue across Ch. 21-30 is notably disciplined. The cooperage scene in Ch. 21, which is the chapter most at risk for expository dialogue, handles the around-the-table sequence through compressed report ("Four years on the astronomical question") rather than expanded speech. The cross-dialogue between Davan and Aldric in Ch. 24-25 is the strongest in the section — the "same object" exchange, the "pieces" exchange — both feel earned and not pre-arranged.

One minor flag:

**Issue: Edvar at the outcrop — too clean a setup line**
> "And Edvar knows someone," Marrick said. [Ch. 21 variant, cooperage scene]

The cooperage scene's structured round-the-table introductions produce a few exchanges that feel like dialogue written for the reader's benefit. Particularly the moment where Marrick's introduction of Edvar implies "and here is the person who has the relevant piece of information." Marrick functions here as a structural prop. Not a large problem, and likely unavoidable in an ensemble-assembly scene. **Severity: Low.**

## 8. Climax / Resolution Tonal Risk

The climax is admirably restrained. The captain's voice, when it arrives in Ch. 30 Maren, earns its content through the long preparation of every voice-record note in Ch. 29. The bone-sound / saying-and-answer mechanism is executed cleanly.

**Issue: The captain's message is slightly overlong for the register it is meant to occupy**

> *"We failed our descent. The lander is down in the eastern foothills. Most of us did not reach the ground. Those of us who did are not, I think, going to remain who we are for very long. The world is in us. There is no way out of it."*
> *"We have moved what could be moved from the holds to the secondary cache at the river-meeting site. The archive goes with it..."*
> *"To the three still on station: we did what we could..."*
> *"This is the last of the log I am going to record. There is a morning outside..."*

The Story Bible specifies this as "a tired man getting up from a desk. Restraint. Not prophecy." The prose version largely achieves this. But four distinct paragraphs, each with full sentences and a complete emotional note, accumulate beyond what a man who has sat down at the end of a day would say before "getting up." The third paragraph — *"to the three still on station: we did what we could. You are not coming for us. I am sorry. Hold your post..."* — is the strongest, because it is the most personal and least explanatory. The first and fourth paragraphs bracket the necessary content; the second (cargo-logistics) interrupts the emotional through-line for a plot-continuity note that the *reader* needs but the captain would not have foregrounded. A minor structural concern in an otherwise properly calibrated sequence. **Severity: Low.**

**Issue: "a bell rung at a distance of ten thousand seasons, and the bell had rung as a bell rings when the hand that strikes it is, just before striking, alive"**

This sentence, from Ch. 30 Maren's reception of the captain's voice, is the one place in the denouement where the prose reaches for an elaborate formal simile at the moment of highest emotional intensity. The image is technically accomplished but it is also the moment the reader would be most likely to feel the author's hand. "A bell rung at a distance of ten thousand seasons" is beautiful. "The bell had rung as a bell rings when the hand that strikes it is, just before striking, alive" is the elaboration one beat past where the image does its best work. **Severity: Low.**

## 9. Paragraphs That Earn Their Page Space

**First — Vane's instrument inventory, Ch. 26:**

> *Left hand, shaking, usable. Right hand, shaking, usable. Legs, tired, usable. Eyes, tired, good at distance. Ears, off, coming back in a week. Nose, working. Arm, losing bark at the edges. Knife, in pack. Rope, in pack. Lock-tools, in pack. The man at three-quarters of a mile, catalogued. The party, here, sleeping. The disc in the dark-haired woman's coat, across the fold.*
>
> She got to the end of the list.
>
> She started again.
>
> She stopped at *nose, working* and did not go further.
>
> *Nose, working,* she thought. And then, because the ledger was broken in places tonight but was not broken here: *mine.*

This is the section's single best-executed passage for character voice. The inventory is Vane's form; the "mine" at the end is the emotional content she would never approach directly. The form doing the feeling's work, which is exactly what the Story Bible's "interiority presented as professional analysis even when it is feeling" means in practice. It costs nothing to the reader except the attention it demands, and it earns the cost.

**Second — the disc seating and its aftermath, Ch. 27:**

> The contact came before the seating. A moment where the material of the disc met the material of the hill — not a click, not a sound at all, but a joining, a thing-become-one-thing under her thumb — and then the seating, a quarter-inch of settle, the disc sinking the rest of the way into the recess and stopping at a depth that was the correct depth.

followed immediately by:

> Her thumb was on the symbol on the back of the disc. She pressed.
>
> She had not been told to press. The pressing was what the hand knew.

The restraint of "the pressing was what the hand knew" is the right amount of information. The long build makes this payoff structurally sound. The prose here does not explain the moment; it moves through the moment at the pace the moment requires. "She had not been told to press" is the sentence that earns everything before it — the three-year preparation, the pulsing disc, the cooperage, the cooperage, all of it, arriving at an action so instinctive the character did not choose it.

**Third — Davan's carved hawthorn piece, Ch. 24:**

> He had carved a buried thing.
>
> He had carved it without deciding to, and he had carved it with the note under the country working in his wrists all day. The piece of hawthorn was not the hill east of New Plymouth — the hill was five days and he was on day two — but the shape of what his body had been reading along the road was in his hand now, small, four-fingers long, the wood a pale hawthorn-yellow in the lamp-edge light.

...and then the passage ending:

> Davan closed his hand around the carved piece. The hawthorn was cold on one side where the air had reached it and warm on the other side where his palm had been. He kept his palm against the cold side until the cold side was not cold any more, and he held it there past the warming — past the point where holding did anything — because the country under the camp was cold in a way that a palm could not warm, and the hawthorn in his hand was a small thing, and the smallness of it was what he had to give.

This is the best Davan passage in the section. The logic of giving warmth to a cold thing past the warming, because the country under everything is cold in a way no warmth reaches — this is the novel's central register (human smallness against geological and historical scale) rendered in a gesture. Earned through the full chapter's sensory preparation, landing without any summary sentence.

## 10. Verdict

Compared to what earlier passes would have found in the rough versions of this material, the V3 manuscript has notably converged. The main structural vulnerabilities — the Aldric manifesto bullet-structure, a handful of narrator intrusions at the climax, and one sequence of summary-thesis in Maren's coat scene — are localized and repairable. The withdrawal arc, the disc-seating, the cargo room, and the captain's voice are all well-executed. The four-POV Ch. 30 architecture holds. The prose's single dominant overuse is the "the way a X did Y" comparator construction, which has accumulated density across thirty pages but is not a systematic flaw. This is a manuscript in diminishing-returns territory: the remaining issues are real, but they are not the issues of a draft that needs rethinking. They are the issues of a draft that needs a final light pass.

---

*Top voice-drift offender:* Aldric's three-bullet thematic declaration at the Ch. 29 passage angle, where italicized internal summary replaces the enacted recognition that characterizes his best pages.

*Top narrator-intrusion moment:* Maren Ch. 30 moon passage — "the moment was also a moment she would carry for the rest of her life and would not, afterward, describe to anyone" — the narrator speaking from outside the book's time to guarantee the moment's significance.

*Top working passage:* Vane's instrument inventory ending at "nose, working. *Mine.*" — the form of the ledger doing the work of the feeling without naming it.
