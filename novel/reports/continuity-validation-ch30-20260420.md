## Verdict

Chapter 30 is substantially coherent with the bible and preceding chapters.
Voice, structure, four archive positions, captain's message, Horst accounting,
agent resolution, and the closing quality all hold. One confirmed continuity
contradiction (the moons-look), one flag requiring author judgment (Maren's
position phrasing), and no new canon that conflicts with prior bible.

## 1. Opening Transition from Ch. 29

PASS. Ch. 29 closes with Aldric at the angle after Vane calls "Come to the
angle," boot-scrape on scree above the cart, agent approaching. Ch. 30 opens
with Vane flat in the north-shoulder lee, counting the agent's paces down from
the crest. The transition is continuous and correctly overlapping — same moment,
different vantage. Edvar confirmed flat in the southern lee, off-horse at the
cheek-strap. Aldric emerges from the passage at the angle. Maren still at the
recess, thumb on disc, back to the slope. All four positions match Ch. 29 canon.

## 2. The Four Archive Positions

### Vane — "Leave as found"

PASS, and tonally precise. The chapter does not state Vane's position in her
own POV section; it is rendered through Maren's interior in the disagreement
passage. Maren reads Vane's position as: "The question is not mine. It is not
yours. It is the next generation's, if there is one. We leave the room as we
found it." This is exactly consistent with Vane's Ch. 26 stripped-down
cost-benefit register and Ch. 18 refusal-not-conquest frame. The position is
presented as a reckoning with generational scale, not indifference — the cost-
yield ledger applied to a question bigger than any single generation. Authentic.

### Aldric — "Build the table"

PASS. Aldric's section renders this as three sequential statements: records are
a floor under his life's work; the floor belongs to every living person on Gaius,
not to him; the channel is not the Temple's and must be built by the cooperage
group, Brennan, Reth, others. This is a direct, almost word-for-word echo of
his Ch. 25 "next Keeper takes the floor up" realization and his Ch. 29 "the
channel would have to be elsewhere" sentence. The position also honors the
lamp-tic arc: the hand that "did not move toward any cord" in his section is a
clean payoff. His internal framing ("I will carry documents... I will, for a
while, be a man who has lost his frock") is consistent with the institutional-
shift-realization arc established in Ch. 25. Authentic.

### Davan — "Never open / I am the Chrysalis now"

PASS. Davan's section is the most interior and empathic of the four, and it
earns its position through his palm-on-object reading rather than through
argument. The "seed pressing gently outward" passage is consistent with Ch. 28's
storage-as-holding register. His stated position — "I am not going to ask anyone
to be opened... let them rest. Let the Chrysalis be what the Chrysalis is. I am
the Chrysalis now" — is fully grounded in his empathic/bodily mode and the new
wrist-light color settled from Ch. 28. The position honors the bio-lock
specificity: he understands what is being held is living material kin to his
mutation; his restraint is the attention owed to kin. Authentic.

### Maren — "Open eventually, for company"

PASS WITH ONE FLAG. The position itself is earned: the captain's message
delivers it directly ("the children will be a new people... a people worth the
coming"), and Maren's specific stake — the only kin she has left is in the
archive — is logically consistent with her arc from hollow-reframe through
father's-death to disc-carrier-not-keepsake-carrier. The "for company" framing
is a genuine surprise because it is personal rather than institutional or
ethical, which makes it distinctively Maren's.

The flag: in rendering her position, the chapter does not clearly anchor that
"open eventually" means "at some future generation after the bio-lock problem
is solved," rather than "open now or soon." The bible's bio-lock is explicit:
no living person on Gaius qualifies. Maren's position as written says "not
soon, not in a panic, not for the bloodline or the Temple" but does not specify
the multi-generational biological project as the mechanism. The four-wants
paragraph ("the four wants were in her at once") recovers this somewhat —
Maren explicitly holds all four positions simultaneously, including Davan's
"let them rest" — but the position-as-stated reads closer to "open eventually
for a good enough reason" than "open by making a line of people who can." The
bio-lock should sit more explicitly under her position for the genetic-temporal
framing to land. This is a flag for revision, not a contradiction.

## 3. Captain's Recorded Message

### Form

PASS. The record arrives via the "saying-and-answer at a distance" (Davan's
naming from Ch. 28) reaching "close" and bringing the matching half of the
record to overlay the voiced wall's voice. This is coherent with Ch. 28's
orbital-resonance register as "a pressure at the back of the inner ear" / "a
saying-and-answer at a distance the word distance was not built for." The
mechanism — orbital response completing a paired record — is new canon but
consistent with the orbital-resonance register already established. The chapter
carefully does not name it as orbital contact; readers infer. Aldric in Ch. 29
hears the voiced wall in the ship's language without understanding it; Ch. 30's
"pairing" explains why Maren can now receive the sense while the four vowels
and consonants remain. The pairing metaphor (transparent page laid over a page
it was drawn to fit) is clean and does not overclaim.

### Content

PASS WITH ONE NOTE. The captain's message covers:
- Failed descent, lander down in eastern foothills
- Those who descended will not remain who they are (Chrysalis contact)
- Cargo moved to secondary cache at river-meeting site (Confluence site)
- Archive does not open with current hands; meant for whoever comes after
- Release of orbital crews: "Hold your post as long as the post will hold you.
  When it does not hold you any longer, go home."
- Morning-in-the-hull close: a hillside moving in the light, going to sit in it

The move-cargo-to-secondary-cache line is new; it retroactively explains the
Confluence debris scatter and the containers' presence under the temple. This
is tight new canon that consolidates what Ch. 29's manifest reading implied.
No contradiction with prior bible.

The "go home" instruction is the one the drafter flagged. It deepens, not
contradicts, the three-orbital-ships canon. Bible Section 3 states the three
ships are "crewed by long-dead descendants running mission protocols" and are
"performing protocol" (Ch. 29, Aldric). The captain's message does not order
them to stay; it permits them to leave when the post no longer holds them.
They stayed anyway. This reframes the three moons from "ships locked in
orbit by protocol" to "ships crewed by people who were given permission to
leave and chose to remain." That is a meaningful thematic deepening of the
Option C cosmology: stewardship by choice, not protocol alone. No contradiction.

One small note: the captain's message says "we have moved what could be moved
from the holds to the secondary cache." Bible canon (Section 7, Author Canon)
confirms 1 message container + 3 genetic archive containers in Confluence
sealed chamber. Ch. 30 does not explicitly identify which containers were moved;
the implication is all four went to the Confluence site (which the bible
confirms). This is not a contradiction, just an ellipsis that the archive-
document Aldric read in Ch. 29 (heading: "For the colony, archive, colonial-
genetic, Mission Office, Hold IV") already resolved.

this explicitly), which is consistent with her Ch. 26 instrumental register.

## 5. Bio-Lock Specificity in the Disagreement

PASS. None of the four positions assumes the archive can be opened by anyone
alive. Vane's position is purely deferral. Aldric's is process-building with
no timeline for opening. Davan's is explicit restraint ("let them rest"). Only
Maren's "open eventually" could be read as skating past the bio-lock; see flag
in Section 2 above. The disagreement is correctly framed as timeline / process
/ ethics, not as "who gets to open it now." The bio-lock floor holds.

## 7. Maren's First Moons-Look

CONTRADICTION — REQUIRES AUTHOR DECISION.

Ch. 30 states: "She had not looked at the moons in three years." The moons-look
passage explicitly frames Ch. 30 as the first time she has looked up in three
years: "Her eye gave her what her eye had not given her in three years." The
closing summary confirms: "She had not looked in three years. She had looked
twice in three minutes."

However, Ch. 23 contains a moons-look (lines 549-594 in that draft) in which
Maren looks at the three moons before sleeping on Day 1 east. The Ch. 23 text
explicitly acknowledges the three-year avoidance: "She had been avoiding the
moons for three years, since the first night on the road — eyes low, hood up,
attention on the ground." The look in Ch. 23 is qualified by "whether tonight
she was breaking it or only putting it down for a minute to rest her neck she
[the sentence cuts off in the draft]," and the text shows her turning away: "She
turned her face from the moons."

The bible's Section 7 Draft-Level Revision Directive on this point reads: "Ch.
23 ends with Maren looking up at the three moons for the first time in three
years — a soft, exhausted, pre-sleep gesture immediately undercut by a disc
pulse. Not a revelation. The continuity pass deemed this safe but conditional:
do NOT promote this beat in Chs. 24-26 into 'the moment she started to
understand.' Hold the look unresolved through at least Ch. 26."

The directive's intent was to protect Ch. 30's look as the structurally
significant one. Ch. 30's look does retain structural priority — it is full-
bright, three moons together, post-captain's-message, with the Tidemark-quality
grief arriving. But Ch. 30's narration claims it as the first look in three
years, which is technically false given Ch. 23.

Resolution options for author:
(a) Revise Ch. 30 to acknowledge the Ch. 23 look: "She had looked once, on the
    road east, briefly, before the disc pulled her attention back. That had been
    looking away. This was looking." This preserves both beats and gives Ch. 30
    the weight of the first *full* look.
(b) Revise Ch. 23 to remove or further qualify the moons-look so it reads as
    non-looking (eyes drift up but don't register, undercut immediately by the
    pulse before she actually sees them).
(c) Leave both drafts as-is and accept the slight inconsistency on the logic
    that Ch. 23's look was suppressed/disavowed by Maren's interior and Ch. 30's
    narration is working from her subjective experience of avoidance ("the
    version of herself that had looked at moons" remained at a distance).

Option (a) is the most conservative and requires the smallest Ch. 30 revision.
Option (b) requires a Ch. 23 revision. Option (c) is editorially defensible
given the subjective framing but a reader will notice the inconsistency.

## 8. The "Old Patient Grief" Closing Quality

PASS. The Tidemark harbor-master's passage (what_the_moons_keep.md, section IV):

> "...that old patient grief that doesn't belong to you personally but is yours
> anyway because it's been handed down so many times that it's worn itself into
> the shape of a thing you were born knowing."

Ch. 30's closing passage:
> "The register was the register of the Tidemark harbor-master, whose words she
> had read at eleven without remembering afterward that she had read them. The
> words had sat in her. She knew them as the condition at her sternum when she
> looked up at three full-bright moons over an eastern ridge with her thumb on a
> disc that had been her father's, knowing herself to be carrying a thing worn
> into the shape of a thing you were born knowing."
>
> "Grief that did not belong to her personally. Grief that was hers anyway."

The echo is direct but not a quotation — the closing distillation strips the
Tidemark phrase to its thesis and lands it as Maren's embodied condition rather
than a remembered text. The "worn into the shape of a thing you were born
knowing" is a deliberate half-quotation that connects the harbor-master's
spoken framing to Maren's body-state. The chapter does not name the harbor-
master's words as a literary reference Maren consciously recalls; it renders
them as a condition she discovers she is already in. This is the correct
execution: thesis as feeling, not quotation.

The closing sentence — "She kept walking" — comes after the moons-look and the
grief passage. The three moons are the closing image; "she kept walking" is the
motion past the image. Bible Section 7 specifies "closing image = three moons
full-bright + Maren's first look." The chapter's structure gives the moons-look
full treatment (two separate passes, the grief passage, the memory of the
father's shoulder) before the walk. The final motion is away from the image
into the world, which is consistent with the novel's cut-at-commitment-not-
resolution pattern.

## 10. Maren's Disc Position

The disc stays seated in the recess throughout Ch. 30. Maren lifts her thumb
when she decides to stand (the disc stays seated; "the disc stayed seated. The
symbol stayed lit. The bone-sound did not stop."). She stands and walks up the
slope. The chapter does not address whether she removes the disc before leaving.
This is an ellipsis, not a contradiction. Given that the hill's saying-and-
answer is described as continuing without her thumb, the disc appears to remain
at the recess. Author should confirm whether this is intentional — if so, the
disc-at-the-recess is itself a closing image (the key left in the lock) and
worth one sentence of confirmation in the Maren section.

## 11. Prologue Echo (Structural)

PASS. The four-POV structure echoes the multi-vantage prologue (8 First Coming
accounts). The echo is clean: same event (the captain's voice), rendered
through four distinct registers (Vane's economy, Davan's empathic read, Aldric's
institutional-reckoning, Maren's personal grief). Each POV hears the same voice
and is "the only person in the world who had heard it, because each of them had
heard it in the register their own body had made to catch it." This is a direct
structural mirror of the prologue's thesis that the same event looks different
from each vantage and none of the accounts is completely right. No contradiction
with prologue's specific claims (the 8 accounts are about the First Coming
crash, not about this moment).

## 12. "She Kept Walking" Closing Sentence

PASS. Bible Section 7 closing image canon: "three moons full-bright + Maren's
first look." The moons-look passage is the emotional and structural climax of
Maren's section. "She kept walking" is the sentence after. The novel's
structural pattern (noted in Section 8: "Chapter endings tend to cut at the
moment of commitment or incompletion — not resolution") is honored: the final
cut is not at the moons but at the motion forward. The moons are the image; the
walk is the commitment that follows it. Consistent.

## 13. New Canon Worth Folding into the Bible

The following new material in Ch. 30 should be added to the bible if the
chapter is approved:

1. **Orbital-pair mechanism for record playback.** The saying-and-answer (orbital
   resonance) arriving at "close" completes the voiced record by bringing its
   matching half — a "transparent page laid over a page it was drawn to fit."
   Not translation: pairing. One ear hears the vowels; another hears the sense.
   This is the first in-world explanation for how the voiced record becomes
   comprehensible.

2. **Captain's message content (canonical).** Failed descent; cargo moved to
   river-meeting site (Confluence); archive sealed for unchanged hands; three
   orbital crews released from protocol obligation ("go home when the post no
   longer holds you"); morning-in-the-hull closing (goes to sit and look at "the
   hillside that moves in the light — it is our own hull"). Mission name stated
   as four-letter unit with inner-orbit figure as first letter.

3. **Three ships stayed by choice.** The captain's message permits orbital crews
   to leave. The three moons are on station by choice, not only protocol. This
   deepens Option C cosmology: stewardship, not mere protocol execution.

4. **Horst team distribution (Ch. 30-confirmed).** Valley road (1), NP with
   Horst (2), Stillwater different matter (1), captured at hill (1) = 5 total.
   The Stillwater agent fills the previously-unspecified "1-2 reserve" slot.

5. **Agent-release with falsified report.** Captured agent walks back to New
   Plymouth under threat of future consequence; reports "signature did not
   register." This is the group's security resolution at the hill and should be
   noted as Ch. 30 canon for any future chapters involving Horst's operation.

6. **Disc stays seated after Maren lifts thumb.** The continuing saying-and-
   answer does not require her thumb. Whether this means the disc remains in
   the recess permanently or until she returns to retrieve it is currently an
   ellipsis in Ch. 30 — flag for author confirmation.
