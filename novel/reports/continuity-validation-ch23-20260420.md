## Contradictions (must-fix)

**1. Tela is a decoy rider — inconsistent with group composition canon.**
Ch. 23 (p.3 of draft) states: "Two riders had gone through the east gate
two hours earlier under papers Marrick had drawn up — decoys, Tela among
them, Maren understood without having asked."
Ch. 22 established the traveling group as Maren, Vane, Davan, Aldric,
Edvar (with Edvar's cart and the iron-banded trunk). Ch. 22 (Davan's
dusk visit, p.10): "He says Tela will come too" — explicitly enrolling
Tela as a fifth traveler, not a decoy.
The decoy role contradicts Davan's confirmed line. Either Davan's
statement must be retconned (Ch. 22 edit) or Tela must not be the decoy
rider. She cannot both "come too" in Ch. 22 and depart as a deliberate
non-traveler in Ch. 23 without an intervening scene or explanation.
**Must be reconciled before Ch. 23 can be considered clean.**

**2. Edvar riding without Tela — contradicts Ch. 22's "Tela will come
too."**
This is the same root contradiction as above, rendered in action: Edvar
is shown on the bench alone ("Tela was not with him") with no
acknowledgment of the change. If the decoy-rider solution is kept, Ch.
22's Davan line must be revised. If Tela is a full traveler, the decoy
beats must be rewritten.

## Drift / soft inconsistencies

**A. Disc pulse count is internally inconsistent within Ch. 23.**
The chapter tracks pulses as follows:
- Pre-bell, in the room (implied by "first was in the dark before the
  bell") — pulse 1.
- Afternoon on the heath, under her knuckle — pulse 2 (stated: "two
  pulses today… this is the second").
- Pre-sleep, moon-look moment — pulse 3 (stated: "three pulses today").
- Final pre-sleep beat: "four pulses" (stated: "she had counted the
  fourth without meaning to").
The chapter's own arithmetic is correct, but the move from "three pulses
today" in the moon-look section to "four pulses" on the final line
happens within the same scene (same night, no intervening scene
break), and the fourth pulse is described as arriving "in the last
breath before sleep" — after she had already turned from the moons and
put her head down. This is internally fine as a narrative event but the
two pulse-counts ("three" then "four") within about ten paragraphs risk
feeling like an error to a careful reader. A brief transition marker or
a light narrative beat between the two counts would eliminate the
soft-error appearance. Low urgency but worth flagging for prose pass.

**B. Vane's bark-skin: long-sleeve coverage is a clean sidestep, but
inference risk is moderate.**
The chapter covers Vane's bark-skin by mentioning she is wearing her
coat with hands inside the cuffs, showing cuff-fabric tremor. This
does not contradict the bible's locked upper-arm / near-shoulder
escalation, but the draft does not provide any single physical
confirmation that the bark-skin is still advancing (the Ch. 22 dry-
flaking detail is in the past tense and off-page here). There is no
inference risk for Ch. 23 itself — readers will not presume regression
from the absence of description. However, note that Ch. 26 must give
the group their first clear look; this draft should not be
retroactively used to imply the bark-skin was already visible to
others in Ch. 23 (and it isn't — clean sidestep confirmed).

**C. "Vane managing withdrawal privately" — outline checks out, with
one note.**
The outline says withdrawal is private in Ch. 23, visible to the group
in Ch. 26. This draft honors that: Vane's tremors are visible to
Maren-POV but not named aloud, not acknowledged by any other character
on the page, and Maren deliberately does not go to her. However, the
draft does have Maren register Vane's withdrawal-adjacent behaviors in
unusually specific detail (the deliberate pace, the seam-thumb, the
jaw set, the "fine tremor" in cuff-fabric). This is technically
consistent — Maren is the POV and she knows the situation — but a
future reviser should be aware that Maren's silent detailed observation
should not be extrapolated into the group "knowing" in Ch. 24. Davan
and Aldric see nothing marked in this chapter, and that should hold.

**D. Disc frequency ramp vs. bible canon.**
Bible (Section 5, resolved disc behavior): "several times a day"
escalating through Act 3. Ch. 23 gives 4 pulses on Day 1 east.
Day before was "one pulse at sunset and one just after sleep" (=2).
Day before that was "one." This is a plausible ramp from pre-departure
single pulses into 4/day on Day 1, consistent with the escalation
direction. However, the cooperage (Ch. 20) showed a single visible
pulse during the meeting — and the draft's retrospective note ("the
day before that had been the cooperage") implies only 1 pulse that day.
The escalation from 1 (cooperage day) → 1 → 2 → 4 on Day 1 east is
fast but defensible as proximity-to-site acceleration. No hard
contradiction; flag for the author to confirm the intended rate for
Days 2–5 to avoid overcrowding at the hill.

## New canon introduced

The following elements are new in Ch. 23 and do not conflict with
established bible. They should be folded into the bible on the next
update pass:

1. **Ford crossing, not east gate**: The group exits New Plymouth via
   a ford with a plank walkway on pilings, a gravel riverbed, and an
   upland pasture road climbing to the highland road. No toll-house, no
   ledger. An hour's walk east of the city. The east gate runs a ledger.
   This distinction is now world-canon.

2. **Decoy-riders strategy (if the Tela contradiction is resolved)**:
   Marrick draws papers for two decoy riders who transit the east gate
   visibly and peel north at the first side-road to return. If the
   Tela-as-decoy version is retained after Ch. 22 is edited, this tactic
   and Marrick's document-preparation role are new canon.

3. **Edvar's horses**: A pair, shaggy and short-legged. New canon.

4. **Aldric's horse**: A deep-chested bay, described as older than Maren
   first took it for. New canon. Does not conflict with any previous
   reference.

5. **First camp geography**: Dry-stone sheep-fold in the lee of a rise,
   three walls standing, one fallen, a dry trough, a stand of stunted
   north-bent hawthorns, a stream a short distance off (Davan fetches
   water-skins). One day east of the ford, which is one hour east of New
   Plymouth. This makes Day 1's travel roughly consistent with the
   "upland pasture → highland road → heath → sheep-fold" sequence and
   with the five-day-east hill target.

6. **Aldric not mounting his bay all Day 1**: He walks the horse rather
   than ride. The draft frames this as deference to group pacing. New
   behavioral detail; no conflict.

7. **Aldric's travel kit**: Plain dark travel coat, no cassock. A small
   sealed case (inferred letter-kit). Flat-packed saddlebags. A
   traveler's almanac used as a prop for occupied hands. New canon;
   the almanac detail is consistent with his lamp-tic displacement

8. **Group positions on the march**: Vane scouting ten paces ahead,
   Aldric leading the bay second, cart third, Davan walking beside the
   near wheel, Maren off-wheel side. This is Maren-POV description, not
   assigned formation — but it establishes a Day 1 marching default that
   future chapters may reference.

## Partial-moons-look beat: erodes Ch. 30 or compatible?

**Judgment: Compatible, with one condition.**

The Ch. 23 moon-look is structurally distinct from Ch. 30's closing
communal image. Specifically:

- Ch. 23: Maren, alone, pre-sleep, looking up once from a prone
  position, from inside a sheep-fold. She looks "without counting."
  She notes the three moons' positions but the look is not framed as
  a revelation or a joining — it is a moment of avoidance-habit
  interrupted by exhaustion, followed immediately by a disc pulse.
  She then turns away. The draft frames this explicitly as ambiguous:
  "Whether tonight she was breaking it or only putting it down for a
  minute to rest her neck she did not know."

- Ch. 30 (bible, locked): Moons over the eastern hills. A communal
  gesture. The disagreement held open as the closing image.

These are different registers. Ch. 23's look is solitary, unresolved,
embedded in physical tiredness and travel-anxiety. It is not a
"looking at the moons" moment in the revelatory sense; it is an "I
stopped avoiding them for a minute" moment, and the draft is careful to
leave it unresolved. The disc's pulse immediately after functionally
redirects the beat back to the object-mystery, so the emotional weight
of the scene lands on the disc escalation, not the moons.

Ch. 30 will need to be a *communal* look with all four POVs present,
and it will need to carry the full weight of the "moons are at their
stations" revelation — which this draft does not approach. The Ch. 23
look would only erode Ch. 30 if a later draft promoted it to revelatory
status (having Maren think or say something about the moons' nature).
As written, it does not. The condition: do not let any intervening
chapter (24, 25) have Maren reference this look as the moment she
"started to understand" — keep it unresolved through at least Ch. 26.

**One structural note**: "first time in three years" is implicit (three
years of avoidance), not stated in the draft. That framing appears in
the mission brief, not the chapter text itself. The draft says "I had
been avoiding the moons for three years" — correct, consistent with
the three-year coal-pouch deception timeline from Ch. 1. No
contradiction.

## Verdict

**Conditional pass. One must-fix before Ch. 23 is production-ready.**

The Tela contradiction is the only blocking issue: Ch. 22 establishes
her as a traveler ("Tela will come too") and Ch. 23 casts her as a
decoy rider who does not travel. One of these chapters must be edited.
The simplest fix is a Ch. 22 revision: change Davan's line so that Tela
is named as providing help with departure logistics rather than joining
the journey. Alternatively, if the author prefers Tela as a traveler,
the Ch. 23 decoy-rider passage must be rewritten to name an unnamed
second decoy in Tela's place.

Everything else is structurally sound. The disc escalation, the
withdrawal sidestep, the ford-vs-gate geography, the group dynamics,
the Aldric-footing scene, and the moon look are all handled cleanly.
The partial-moons-look beat is safe as written and does not erode
Ch. 30's climactic communal image provided it is not promoted in
later chapters.

New canon worth folding: ford route, decoy-rider tactic (after Tela
resolution), Edvar's shaggy horses, Aldric's deep-chested bay, and
the sheep-fold first camp. All are addition-only and conflict-free.
