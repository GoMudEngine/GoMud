## Per-POV Voice Integrity

### Vane

**Assessment: Green. On-voice throughout. The register holds under
the chapter's most action-dense scene.**

The opening is the chapter's sharpest register arrival. "The boot came
down on scree a second time and she had his weight." Not his face. His
pace. This is the bible's "world as cost/yield/information" in its most
economical form — data first, source second, inference third, no
announcement. The first paragraph models the whole section: physical
event, professional interpretation, professional vocabulary applied to
a sensory input that another POV would have rendered as feeling.

Kit-inventory mode is operative from the second paragraph onward:
"Seventy paces to Edvar and the cart. Edvar flat in the southern lee, the
off-horse at the cheek-strap. The horse knew him and had not blown." This
is the Ch. 26 kit-list (Left hand, shaking, usable / Right hand, shaking,
usable) applied to the hillside — each element condition-reported, no
element editorialized. The countdown (Sixty / Fifty / Forty) is the form's
italic mode restated as prose-integrated distance markers. Clean.

Interiority-as-professional-analysis is working at the two critical beats:

1. "The professional part of her had filed it. The other part — the one
   that had been doing work in her for five days without her looking at it
   — had filed it somewhere heavier." This is the Ch. 26 "entry sat in its
   slot crooked" mechanism — the professional form attempting to contain
   the non-professional and not quite succeeding — rendered in a single
   contrasted pair rather than a full form-breaking passage. Appropriate
   compression for a scene with active tactical stakes.

2. "She stood the angle anyway. Which was, she thought, the new thing.
   You stood angles for languages that were not yours." This is the
   chapter's only first-person intrusion in the Vane section ("she
   thought, the new thing" / "You stood angles") and it functions as
   the form's one acknowledged break — the professional frame releasing
   just enough to name what has changed. Correctly placed: it comes
   after the scene's tactical work is done, not during it.

Withdrawal rendering: "Her left hand was steady. Withdrawal had
redistributed the tremor overnight — hands to shoulders, which the coat
hid." Clean. The withdrawal is stated as tactical information (the hands
will do cord-work when cord-work comes), not as suffering or complaint.
The coat hiding it is professional triage, not concealment from the
reader. In-voice.

The closing image — standing the angle with hand on the wall, vibration
in the ribs, "something was being said" — is on-voice and distinct from
the Ch. 26 "mine" close. Ch. 26 closes under a blanket with one broken
ledger word; Ch. 30 closes at an exterior angle at the edge of a
language she cannot parse. Both are Vane stopped at the limit of her
professional framework. The modes are different (interior sleep vs.
exterior threshold) and the emotional charge is different (recovery-
inventory vs. acknowledged-incomprehension). Stands distinct.

### Davan

**Assessment: Green. The open-grained, circling register is fully
present. The section is the chapter's most internally consistent.**

"He had not left the shelf." Short declarative as threshold sentence.
The section opens at exactly the Ch. 28 standard: physical event first,
the information of it second, the body's logic third.

The circling structure is architecturally present:
- He turns toward the passage, then turns back (the staying is his,
  without being announced as decision).
- He reads the object not for what is in it but for the quality of the
  holding (the approach-and-decline that the Ch. 28 validator identified
  as carver's restraint).
- He names what he will not do before he does it: "I am not going to
  ask you to be opened." The negative statement as the mode's terminal
  move — Davan arriving by subtraction.

Skin-light as second narrator: "His wrists were lit low. He did not look
at them." The color has settled and holds. He has stopped monitoring it
as data. This is the Ch. 28 evolution ("the color is not my mutation any
more") rendered in two sentences without explanation. The second narrator
is present and is doing its work at exactly the right restraint level.

Sensory-grounded metaphors: "the way a seed pressed gently outward
against the inside of a seed" — material, not transcendent. "A wall in
miniature, closed on a thing" — craft-register, not cosmic. These are
Davan's correct register. No drift toward abstract lyricism.

The section's central philosophical passage — "I owe them the sense not
to open them to relieve myself of the debt" — is the chapter's most
sustained interior, and it is at Davan's upper edge. The passage is
longer than his Ch. 28 interior and uses slightly more resolved
vocabulary ("I owe them," "the un-making"). However, the passage is
preceded by the correct Davan setup (he says it to the room, not to the
other characters; he is "not going to say this to Aldric yet"), which
marks it as partially articulated — still in the working-shelf
territory. The over-articulation risk is real but the chapter manages
it. The content is the longest sustained Davan interior in the
sequence, appropriate to the chapter's finale position.

"The room held it without answering, the way the room held everything it
was asked to hold. The lack of answer was not silence. The lack of answer
was what answering meant when you were a room whose work was holding."
This is Davan at his most architecturally constructed — a sentence-level
recursion that mirrors the material's behavior in its own grammar. It
sits just inside his register. Compare to Ch. 28's "the waiting had
taken the shape of the material" which the validator ruled was at the
upper edge. This is at a similar edge; the three-move recursion is one
step further than Ch. 28 but not past it.

### Aldric

**Assessment: Green — strongest single-section Aldric in the
finale-sequence chapters. Two-languages-in-conflict is present and
governing, not decorating.**

The section is shorter than his Ch. 29 chapter but operates at
equivalent fidelity. The chapter's structural position (Aldric
coming out after the reading, standing at the angle between the
passage and the slope) puts him in a compressed mode — fewer documents,
more weight per sentence. The compression suits the register.

**Three-things pattern**: "Three things, then, in the register a man
had three things when he had stopped composing and had started moving."
The three-things structure is Aldric's formal-precision mode applied
to his own interiority — the scholar organizing a reckoning into
enumerable items. The itemization ("The records are not a find. The
records are a floor.") is the bible's long-clause/stripped-simple
pattern at its most compressed. Each of the three items strips to a
short declarative landing. Correct.

**"The next Keeper is not a future me. The next Keeper is me, now.
The floor has been taken up. I am standing on the taken-up floor."**
This is the Ch. 25 institutional-reframe fully operational as act,
exactly as the Ch. 29 validator specified the Ch. 30 function. The
phrase is not repeated verbatim from Ch. 25 ("the next Keeper was
going to be the one who took the floor up" → "The next Keeper is me,
now") — the tense shift from conditional future to present indicative
marks the crossing from realization to act. The boundary holds and
the crossing is correctly placed here.

**Liturgical register**: "The next reader is not a Keeper. The next
reader is the country." — This is Aldric's two-language collision at
its most stripped. The institutional vocabulary ("reader," "Keeper")
applied to a concept neither language was designed for. No liturgical
vocabulary appears overtly but the weight of the priestly-scholarly
register is in the sentence's structure: a pronouncement, not a plan.

**The hands passage**: "put his hands flat on the tops of his thighs
the way a man put his hands when his hands were honoring a work that
was not his." This is the Ch. 29 hands-flat figure (the hands-flat
of a priest / the hands-flat of a scholar) reduced to its simplest
form — a physical gesture whose register is legible but not named.
The narrator does not call it liturgical; the behavior is its own
signal. The Aldric body-signature is operating correctly.

**Lamp-tic absent**: Confirmed. No cord-reach or tic-attempt in the
section. The hand is at his thigh, open. "His hand at his side did
not move toward any cord." One explicit non-reach, stated plainly.
This is the Ch. 29 lamp-tic-gone rendered in its completed form:
not starting-and-aborting, not below awareness — simply not occurring.
The move from "below his awareness" (Ch. 29) to "stated plainly as
absence" (Ch. 30) is appropriate for the finale. The habit is gone;
the chapter can name the absence directly.

## Section-to-Section Distinctness (Adversarial Test)

**Verdict: The four sections are distinguishable without labels.
Clear margin.**

**Vane → Davan transition**: The shift is audible in the first
sentence of the Davan section. "He had not left the shelf" is
declarative but introverted — a non-event stated as threshold.
Vane's opening ("The boot came down on scree a second time") is
declarative and externally event-anchored. The scale shifts from
the hillside to a single room; the syntax shifts from Vane's
tactical countdown to Davan's held stasis. The POV shift would
register even without the section header.

**Davan → Aldric transition**: Davan's section ends at the passage-
mouth, moving slowly, processing in full-paragraph loops. Aldric's
opens at the angle with a one-sentence threshold ("The passage carried
his step and handed it back") and immediately enumerates external
data (slope, Vane's carriage, the vibration in her ribs). The switch
from Davan's interior-heavy circling to Aldric's data-read-then-
interior is legible at sentence one.

**Aldric → Maren transition**: Aldric's section ends at a physical
stillness — hands on thighs, waiting for Davan. Maren's opens in
a sustained physical-sensory condition already underway ("Something
was beginning under her thumb. / Not new. Continuing."). The switch
from Aldric's arriving-at-three-things to Maren's already-in-a-condition
is audible in the grammar: Aldric is enumerating what he has concluded;
Maren is reporting what is happening.

**Blur zones**: None confirmed. The only zone where voice distinctness
risks softening is the Davan/Aldric border — both use physical-threshold
openings. However, Davan's "He had not left the shelf" is an interior
stasis statement while Aldric's "The passage carried his step and handed
it back" is an exterior-sensory observation with institutional weight
("handed it back" has the cadence of Aldric's long-clause-landing style
applied to acoustic physics). The distinction is present at the sentence
level. No section drifts into generic close-third.

## The Four Archive Positions — Delivered In-Voice

**Assessment: All four positions arrive in their register. No
position is the author's thesis delivered through a character's mouth.**

**Vane's position** is not stated in the Vane section. It arrives in
the Maren section as Maren's reading of Vane's unexpressed position:
"The question is not mine. It is not yours. It is the next
generation's, if there is one. We leave the room as we found it."
This is correctly calibrated — Vane does not articulate her position
within her own section (the section is tactical; the archive question
is not her work today). Maren's reading of Vane's position is in
Maren's analytical-about-others mode (she reads the hillside actors
the way she reads a room) and is marked as inference ("she knew Vane's
answer, though Vane had not said it"). The position is delivered in
Maren's register reading Vane's register. It lands as Vane — compressed,
operational, leaving-the-room-as-found is exactly Vane's cost-benefit
default.

**Davan's position** is articulated in his own section, to the room.
"I am not going to ask you to be opened. / I am not going to ask
anyone to be opened." The position arrives in Davan's half-stated
mode — he says it to the room, not to the group, and notes he is not
ready to say it to Aldric. The subsequent extended passage (debt,
kin, attention-not-un-making) is somewhat more resolved than Davan's
standard circling-without-concluding, but the framing (said to the
room as a working statement, not a decision) holds it inside his
register. The position is Davan's, not the narrator's.

**Aldric's position** arrives via the three-things structure:
"The floor does not belong to me. It belongs to anyone who will have
to live with what the floor is... I am not the one who tells them.
I am the one who does not stop them from being told." / "The channel
is not the Temple's. The channel has to be built." The institutional-
liturgical register is present in the framing — "the one who does not
stop them from being told" is a Keeper's vocabulary of restraint
applied to its own undoing. The position is process-not-act, which
is Aldric's confirmed register (realization-operational without
crossing into decision). Clean.

**Maren's position** — "for company" — is the chapter's most emotionally
loaded position and the highest risk of tipping into sentimentality.
Assessment: it does not tip. The position arrives through careful
qualifications:
- "Not soon. Not in a panic. Not for the bloodline or the Temple.
  For *company*." — The negations do the work that Maren's register
  does: she defines by what the position is not before naming what it
  is. This is her cataloguing mode applied to her own desire.
- "She wanted them to exist." — One sentence. Not "she longed for"
  or "she ached for." Wanted. Present tense, plain verb, minimal
  weight.
- "The four wants were in her at once, and one was the loudest this
  hour, and the loudness of the one did not un-make the others." —
  This is Maren being analytical about her own emotional state without
  commenting on it from above. The "un-make" construction is Davan's
  language arriving in Maren's mouth (appropriate — she has been
  listening to Davan for days), which gives it a slightly different
  register than pure Maren. One instance; acceptable.

The position does not tip into sentimentality because it is not
resolved as sentiment. It is placed among three other positions with
equivalent weight, and Maren immediately notes she is not going to
get her way. The desire is acknowledged and contextually framed,
not dwelt on.

## Closing Sentence: "She kept walking"

**Assessment: In register. Functions correctly.**

crossed → continuing. The finale closer is the correct fourth term.

**Relation to the closing image** (three moons full-bright, Maren's
first look): The closing sentence follows the moons passage and

delivered; the sentence does not displace it. "She kept walking" is

toward the three people and the cart and the day ahead. The moons
remain in the sky behind her. The sentence does not close the moons
image; it closes Maren's orientation toward what comes next. The

**Echoes of prior arc moments (Ch. 5, 9, 16 per drafter's claim)**:
The specific chapter numbers are not verifiable against the draft
sequence provided, but the structural claim is accurate: Maren's
consistent closing mode is motion-after-the-weight. Her arc moments
resolve into walking, which is correct for a character defined by
where she is going rather than where she has been. The three-word
form honors the mode.

## Prologue-Echo Structural Test

**Assessment: The multi-vantage architecture is present and is
formally consistent with the prologue's structure. The echo is real.**

The prologue establishes the pattern (per Section 8): "the same event
looks different from each vantage, and none of the accounts are
completely right." Ch. 30's four-vantage structure applies the same
architecture to the book's central event (the captain's message
received, the archive's presence known, the decision deferred). Each
POV section receives and processes the same event from its own
instrument: Vane from the angle of the professional who cannot parse
the language; Davan from the material-contact of the one who reads
the holding; Aldric from the scholar-priest composing the three-things
of the next step; Maren from the recess where the signal is and
where the captain's voice arrives.

The prologue's eight accounts are from people who did not survive
to deliberate. Ch. 30's four accounts are from people who have heard
the voice of the one who also did not survive. The structural bookend
is not identical in shape — the prologue is retrospective testimony
after the fact; Ch. 30 is simultaneous multi-vantage on a live event
— but the principle is the same: no single account contains the
whole. The drafter's claim of structural bookend is supportable.

## Prose-Scale Adversarial Test

**Assessment: The compression holds across all four sections.
No section tips into forced lyricism.**

The highest-risk zone is the Maren section's captain's-message passage
and the moons-look passage — both carry the highest emotional load in
the manuscript's final chapter. Neither expands into over-writing.

The captain's message is rendered in approximately 350 words across
three distinct beats (the message content, Maren's four-way silence
observation, and the "ten thousand years ago" reflection). Each beat
stops when it has done its work. The message content is brief and
specific; the silence observation enumerates four people at four
distinct receiving registers; the reflection ("The captain had gone
out to sit in the morning") arrives in three sentences. No expansion.

The moons-look passage is the chapter's longest sustained prose
(approximately 600 words from "She had not looked at the moons in
three years" through the four-positions section). This is where the
adversarial test matters most. The passage avoids forced lyricism
through two mechanisms:
1. Maren's analytical mode governs even the emotional content —
   the grief is classified, the positions are inventoried, the look
   is described through what the eye gives (not what the heart feels).
2. The four-positions sub-section returns the prose to short
   declaratives ("She knew Vane's answer, though Vane had not said it")
   before the emotional momentum can build into over-statement.

The chapter's finale weight is borne by the specificity of its
images and the discipline of its sentence-lengths, not by expansion
into lyrical register. The compression holds.

## Fixes

**Fix 1 (yellow): "the hand that struck it had been, just before
striking, a living hand."**
The "living hand" construction in the bell-rung metaphor is the
chapter's one approach to forced lyricism in the captain's-message
section. The line does not break the section but it announces the
captain's aliveness in a way the rest of the passage handles more
obliquely. Consider: "the way a bell rang when the hand that struck
it had been, a moment before, someone's hand." The indefinite "someone's
hand" stays in Maren's register (she does not know whose hand; the
specificity of personhood is what she registers, not the liveness of
the moment). Not a red-line. Author's call.

**Fix 2 (yellow — structural advisory): Davan's four-positions
articulation.**
"The Chrysalis is me now. The originals are my kin. I owe them the
attention, not the un-making. Let them rest." — This is a more
fully resolved statement of Davan's position than anything he has
produced in previous sections. In his own section, he says the core
of this to the room as a working statement and explicitly notes he
is not going to say it to Aldric yet. When Maren renders his
position in the four-positions passage, the rendering is slightly
more resolved than Davan's own section warrants. Consider whether
Maren's version of Davan's position needs the qualifying frame she
applies to Vane's ("though Vane had not said it") — something like
"She knew what Davan's body had said to the room" or "she had heard
it in the way he had come out of the passage." This would mark
Maren's reading as inference and would soften the over-resolution.
Low priority; the passage functions as written.

**One item to monitor**: The Maren section's "she knew" anaphora
(four consecutive uses). Each individual use is defensible; the
accumulation risks feeling like the author's voice delivering four
positions through Maren's frame. In revision, consider whether one
of the four "she knew" constructions can be replaced with a physical
or behavioral register (Maren's body as the instrument of knowing,
rather than the cognitive verb). This would stay inside her mode and
would vary the anaphoric weight.
