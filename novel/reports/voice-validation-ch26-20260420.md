failures.**

The chapter opens in a third-person equivalent of Vane's italic
diagnostic mode, without the italics:

> "Dead hour did not begin at an hour. It began at a change in the cold."

Two sentences. Declarative. The compression is exact. The second
sentence reframes the first before the reader has finished reading it —
which is the cost-benefit ledger move applied to temporal categorization.
Vane does not experience time; she reads it for pattern.

The voice holds across all five sections of the chapter's structure:
the night watch, the morning camp-break, the road under Irongate, the
follower-management scene, and the fold close. No section breaks

## Form-breaking at the prose level

**This is the chapter's structural subject. It renders at the prose
level. The rendering is accomplished.**

The chapter uses three distinct mechanisms to render the form breaking
while the form is in use:

**Mechanism 1: The analytic paragraph that does not complete.**

The follower-analysis passage (back of the cart, pp. 383-389):

> "*Not green. Contract man. Horst's. Not the raw lads. Works
> highland routes. Reads rock cover. Knows how carters break the
> day.*
>
> The analysis began to stretch and snag. *Male. Right-handed,
> probably. Not young.* The next sentence was going to be a
> category that did not fit the data. She did not reach for it.
>
> He was a man behind her at three-quarters of a mile. She could
> smell him. That was enough."

The form's breakdown is the subject of the paragraph: "began to
stretch and snag," "the next sentence was going to be a category
that did not fit the data," "she did not reach for it." The
professional inventory fails mid-inventory and is deliberately
abandoned. The chapter does not explain the failure; it enacts it
in the italic form, then narrates Vane's decision to stop trying.
The three-sentence landing ("He was a man...") is the stripped
declarative that comes when the tools fail — not as defeat but as
triage. This is the most precise execution of form-breaking in any
Vane chapter in the draft sequence.

**Mechanism 2: Filing that does not file cleanly.**

The Davan tailboard passage:

> "She filed it. Not the way she had filed his warmth a week ago.
> Differently. The entry sat in its slot crooked.
>
> She did not try to re-cut the stone."

"Filed" appears here as the last resort of the professional form.
The filing is acknowledged to be imprecise ("crooked"), and the
second paragraph gives up on repair. The double short-declarative
close ("She did not try to re-cut the stone") is the same stripped
landing as the follower-passage above. In both cases the form gives
up correctly rather than forcing a category that does not fit.

**Mechanism 3: The kit-inventory that halts at an entry and repeats.**

The closing sequence (pp. 645-663) is the chapter's structural
climax. The kit-inventory is the clearest formal echo of Ch. 18's
cost-benefit ledger:

> "*Left hand, shaking, usable. Right hand, shaking, usable. Legs,
> tired, usable. Eyes, tired, good at distance. Ears, off, coming
> back in a week. Nose, working. Arm, losing bark at the edges.
> Knife, in pack. Rope, in pack. Lock-tools, in pack. The man at
> three-quarters of a mile, catalogued. The party, here, sleeping.
> The disc in the dark-haired woman's coat, across the fold.*
>
> She got to the end of the list.
>
> She started again.
>
> She stopped at *nose, working* and did not go further."

The restart-and-halt is the form's emotional disclosure. The list
is Vane's professional reflex; stopping at *nose, working* is the
affect the form cannot contain. The subsequent "mine" is the
ledger's only non-operational entry — a property claim where a
condition report belongs, a first-person possessive where every
other entry has been a third-person instrument. The formal break
is precise and earned.

**Assessment: All three mechanisms work. The form-breaking renders at
the prose level, not through narrator announcement.**

## "Filed" tic watch

**Instances: 2.**

1. p. 99: "Vane catalogued it. *Second of the day. First was before
   the bell.*" — "catalogued," not "filed."

2. p. 461: "She filed it. Not the way she had filed his warmth a week
   ago. Differently. The entry sat in its slot crooked."

This is the chapter's only "filed" deployment. It appears twice in
the same passage (once at the sentence level, once in the cross-
reference "the way she had filed his warmth a week ago"), counting
as two tokens from a single placement decision.

**Cross-chapter "filed" tally:**

- Ch. 22: 2 instances ("Vane filed the trust" p. 2; "she filed
  without commentary" p. 11).
- Ch. 25 (Aldric POV): 0 instances. Ch. 25 voice-validation
  confirmed the verb absent from the Aldric chapter.
- Ch. 26: 2 tokens from 1 placement.

The Ch. 24 validator noted "filed" appearing in Davan's POV as an
Aldric-register verb — a cross-POV spread. Ch. 26 returns the verb
to Vane, which is correct. The question is whether 4 "filed" tokens
across Chs. 22 and 26 (the two most recent Vane chapters) signal
author-habit or character-voice.

**Verdict: Load-bearing in this chapter; not clean across the
chapter pair.**

The Ch. 26 placement works — the failed-filing passage earns the
verb by making its own failure the subject. But 4 tokens across
2 adjacent Vane chapters, with the Ch. 22 validator already noting
the cross-chapter accumulation, means the verb is developing into an
author-habit regardless of whether individual deployments are
justified.

**Recommendation:** The Ch. 26 "filed" passage is one of the
chapter's strongest moments and should not be cut. The Ch. 22
instances (p. 2 and p. 11) are the lower-value deployments. In
revision, replace one Ch. 22 instance with a different cognitive
verb — "registered," "logged," "inventoried" — to distribute the
weight. The pattern of "filed" appearing at the end of important
Vane sections is itself becoming a structural tell that breaks the
compression.

### "Sit with this"

**Count: 1 instance.**

> "Vane sat with the empty cup in her lap and the half-hour of
> quiet hands ahead of her and the old professional ledger in
> her head trying to book the transaction—"

This is the prose-audit's flagged phrase in its most literal form:

marginal. The sentence works only because what follows it works.

**Recommendation:** Replace "sat with" with a more specific
physical rendering — "held the empty cup in her lap and let
the ledger run" — to avoid the flagged phrase while preserving
the ledger-failure scene that earns it. Low priority; do in
revision if the passage is touched for other reasons.

### Davan's amber spike

**Clean. Rendered externally at distance and at the tailboard.**

The first Davan read (p. 289-292):

> "His skin at the open collar / took on a thin cool amber for
> the length of a breath and dropped. / He had read the spring
> from ten paces by reading her face."

Vane observes the skin-color from ten paces as an external signal.
She does not enter Davan's interior; she reads his amber as she
would read any external information source. The parenthetical note
("Her face had done a thing without her") is Vane's self-assessment,
not a penetration of Davan's interior. **Clean.**

The tailboard scene reads Davan's pale amber as an external
observation similarly: "His skin at the wrist had a pale amber
wash to it that was not fear." Vane's capacity to distinguish
amber-fear from amber-weight-carried is established in her register
as professional reading, not omniscience. The interpretation stays
within what Vane would infer from behavioral evidence. **Clean.**

### Maren's sweet-flag cup

**Clean. Rendered through visible action, speech, and what Vane
reads from Maren's posture.**

Maren's interior is never entered. The entire sweet-flag scene is

### Aldric's "registers without comment"

**Clean. One appearance. External presence only.**

Aldric is rendered through face-absence and hand-stillness — the

### The follower

**Clean. No omniscient reveal of plans or interior.**

The follower is rendered entirely through Vane's nose and

## Closing image

**Assessment: Lands. Stands distinct from Ch. 22.**

The "mine" landing:

> "*Nose, working,* she thought. And then, because the ledger was
> broken in places tonight but was not broken here: *mine.*"

The single word "mine" works because the ledger has been established
across the chapter as Vane's professional instrument, and every
other entry in the kit-list has been a third-person condition
report ("usable," "in pack," "catalogued"). The possessive
"mine" is the only first-person-singular claim in the entire list.
Its isolation is the disclosure: the ledger, which cannot hold
feeling, holds this. The italics are the form insisting the word
belongs there; the word itself breaks the form's grammar.

The chapter's closing after "mine" — the tremor restarting, the
"The cataloguing, tonight, had done its work," the return to sleep
— is correctly calibrated. The chapter does not linger on "mine."
It places it, observes it briefly, and closes. The restraint is
the form's own characteristic restraint applied to itself.

**Rhymes check:**

- Ch. 22 close ("doing what they had always done") — the red-line
  item from the Ch. 21-22 validator; the revised version removed
  the narrator-over-shoulder clause. As revised, Ch. 22 closes
  under a roof with the body doing its minimum. Ch. 26 closes in
  the same physical mode (sleep, under a blanket, in a fold) but
  arrives there through an active professional inventory rather
  than through the withdrawal-onset. The structural rhyme is
  conscious (sleeping through the withdrawal), but the emotional
  content is different: Ch. 22 is loss-inventory (what the tool
  used to do), Ch. 26 is recovery-inventory (what the tool can
  still do). The distinction holds.

- Ch. 25 Aldric close (hand resting on air) — different POV,
  different mode. No blur.

- Ch. 24 Davan close (carved-wood-against-ribs) — different POV.
  No blur.

**The tremor under the blanket:** "The tremor in her left hand
started up again under the blanket. She did not catalogue it.
The cataloguing, tonight, had done its work." This is on-voice and
does not tip into sentimentality. The tremor is noted and then
the noting stops — which is itself Vane's mode. The sentence
"She did not catalogue it" is the chapter in miniature: the form
acknowledged, the form released, a night's work done. Not sentimental
because it does not grieve the cataloguing. It simply stops.

## Adversarial read: Ch. 25 Aldric vs. Ch. 26 Vane

**Verdict: Clear, unambiguous distinction. No blur.**

**Syntax:** Aldric's sentences in Ch. 25 are built toward landings
("They had built the temple to hide the face") through nested
subordinate clauses. Vane's sentences in Ch. 26 stop. Full stop.
"She kept the coat on." "She walked." "She came back in with nothing
new to report except the wider circuit, and she set that against the
report she had not made and went to the cart." The longest Vane
sentence in Ch. 26 is operational coordination; Aldric's longest
sentences are theological self-examination.

**Diction:** Aldric: sanctuary lamp, pastoral trim, liturgical,
institutional, vault-stone, undercroft, providence, wreckage. Vane:
ash-lee, cold-metal, tremor, bark-skin, extraction, contract man,
sightline, lock-tools. The diction sets do not overlap. "Professional"
appears in both POVs but carries different semantic charge: Aldric's
professional register is the scholar-priest's formation; Vane's is
operational fieldwork.

**Interiority mode:** Aldric builds conclusions in complete sentences
and sits with them. Vane's interiority arrives in italic fragments
that either confirm professional categories or fail to. The italic
form is Vane-exclusive in this chapter, as in Ch. 22.

**Verdict: No blur risk. The registers are immediately distinguishable
without chapter headers or proper nouns.**

## Dialogue economy

**Vane:** Her lines are minimal and functional throughout. "One behind
us." / "Three-quarter mile. Professional. Singular." / "Since the ford."
The tactical briefing at the cart runs longer but is correctly built
as a logistics report — not explanation, just sequence. "I do not walk
point today" is the longest single Vane speech. It lands because the
brevity of everything before it has established that when she speaks
at length, it is because the information requires it. **Clean.**

**Maren:** Her economy matches and mirrors Vane's. "There's tea. Short
kettle. Davan set it." / "How far." / "How long." / "Since the ford."
The sweet-flag speech (eight lines) is the chapter's longest Maren
line and is correctly placed at the chapter's emotional hinge — the
cup delivery scene. The explanation of the herb is spare and practical:
not a gift speech, a pharmacopeia. "It's not a cure. It's a thing that
slows the hands for a half-hour. Drink it or don't." This is Maren's
register at its most precise. **Clean.**

**Davan:** Brief. "Two" and "Three" as the tactical briefing moves
through its points — he tracks the structure and marks the progressions
without elaborating. The tailboard exchange is the longest Davan
passage: "Water?" / "Shoulder?" / "Don't what." / "I don't know how
to turn it in, exactly. Yet." The "Yet" is the right Davan word — he
circles but he does not lie about the circling. The half-committed
half-answer ("I don't know... yet") is his characteristic mode. **Clean.**

**Aldric:** Sparse. "Not Horst." / "Not Horst." / "How certain." /
"Tactics." / "He'll find it empty." Three of his five lines are
repetitions or responses. In Ch. 26 this is correct: Aldric is in
the scholar's receiver mode (confirmed in Ch. 25), not leading the
tactical problem. The one line that characterizes him distinctly:
"Not Horst. Horst is in New Plymouth. This is one of his. Horst stays
with a city contract." — He has absorbed the operational framework
and can deploy it, but the sentence builds in Aldric's long-clause-
short-statement mode even in fieldwork. Legible as Aldric without
the tag. **Clean.**

## One flag: "sit with this" adjacent construction

**Line:** "Vane sat with the empty cup in her lap and the
half-hour of quiet hands ahead of her and the old professional
ledger in her head trying to book the transaction —"

The phrase "sat with" appears in the manuscript-wide tic list.
The Ch. 25 validator ruled one "sat with" earned by the
specificity of its object ("the not-reaching"). This instance
is less defended — Vane literally sits and processes, which
is the workshop version of the cliché. The ledger-failure that

**Suggested fix:** "Vane held the empty cup in her lap with the
half-hour of quiet hands ahead of her and the old professional
ledger trying to book the transaction —" The physical act of
holding gives the body something to do; the cognitive ledger
proceeds on its own without the verb "sat" carrying both the
body and the metaphor.

cognitive-verb deployment to prevent "catalogued" and "filed"
from monopolizing the register.
