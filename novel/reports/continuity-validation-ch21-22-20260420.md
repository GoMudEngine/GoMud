## Chapter 21

### Contradictions (must-fix)

**C21-1: Reth's survey timeline stated as "forty years ago" in Ch. 17
but described as "forty years ago" again in Ch. 21.**
In Ch. 17 v2, Reth explicitly says: "Forty-one years ago." Aldric's
account in Ch. 21 condenses this to "a surveyor named Reth who walked
the eastern highlands forty years ago." Minor rounding, but the draft
gives Aldric a precise figure from a first-hand conversation; the
compression to a round "forty" when "forty-one" was locked in
manuscript is a soft error that may confuse a later continuity pass.
Recommend: "forty-odd years ago" or "forty-one years ago" to match
the source precisely.

**C21-2: Aldric refers to "twelve maps" in Brennan's possession during
his account to the group (p. 17 of chapter: "Twelve maps in his
possession. Four in the oldest, three in the youngest.").**
Bible (Section 5, Brennan's Letter) and Ch. 17 v2 confirm Brennan
has twelve maps. This is correct. Clean check — noted here only
to flag that the count should remain locked at twelve in all
future chapters.

**C21-3: Edvar's face display — Aldric's account describes "one
irregular, damaged" (his own Confluence face, not Edvar's).**
In Ch. 21, when Aldric gives his account to the group, he says:
"The image is an arrangement of orbital bodies. Four of them. One
of them is not like the others. It is irregular. It reads as
damaged." This is correct for the Confluence face per bible Section
5 (Aldric's face = "irregular, damaged"). Edvar's face (smaller/
distant) is present in the room as the iron-banded trunk, but Ch. 21
does not compare the two displays, which is correct — that comparison
is reserved for Ch. 25. Clean check.

**C21-4: Crane's New Plymouth agent — "a week past, perhaps ten"
since the false filing.**
Bible (Section 2, Prioress Crane): "Reach extends to New Plymouth
office (Ch. 21)." Ch. 21 correctly uses this. The agent is described
as "a Keeper from Confluence" with "scholarly consultation, eastern
provinces" as cover — consistent with Crane's methodical, procedural
character. Clean check.

**C21-5: Aldric does NOT reveal the sealed chamber to the group in
full in Ch. 21.**
He omits Crane, omits the false entry, and withholds the four
containers ("five objects... one of them is a flat dark surface").
He describes the face and its display, Brennan's twelve maps, and
Reth's survey/recess, but does NOT inventory all five objects or
name the four containers. Per bible, Aldric tells the group about
the containers fully in Ch. 24; this restraint is consistent.
Clean check.

**C21-6: Young cartographer named "Tela" — NEW PROPER NOUN.**
The bible describes the young cartographer as "Unnamed. Female.
Fresh ink stains on the heel of her writing hand." Ch. 21 assigns
her the name "Tela" — and notably Aldric "did not catch the family
name on first hearing." This is new canon. See New Canon section.
This is a flag, not a contradiction, since the bible left her
deliberately unnamed pending a decision on her purpose.

## Chapter 22

### Contradictions (must-fix)

**C22-1: Bark-skin direction — regression risk at the wrist.**
Bible (Section 2, Vane; Section 7, draft-revision directive):
"upper arm nearly to the shoulder" as of Ch. 18 v2. This is the
locked canon. Ch. 22 introduces the bark-skin change at "the patch
at the back of her right wrist" — a drier, looser patch beginning
to flake. A wrist-patch showing change would be consistent ONLY if
the established spread (upper arm / near shoulder) is also present
and the wrist-patch is read as a SEPARATE observation on the same
patch she has always had (spread upward from wrists). The
chapter's framing is ambiguous: Vane observes the wrist patch
specifically, as if it is her primary bark-skin observation point.
If a reader infers from this that the bark-skin is concentrated
at the wrist (not the upper arm / near shoulder), that is a
regression from Ch. 18.

**Required fix:** Add at least one reference to the upper-arm
extent in Ch. 22 to anchor the full canonical spread. The wrist-
patch observation can coexist if it is framed as "the lowest
edge of the patch" or "at the edge where the bark-skin first
began." Without this anchor, Ch. 22 reads as if the bark-skin
has regressed to wrist-level only. The one Ch. 22 line that does
reference upper arm — "the bark-skin on her right arm... from
shoulder to wrist" in the coat-removal scene — partially addresses
this (she gives away the coat and her "right arm was exposed from
shoulder to wrist"), which implies full-arm extent. This is helpful
but the phrasing "shoulder to wrist" describes exposure range,
not bark-skin extent. One explicit note on bark-skin extent
(e.g., "the patch at the back of her wrist — the lowest edge of
the grain that had been climbing since Cade's") would lock the
canon unambiguously.

**C22-2: Bloom-giver's luminescence described as "diminished" in
Ch. 18 canon but "faint luminescence" implied in Ch. 22.**
Bible (Ch. 18 v2 manuscript text): "luminescence was dim. Banked."
Ch. 22: "The mutations were the same. The frond-fingers, the
translucency at temples and wrists." The luminescence is not
explicitly re-described in Ch. 22 — the frond-fingers and
translucency are mentioned but the "dim / banked" luminescence
that was specifically noted in Ch. 18 is not carried forward.
This is a soft omission rather than a hard contradiction; if the
luminescence is still dim and banked (which is likely, the woman
has been captive), the chapter should confirm this rather than
leave it open. Recommend adding one word confirming the luminescence
state when Ch. 22 re-describes her. (The woman is thinner than
four days ago — the luminescence is likely even dimmer.)

**C22-3: "Junie" — the unbidden name — is spoken aloud by Vane.**
Bible (Ch. 18 v2): "*Junie,* the thought arrived, unbidden — not
a name she knew this woman by, not a name at all, just the kind
of name a farmhand girl might have had before someone took her."
In Ch. 22, Vane gives the woman the name "Junie" when the woman
asks for a name for the road. The woman explicitly says "That's
not mine" and Vane says "I know" — both characters acknowledge
it is not her real name. This is internally consistent: the
unbidden thought from Ch. 18 becomes the word Vane offers when
the woman needs a name. This is not a contradiction; it is a
deliberate callback. However, the chapter should be marked as
having CANONIZED the name "Junie" as the identity the Bloom-giver
takes for her journey south. This is new canon (the woman's fate
is now partially resolved — she goes to a cousin in the fishing
villages south, via the coast road, with a coin her mother sewed
into her shoe). See New Canon section.

### Drift / soft inconsistencies

## Cross-chapter consistency

### Time gap / continuity between 21 and 22

Ch. 21 takes place over approximately two days (Aldric arrives
Day 1, finds Edvar, sleeps; Day 2 goes to cooperage evening
meeting). Ch. 22 opens the following morning ("the morning" after
the cooperage meeting). This is internally consistent. The
reference bridge: Ch. 22 has Vane reference "last night in the
cooperage basement" confirming the meeting was the night before
Ch. 22's action.

The cooperage meeting in Ch. 21 and Vane's freeing of the Bloom-
giver in Ch. 22 are consecutive days. This timeline works: the
group decides to leave tomorrow (Ch. 22 close = tonight), meaning
Ch. 23 begins the morning after Ch. 22's events.

### Bark-skin state across chapters

Ch. 21 does not show Vane directly (she is at the gate perimeter).
Ch. 22 opens with Vane in the rooming house. No inconsistency
introduced — the chapter break covers the gap. However, see C22-1:
the upper-arm extent of the bark-skin should be explicitly anchored
in Ch. 22 to prevent regression inference.

### Davan's skin-light cross-chapter

In Ch. 21, Aldric reads Davan's amber-then-green-gold during the
meeting. In Ch. 22 (from Vane's POV), Davan arrives and his skin
is "blue, calm, and held without fluctuation." Both are consistent
with the emotion-coded system and the specific emotional context
(Ch. 21 = recognition/processing; Ch. 22 = arriving at a sick
friend's room, choosing calm). Clean.

### Vane observing Aldric's disc-hand hesitation

Vane in Ch. 22 knows Aldric's hand stopped "a span from the disc"
— information available only to someone who saw the cooperage
basement interior. This observation bridges the chapters but
requires Vane to have had line-of-sight to the interior. As noted
in D22-9, the mechanism is not specified. This is the one
cross-chapter logic gap requiring attention.

### Any conflicts

No additional conflicts found between Ch. 21 and Ch. 22.

## Verdict

**Chapter 21 — Yellow flags (light).** No hard factual
contradictions against locked canon. The chapter correctly carries
the Ch. 20 aftermath (Renner's broken framework, disc pulsing,
container inert), accurately deploys Aldric's knowledge cargo
(face display as "irregular/damaged," Brennan's twelve maps, Reth's
survey with coin-recess and symbol, the "for unchanged hands"
phrase), and introduces him to the group without forcing his hand
or revealing the containers. The primary flags are: (1) the young
cartographer named "Tela" — the bible had left her deliberately
unnamed, so this is a canon-addition requiring author sign-off;
(2) Aldric's surname "Voss" now canonized on-page for the first
time; (3) a cluster of new NP proper nouns (Inkwalk, Yelin, Father
Thane, Old Capital, Saint Imret). All of these are additive, not
contradictory, but must be folded into the bible. The Reth "forty"
vs. "forty-one" rounding is the one precision error to fix.

**Chapter 22 — Yellow flag (one must-fix).** The chapter handles
Vane's withdrawal onset, bark-skin regression framing, Bloom-giver
liberation, "five days east," and "leave tomorrow" correctly. The
sole must-fix is C22-1: the bark-skin upper-arm extent needs one
explicit anchor to prevent a reader inferring regression to wrist-
level only. The coat-removal scene partially covers this ("from
shoulder to wrist" describes exposure, not bark-skin extent) but
is ambiguous. One sentence is sufficient to close this. C22-2
(luminescence state) is a soft omission worth one word in revision.
D22-9 (Vane's observation mechanism) is a logic gap that should be
addressed in either Ch. 22 or Ch. 23.
