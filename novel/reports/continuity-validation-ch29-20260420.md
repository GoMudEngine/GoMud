### 9. "The Channel Would Be Something He Built" — AUTHORIAL CALL REQUIRED

**VERDICT: SOFTEN, but the author's final call.**

The Bible is explicit: Ch. 25 delivers "realization, not decision; the
decision lands later." The chapter prompt explicitly says "He does NOT
decide in Ch. 29. He arrives at the decision's threshold."

The line in question: "The channels he had been trained to use would
suppress what he sent through them. The channel would be something he built."

As written, "would be" is future indicative — this is operational
planning, not threshold-awareness. The sentence structure implies Aldric
has already resolved the *that* and moved to the *what* of channel-building.
Compare to Ch. 25's correct version: "The *how* was tomorrow's, or the
week's, or the year's. The *that* was this hour's." Ch. 25 held the *how*
at arm's length. Ch. 29's "the channel would be something he built" hands
him the *how* in embryonic form.

This is not a hard contradiction — one could read "would be" as a realization
arriving rather than a decision taken. But it is the sharpest line in the
chapter and it does shade toward decision. The surrounding paragraph ("That
work was tomorrow's work. This hour's work was to receive what was in front
of him") actively tries to walk back the sharpness, and the walkback is
partially successful. But the line itself is operationally specific enough
that a careful reader will feel the threshold crossed.

**Recommendation:** Soften. Suggested replacement register: something that
registers the channel-problem as a newly-seen obstacle rather than a
newly-identified solution. E.g., "The channels he had been trained to use
would suppress what he sent through them. He did not yet know what a
different channel looked like." This preserves the realization that the
existing institutional channels are blocked (which is new information at
this moment) without landing on "I will build a new one." The *that* of
needing a non-institutional channel can arrive here; the *what* of
building it is Ch. 30's.

**Author's call.** If the author reads "would be" as realization-register
rather than decision-register, keep. If soften, use the direction above or
equivalent.

### 16. New Canon Requiring Fold

The following elements introduced or confirmed in Ch. 29 are new or
specific enough to warrant folding into the Bible at next revision:

**A. Archive-within-archive structure (voiced wall as parallel register).**
The storage room has two distinct registers: written (left wall) and voiced
(right wall), at the same catalog positions and dates. The voiced objects
have a single-panel indentation that activates on palm contact. The drafter
calls the voiced wall Aldric's "second register" for the archive — distinct
from the written objects. This is implied by Ch. 28's materials canon but
not explicitly described there. The "voiced wall" deserves an entry in
Section 3's hull-interior canon.

**B. Catalog/seal format on records.**
Records carry: catalog numbers along one edge (in the mission's alphabet),
the inner-orbit figure as a header, and an office seal (distinct from
church seals, the stamp of an enterprise). The heading format is:
inner-orbit figure + seal, then body text. This is new specific detail.

**C. "Native-resistant baseline" as technical terminology.**
This phrase appears in the native-organism study document's right-hand
cell (the unchanged cell). It is the office's technical name for what the
theology calls "chosen." Lock this as the ship's technical vocabulary for
immune/unchanged humans.

**D. Hold IV designation.**
The four archive containers from the Confluence sealed chamber are
confirmed as Hold IV cargo (stern of the lander, four holds, sub-labeled).
The hold-numbering system (three of a family, one apart) mirrors the
manifest's destination-column structure. Lock Hold IV as the formal
designation for the Confluence containers' origin compartment.

**E. Bio-lock specificity.**
The bio-lock does not distinguish between direction of contamination —
it reads for the absence of the organism in the opener's biology. Even
immune-lineage descendants (the bloodline) cannot open it because
10,000 years of integration means the organism is present even in those
whose immune systems kept it inactive. This is the most specific rendering
of the bio-lock mechanism to date and resolves the question of whether any
living person can open the archive. The answer is no. Fold into Section 5.
