## Cross-chapter patterns (author-wide, not chapter-specific)

1. **Filter-word cluster in Ch. 20 disc-reveal interior.** Five "she thought
   about" openings in sequence. Anaphora is intentional and good; filter
   is redundant and weakens the rhythm. **Fix:** cut the filter, keep the
   object. "She thought about her father" → "Her father: he had carried
   this disc. He had gone east." The rhythm holds; the filter disappears.
   (Line editor: this is the single most important filter fix in the
   whole pass.)

2. **"Non-smile mouth" tic has spread.** What the bible flagged as a
   Vane-specific gesture ("not quite a smile" / "corner of Vane's mouth")
   now appears across three different characters in three chapters:
   Reth (Ch. 17), Asha (Ch. 18, "mouth twitched"), Renner (Ch. 20,
   "mouth did something that might have been the ghost of approval").
   This is an **author-level stage direction for suppressed emotion**,
   not a character tell anymore. Needs a manuscript-wide inventory before
   the next revision pass.

3. **Aldric's liturgical register is thin.** The lamp tic is carrying his
   distinctiveness almost alone. The bible calls for "a man of the cloth
   realizing his god is a shipwreck" — guilt and theological vertigo
   audible in word choice. Ch. 17's Aldric is a thoughtful scholar, not
   quite a Keeper. **Fix:** push ecclesiastical/liturgical vocabulary into
   his interiority, especially at the Reth coin-circle moment and the
   road-west meditation. Even 1-2 church-register terms in those passages
   will anchor his voice against Vane's worldly-operational register.

4. **"Sit with this" cliché.** Appears in Ch. 17 ("He sat with this as
   the road straightened") and Ch. 19 ("They sat with that"). Workshop-era
   metaphor for processing. Once per manuscript is fine; twice in two
   chapters is a tic. **Fix:** revise Ch. 17 (cut framing or use "the
   thought rode with him"); keep Ch. 19's literal/physical use.

5. **Two chapters closing on the same note.** Beta reader flagged:
   Ch. 17 and Ch. 19 both end on "a protagonist thinking a large question
   at a retreating lamp." Rhythm is too similar. **Fix:** vary one — most
   likely Ch. 19 (see Ch. 19 priorities below).

## Decisions needed from the author before revision

### 1. The "chosen not to change" phrase in Ch. 20
Beta reader's sharpest question: **whose voice is "chosen not to change"?**
Is it Maren's interior, or the narrator sliding it in? The word *chosen*
carries theological weight — it implies a chooser. If it's Maren's own
thought, she is claiming the theological frame, which is a different book
than if it arrives as a whisper from outside her. **Author decision
needed.** This is a single phrase but load-bearing.

### 2. Ch. 18 bark-skin direction (minor)
Ch. 18 says "past the elbow now." If bark-skin spreads wrist-to-shoulder,
"past the elbow" means it hasn't yet reached mid-bicep (Ch. 4 baseline).
Regression is explicitly wrong per bible. If the intent is "above the
elbow" (past elbow toward shoulder), rephrase. (Continuity D-2.)

### 3. Bible gaps: fold in or defer?
Continuity agent surfaced 8 bible gaps (Ch. 20 specifics — Pip's physical
build, disc material equivalence with face and container, Brennan's "for
unchanged hands" phrase, face activation persistence, cooperage basement
layout, young cartographer details, Vane-at-gate note, Maren's Ch. 20
disc realization as an arc beat). All small. None load-bearing. **Should
I fold these into the bible now, or defer until after this revision?**
