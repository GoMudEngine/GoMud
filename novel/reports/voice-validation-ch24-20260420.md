## Davan voice signature match

**Assessment: Strong. The chapter is a confident extension of the Ch. 19
benchmark — the same processing architecture, more fully inhabited.**

The open-grained, observational register is operative from the first
paragraph. The stone-cold sequence at the gate wall is canonical Davan:

> "The stones were cold. The cold went into his fingertip and did not
> travel. Old stone did not give warmth back. It took warmth away slowly
> and politely and handed it down into the earth."

This is Davan attending to a physical texture and circling it into
implication — not announcing what it means, letting the fact arrive.
The approach-leave-return structure holds throughout: the note under
the country is introduced ("not a smell, exactly. A register"), dropped,
approached again at the exposed slab near noon, approached again at
camp through Aldric's testimony, never forced. The chapter's final
sequence is the arrival, and it comes by accumulation.

The half-thought register is consistently honored. "Maybe it was —"
is the clearest instance: the sentence breaks at the dash, the thought
is not continued, the narration moves on. No annotation, no
editorial reassurance. The form enacts Davan's characteristic
mid-circling suspension. Similarly, "There's something —" in the
exchange with Maren: he starts, does not finish, she does not prompt.
Both are model deployment of the signature.

Davan's specific-noticing mode (connecting through texture, smell,
shadow) is present and working:

- The bark-flake catch: seen, looked past, absorbed into his carving
  hand before he has named it.
- The wheel-hub touch (warm vs. cold as diagnostic): the small motion
  of a body that reads the world through material contact.
- The greenish cast between collar and jaw: he does not read it
  himself; the reader sees it.
- The ash-tree branches, north-bent: "The country had a way of telling
  you which way the wind lived." Davan noticing landscape as language.

The Ch. 19 precedent ("It came down") for the weight-of-landing
sentence is matched here by "Edvar has one too" — short, flat, plain,
arrived at after a full chapter of circling. The sentence comes out
"flat" and "plain" and the narration marks this precisely. This is
correct technique. The circling has done its work and the sentence
lands because of it.

Metaphors from carving and natural material are present throughout:
the shape-under-the-knife figure for the container-connection
thought; the woodgrain running straight down inside the carved hill;
the water-working-through-stone figure in Ch. 19 still echoing in
register if not in explicit invocation. "The country had a way of
telling you" is one of the chapter's best sentences because it is
Davan extending the carving-intelligence to landscape — reading
grain in an entire country.

**One drift zone:** The passage "He let the thought come without
forcing it to a shape. He circled it." (para beginning "It was not
a human note") is Davan being Davan narrated, rather than Davan
being Davan shown. The sentence is the bible's Ch. 19 red-line
item — "He let the thought arrive at its own pace" — almost exactly
reproduced. The circling is already structurally embedded; the
announcement is redundant.

*Suggested revision:* Cut "He let the thought come without forcing it
to a shape. He circled it." Begin the next beat directly: "Near noon,
he stopped at the verge..." The chapter has earned this ellipsis.

## Aldric-in-dialogue voice check

**Assessment: The two-languages-in-conflict is audible and correctly
calibrated for a Maren/Davan-POV chapter.**

The bible specifies the liturgical-scholarship collision should be
*audible* in dialogue — formal clauses with institutional vocabulary,
stumbling where liturgical words arrive where scholarly ones should sit.
Ch. 24 delivers this in Aldric's outcrop testimony:

> "He said *eight weeks* like a man giving testimony. A scholar gave
> a duration that way; a priest gave a confession that way; and Aldric
> was giving it both ways at once."

This is Davan — not the narrator, Davan — reading the collision from
the outside. The diagnosis is done by the POV character inferring
from voice and posture, which is the correct technique for Aldric's
register when he is not the POV.

The stumble on "descent" is the chapter's best Aldric vocal moment:

> "— and a descent." He said the word like a man who had known for
> weeks it was the right word and had only just now said it aloud.

This is audible conflict: the Keeper finding that a secular word (the
right scholarly word) has been waiting under the liturgical one, and
releasing it. The reader understands both the scientific meaning and
the theological weight it displaces.

**"Patient urns" — verdict: lands correctly.**

The agent's flag is accurate: "patient urns" is liturgical vocabulary
in a material context. Aldric uses it consciously:

> He did not flinch at the comparison. He used the word.

This is the bible's specification made explicit in narration — Aldric
aware of what register his vocabulary is in and choosing not to
retreat from it. The choice is marked on the page, which means the
reader is not required to notice it independently. This is slightly
over-annotated: the phrase itself is sharp enough that "He did not
flinch at the comparison. He used the word." can be cut to just
"He did not flinch." or removed entirely, trusting the phrase to
land without the stage direction. But as a failure mode this is mild
— over-annotation, not a voice miss.

The follow-on to "patient urns" ("— it *showed me.*" with the
liturgical word catching before it) is the chapter's strongest
single instance of the collision. The italics signal the word
arriving from the wrong register; Aldric's "because it was the word
he had" is the self-awareness the bible calls for. This is exactly
the formal clause → stripped admission pattern.

**Aldric's hands folding at the waist:**

> His hands had lifted from the stone and folded themselves at his
> waist in the small involuntary gesture of a man whose hands had
> remembered, for a second, that they had been trained to fold.
> The folding looked awkward on him in plain clothes. He saw that
> it looked awkward, and he did not unfold, because unfolding would
> have been another thing.

This is the body enacting the two-register conflict through gesture.
Not a vocal moment but performing the same work as the liturgical
vocabulary: the Keeper's training in a secular body on a secular road.
Strong.

**One concern:** Aldric says "I have a thing to tell you" — this
register is flatter than his established voice. Aldric's formal
mode tends to subordinate clauses; "I have a thing to tell you" is
colloquial. Not a serious miss — he is mid-moment, self-interrupting
— but worth checking against the author's intention.

## Filter words (counts and citations)

**Count: 2 firm instances. 1 borderline. Clean by Davan's chapter
average (4 instances in Ch. 19 before the v2 revision; the v2 cut
that to 2 borderline).**

1. **"Davan watched him work"** — para. 2, "Across the fold, Aldric was
   crouched..." ending "Davan watched him work and thought, not for the
   first time since the basement, that there was a man who knew how to
   be useful without being in the way."
   The sentence pairs "Davan watched" (filter) with a subsequent
   observation/inference. The observation can arrive directly without
   the watching announcement.
   *Suggested:* "Aldric crouched by the ash of the fire, testing the
   coals, then adding the prepared grass. Not for the first time since
   the basement: a man who knew how to be useful without being in the
   way."

2. **"he had been watching, without meaning to, the way the hand went
   in and out of the coat"** — the tor-stop Maren exchange. "He had
   been watching" is a filter wrapper around what is already being shown
   by the skin-at-the-collar detail. The fact of the watching can be
   cut; Davan stopping at Maren's elbow because of the hand is evidence
   enough.
   *Suggested:* Cut "he had been watching, without meaning to," and
   open directly: "He came over now because her hand had been going in
   and out of the coat across the morning."

3. **"she was listening"** — re: Maren during Aldric's "Brennan's letter"
   passage. "Maren did not move. She was listening." The first sentence
   shows it; the second announces it. Cut the second sentence.
   **Borderline:** the sentence is barely a filter — "listening" here is
   behavioral description more than perceptual annotation. Low priority.

**Notably absent:** No "he felt," "he realized," "he noticed," "he
understood," "he was aware." The chapter is clean on the highest-
priority filter forms. The two firm instances are structural (watching

v2 revision discipline is applied correctly here.

## POV discipline

### Vane's bark-flake glimpse

**Assessment: Correctly handled. No interior leak.**

> "Something had come off her skin and settled on the cuff. Pale.
> A flake. Smaller than a thumbnail. He saw it and looked past it
> and did not know he had seen it until later, when the shape of
> it was already in his carving hand."

The bark-flake is rendered as a visual event Davan perceives,
processes below awareness, and integrates into his hands without
narrating Vane's experience. The chapter does not enter Vane's
interior at this moment — does not say what the bark-flake means
to her, does not describe the texture from inside her arm, does not
name her awareness of being observed. Davan sees it and the
consequence is in *his* carving hand, not in Vane's body.

The phrase "a bit of something that was nobody's business but hers"
is Davan describing what he reads from Vane's motion — her covering
action, not her interior. This is Davan inferring privacy from
behavior, which is legitimate POV inference. **Clean.**

### Aldric's distress at the face-display discussion

**Assessment: Rendered externally. No omniscience breach.**

Aldric's response to the face being placed on the blanket:

> "Aldric made a small sound, the sound a man made when his breath
> caught on a word and let it go without the word coming."

This is a sound Davan can hear. The interpretation of its cause
("breath caught on a word") is Davan inferring from what he
observes. He does not state what Aldric felt.

The hands-folding passage (post-outcrop testimony) is rendered
through external observation: Davan sees the hands fold, sees the
awkward look, sees Aldric noticing the awkwardness. He does not enter
the Keeper's experience of this. His inference ("the small involuntary
gesture of a man whose hands had remembered, for a second, that they
had been trained to fold") is grounded in observation of the gesture
and prior knowledge of Aldric's background. **Clean.**

**One passage on the line:**

> "He had heard himself approaching a sentence a cartographer had
> been unable to finish for twenty years, and his hesitation was not
> theological. It was courtesy."

"His hesitation was not theological. It was courtesy." — These two
sentences are narrated about Aldric's internal state without being
explicitly marked as Davan's inference. Davan can observe the
hesitation; he cannot observe its motivation with certainty. The
sentences are presented as fact about Aldric's interior rather than
as Davan's reading of a behavior.

*Suggested fix:* Mark as inference: "Davan read the hesitation as
courtesy, not theology." Or: "The hesitation had a different shape
than the theological ones had. Courtesy." This is Davan's
empathic-reading register, which the bible establishes for him.
The fix retains the insight while grounding it in Davan's perception.

## New carving beat

**Assessment: The hawthorn hill-piece is the chapter's strongest
sustained passage. Hands-knowing-before-mind is correctly deployed.**

The Ch. 19 precedent is the pine carving made before Davan met
Maren — "his hands working out a shape that his mind had not yet
assembled." Ch. 24's hawthorn piece works the same mechanism:

> "He did not try to see what he was carving. He let his hands work."

And then: the shape is a piece of ground. Not the symbol — he
explicitly distinguishes it from the orbital symbol, which his hands
know by repetition. This is a new shape, produced by a day of the
empathic-country note working in his wrists.

The woodgrain technique ("he had gone against the grain with his
knife-cut at the top, so the fibre ran straight down inside the
shape, and the straight-down of the grain made the top of the rise
look like it had something beneath it that was not the rise") is
the chapter's single best piece of prose for Davan's voice. The
carver's intelligence applied to a discovery about buried things.
His hands are ahead of his mind and have already solved the problem
before he looks down. This is canon.

**"A buried thing" — verdict: correctly half-articulated.**

> "He had carved a buried thing."

This is a bare declarative, following a full paragraph of technical
description of what the carving is. The sentence does not over-
explain. It names the result in Davan's characteristic mode — a
simple short sentence after circling — and stops. The register is
correct. Compare to "It came down" in Ch. 19: same technique, same
weight. The bible precedent is honored.

One risk: the sentence is followed by two additional paragraphs of
reflection ("the note under the country working in his wrists all
day... the shape of what his body had been reading"). This is the
Ch. 19 red-line pattern — explaining the metaphor after it has
landed. "He had carved a buried thing" could end the paragraph, and
the following paragraphs could continue from the scene-return
("He set it on the blanket beside him") without the explanatory
bridge. The explanation slightly dilutes the statement.

*Suggested revision:* Cut "He had carved it without deciding to, and
he had carved it with the note under the country working in his wrists
all day." Begin the next beat with "The piece of hawthorn was not the
hill east of New Plymouth..."

## Empathic-country sustained texture

**Assessment: The chapter's central accomplishment. The "not-naming"
is sustained with one slip.**

The outline called for "less human noise, more of something older
underneath, a quality he doesn't have words for," and the chapter
delivers this without naming it in the passages that matter most.

The approach is enacted in layers:

1. "Not a smell, exactly. A register." — Names only the sense-mode,
   not the content.
2. "Beneath the cold of the surface, not quite reaching him and not
   quite failing to, was — the faintest — something the stone did
   not have on its own account. A carrying." — The sentence performs
   its own incompletion. The dash and the fragment enact the limit
   of the sensation.
3. "The rock was carrying a thing that was not the rock." — Another
   declaration at the limit, not past it.
4. The final camp passage: "the country underneath it all carried its
   carried thing in its cold slow register and said nothing because
   it had not been asked a question."

The last of these is the chapter's best empathic-country sentence.
The country does not offer its content; it holds it passively; the
question has not been asked yet. This is dramatizing the
"not-naming" — the thing is present but the instrument is not yet
calibrated to receive it.

**The one slip:**

> "He was aware of something that was not alive and was not human
> and was not stone, and the something was *under his feet.*"

"He was aware of" is a filter wrapper that announces the empathic
event rather than rendering it. More critically: "that was not alive
and was not human and was not stone" tells the reader what it is not
(i.e., manufactured, non-geological, alien) — which comes close to
naming it. The passage is trying to approach the thing by negation,
but the three negations together effectively describe it. Compare
to "A carrying" earlier: one word, no negations, the sensation
itself rather than its category.

*Suggested revision:* "The proximity-sense had worked in the
cooperage with Corvel's container. It had worked in Edvar's back
room with the face. It worked now. The something was under his feet."
This retains the escalation and the discovery without the
enumerated negations that approach naming.

## Adversarial read vs. Ch. 23

**Assessment: The chapters are clearly distinguishable beyond POV
tags. Rhythm, diction, and interiority mode are all distinct.**

Reading Ch. 23 (Maren) and Ch. 24 (Davan) side by side:

**Rhythm:** Maren's chapter uses short declaratives stacked without
connective tissue: "She catalogued the room before she left it.
Two beds, one stripped." Ch. 24 uses longer reaches that circle
back: "The walls along the field margins went from field-clearing
walls, wide and rubble-filled, to older walls — narrower, tighter-
fitted, built by hands that had cared about the fit and had not been
in a hurry." Maren inventories. Davan attends to craft and process.

**Diction:** Maren's chapter uses exit, arithmetic, reflex, filing,
deferred, arranged. Ch. 24 uses carving, register, circling,
carrying, grain, politely. The material-world vocabulary is entirely
different. Maren's is operational; Davan's is craft and texture.

**Interiority mode:** Maren's interiority is retrospective analysis
("The arithmetic was so automatic that she registered it only after
she had already arranged herself to it"). Davan's is provisional
and ongoing — he is processing while the chapter runs, not after.
The conclusions Maren reaches are clear and owned; Davan's are
approached and partially named, sometimes not named at all.

**Skin-light:** Davan has a second narrator the reader can see but
Maren does not. The green-gold at the collarbones is a continuous
presence in Ch. 24 that has no structural equivalent in Ch. 23.
This alone makes the adjacent chapters immediately distinguishable.

One area where blur is possible: both chapters involve sustained
periods of walking-and-thinking, and both use short-sentence punches
after longer observational paragraphs. But the observational
subjects (Maren: tactical assessment of people and sightlines;
Davan: texture and material and beneath-surface sensing) are
entirely different. The blur risk from the Ch. 17-19 voice audit
(Aldric and Davan both use the approach-resist-land architecture)
is not present here; the adjacent chapters use different POV
characters with meaningfully different sensory orientations.

**Verdict: The adversarial read passes.**

## Closing image

**Assessment: Firmly in Davan's voice. Rhymes correctly with prior
chapter closers without reproducing them.**

> "...he held it there past the warming — past the point where
> holding did anything — because the country under the camp was cold
> in a way that a palm could not warm, and the hawthorn in his hand
> was a small thing, and the smallness of it was what he had to give."
>
> "He did not finish the thought. He held the hawthorn piece against
> his ribs under the coat, and the wood went on taking the warmth
> out of him the way old stone took it out of a hand at a gate,
> slowly and politely, and after a while his hand was on the carved
> thing and the carved thing was against his heart and the country
> was under all of it and did not ask anything of him at all."

**Davan's voice:** The warmth-passage is carving-intelligence at its
most extended. He is applying the same attentiveness he applied to
the gate-wall stone to his own body's transaction with the carved
piece. "Past the point where holding did anything" — this is
Davan's characteristic mode: noticing the exact limit of an action's
efficacy, continuing past it anyway. The smallness is explicit
("the smallness of it was what he had to give") but not over-
articulated; the sentence earns its sentiment by being the chapter's
only full sentimental statement.

"He did not finish the thought" is the signature move: the half-
thought left open. The chapter's final image is not concluded — his
hand is on the carved thing, the carved thing is against his heart,
the country is under all of it, and then the chapter simply stops.
No resolution, no summary. Correct.

**Rhyme with prior closers:**

- Ch. 17 (Aldric): moons over the road. Ch. 24 has moons in the sky
  implied but not stated — the three-and-one count runs under the
  whole chapter. The closer does not reach for the moon image; it
  stays at ground level. Not a repetition.
- Ch. 19 (Davan): "three moons hung in a sky that had once held four."
  Ch. 24's closer deliberately does not name the moons. The chapter
  has been about what is *below* the sky, not above it. The buried
  thing in the hand, the country carrying its carried thing — this is
  the vertical axis inverted from Ch. 19's upward close. Rhymes
  structurally (both are Davan holding something small against a
  large truth), not in image.
- Ch. 23 (Maren): "warmer-than-yesterday was not a sentence she had
  finished yet." Ch. 24 ends with "He did not finish the thought."
  This is the adjacency risk — both chapters end on an explicitly
  unfinished statement. However, the two are doing different work.
  Maren's unfinished sentence is about emotional processing held in
  reserve. Davan's is structural — the chapter's carving-mode enacted
  one final time in the narrative act of stopping. The language is
  different enough that the parallelism reads as controlled echo
  rather than repetition. Acceptable; monitor if Ch. 25 repeats the
  form.

**The "palm could not warm" construction** is this chapter's
signature closing register. It is original to Ch. 24; it does not
borrow from any prior closer's imagery. **Strong close.**

## Verdict

Ch. 24 is the most accomplished Davan chapter in the draft sequence.
The voice signature is operative at high fidelity: circling-processing,
specific-noticing, half-thoughts unheld, skin-light as second narrator,
carving-hands intelligence. The chapter advances both the plot
(Aldric's undercroft disclosure, two faces confirmed on one blanket)
and Davan's empathic development (country-reading at geological scale)
without losing the observational groundedness that is his register.

**Filter count: 2 firm, 1 borderline.** Below the pre-revision Ch. 19
average (4); on par with post-revision Davan chapters. Clean.

**Tic count: 0 non-smile-mouth, 0 "particular [noun]", 0 "sit with
this", 0 "had the quality of."** Fully clean on all four non-
negotiables.

**Verb contamination: none.** "Catalogued" and "filed" absent.

**POV discipline: passes with one fix.** The Aldric-hesitation passage
("It was courtesy") is stated about Aldric's interior without being
marked as Davan's inference. One phrase of attribution corrects it.

**Aldric voice: the two-languages collision is audible and correctly
rendered through dialogue and behavior.** "Patient urns" lands. The
note about over-annotation ("He did not flinch at the comparison. He
used the word.") is yellow, not red.

**Empathic-country texture: sustained with one slip.** The
"was aware of" sentence plus the three negations approach naming what
should remain unnamed. Revise to the shorter "it worked now" pattern.

**Fix 1 (red-line): "He was aware of something that was not alive..."**
Replace the filter wrapper and the three negations with the proximity-
sense escalation pattern: "The proximity-sense had worked in the
cooperage. It had worked in Edvar's back room. It worked now. The
something was under his feet."

**Fix 2 (yellow): "His hesitation was not theological. It was
courtesy."** Mark as Davan's inference. "Davan read the hesitation as
courtesy, not theology." or "The hesitation had a different shape than
the theological ones had. Courtesy."

**Fix 3 (yellow): "He let the thought come without forcing it to a
shape. He circled it."** Cut. Begin the next beat directly at "Near
noon, he stopped at the verge..." The circling is structural; the
announcement is redundant.

**One item to monitor:** "registered" as Davan's proximity-sense verb.
Two uses across Ch. 19 and Ch. 24, developing into a tic. A third in
an Act 3 Davan chapter would confirm the pattern; vary if possible.

The closing image — hawthorn against his ribs, warmth past warming's
limit, country cold in a way that a palm could not warm — is fully
in Davan's voice, earned by the chapter's carving-intelligence logic,
and among the strongest closing images in the draft sequence.
