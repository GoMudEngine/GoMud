## Maren voice signature match

**Assessment: Green. The chapter is the cleanest Maren voice in the
manuscript run.**

The compressed-watchful register is operative from the first paragraph.
The exit-cataloguing reflex fires immediately and correctly:

> "She catalogued the room before she left it. Two beds, one stripped.
> Basin empty. A coin for the landlady flat on the table. Nothing in
> the crack between the boards. Nothing under the bed."

This is the Ch. 9 woodcutter's-shelter pattern — short declaratives, zero
editorial, the room inventoried as a checklist. The resource-mapping
continues at the boatyard ("one exit behind her... cart was on her left...
the disc was on her inside, and anyone wanting to reach her from either
side would have to cross something first") and mirrors the cooperage entry
in Ch. 20 precisely in register and method. Continuity clean.

Analytical self-observation is present and working:

> "The arithmetic was so automatic that she registered it only after she
> had already arranged herself to it."

This is the "caught herself in old habits" mode the bible specifies —
Maren noting her own reflex with the dry retrospective precision that is
her signature. Compare to Ch. 20's "she catalogued it automatically" and
Ch. 9's circuit-breaking self-observation. Consistent.

Short declarative sentences under stress: the departure sequence and the
Vane road-check exchanges are almost entirely short declaratives, with the
emotion carried in what is not said. "All right." / "Pack." / "Just telling
you." / "All right." These are Maren's register from inside. No drift.

Privately furious: not present as a surface note, but the chapter is
correctly calibrated for Day 1 of a journey east toward her father's
probable death. The fury is submerged into focus, which is correct for
this beat in her arc. Not a voice failure — a stage choice.

The father-thought management ("She caught the thought and set it aside.
Not away. Aside.") is one of the chapter's best voice moments: Maren
analytical about her own processing, naming the difference between
suppression and deferral with the precision the bible calls for.

**One drift zone:** The moon-watching passage in the final section
expands Maren's interiority into territory that is slightly more ruminative
than her baseline register. The observation "Avoidance was always a habit
eventually. Whether tonight she was breaking it or only putting it down for
a minute to rest her neck she did not know" reads as correctly in-voice —
it is a Maren-style epistemological hedge. However, the passage is the
chapter's longest sustained interior stretch, and one sentence pushes
toward narrator lyricism (see Closing Image section below).

## Filter words (with counts and citations)

**Count: 3 instances. Below the manuscript average for Maren chapters
(4.0 across Chs. 1-16; 5 in Ch. 20 before the v2 revision).**

1. **"She had noticed this and had assumed it was deference"** — para.
   beginning "Aldric fell in beside her in the last hour before dusk."
   "Had noticed" is a filter on a direct observation. The sentence would
   be stronger as: "He had not mounted the bay all day. Deference — a
   Keeper of thirty years did not enter a group of four people who were
   walking by mounting his horse to the head of the line." The filter
   can be removed; the inference is already doing the work.

2. **"She caught the thought and set it aside"** — wayside trough section.
   Borderline: "caught" is a filter on an internal event, but "set it
   aside. Not away. Aside." is characteristic Maren analytical grammar
   and the construction earns its slight filter overhead. Low priority.
   Flag but do not require revision.

3. **"Maren could see it in the motion of the cuff-fabric"** — fold scene,
   Vane's tremor. "Maren could see it" is a filter on a detail that could
   arrive directly: "the cuff-fabric moved fine and fast at the wrists
   above the fire." Low priority — in-voice for Maren's observational
   distance from Vane — but flaggable.

**Ch. 23 filter count: 1 firm (had noticed), 2 low/borderline.**

Notably absent: no "she felt," no "she realized," no "she understood,"
no "she thought about" cluster. The Ch. 20 anaphoric filter problem is
not reproduced. The disc-pulse interiority ("Two pulses today. First was
in the dark before the bell. This is the second.") is rendered in
italicized direct thought, which is exactly the filter-avoidant technique
the Ch. 20 revision introduced. That technique is now being applied
correctly here.

### "The particular [noun]"

**Count: 1 instance.**

> "a slightly-too-deliberate pace, a body not trusting itself to move on
> instinct and putting a measure on the movement instead"

This is not the template — it is a compound adjective construction, not
"the particular [noun]." Scanning carefully: no instance of "the
particular [adjective noun]" appears in Ch. 23. **Clean on the primary
template.**

Adjacent construction present once: "the economy of the motion told

### "Had the quality of"

**Count: 2 instances.**

1. "Edvar's face had the settled look of a man who had argued the question
   and closed it" — the "[adjective] look of a man who had [past
   participle]" construction. This is the "had the quality of" template
   under a one-word variation ("look" not "quality"). The prose audit
   counts 15+ of this construction across Chs. 1-16 and flags it as
   the author's preferred secondary-characterization mode.

2. "a man who had not chosen plain clothes for thirty years, moving in
   them with the small stiffness of a body learning to occupy itself
   without its professional scaffolding" — not the template; this is
   specific physical observation with an inference attached. Clean.

One confirmed, one borderline. The Edvar instance is the cleaner use of
the two (it is doing real work — it settles the question of whether
he regrets coming, without stating it). Given Ch. 21 showed three
instances, one in Ch. 23 is acceptable frequency. **Yellow, not red.**

## POV discipline (inference vs. omniscience on Vane/Aldric/Davan)

### Vane

**Assessment: Near-clean. One passage on the line; no outright
omniscience breach.**

Vane's withdrawal is rendered through:
- Body: "Vane was repacking a small thing for the third or fourth time.
  She would do it again." — behavioral observation, no interior access.
- Motion: "a slightly-too-deliberate pace, a body not trusting itself
  to move on instinct" — Maren inferring from gait, not stating Vane's
  experience.
- Physical tell at fire: "her hands inside her cuffs were shaking finely
  at the fingertips" — external; the cuff-fabric carries it.

**The "the company mattered. The alone mattered more" passage is the
chapter's most significant POV discipline question:**

> "A body working through something alone in company. The company
> mattered. The alone mattered more."

These two sentences are narrated without attribution to Maren's
inference. They are framed through Maren's observation of a parallel
she recognizes ("She had watched her mother work through things like
this"), which grounds the inference in Maren's experience. However, "the
alone mattered more" is stated as fact about Vane's interior without
being marked as Maren's reading of the signs. Compare to the cleaner
handling elsewhere: the shoulder-ache passage correctly ends with Maren
logging the data rather than stating Vane's motive.

**Recommended fix:** One phrase to mark this as inference rather than
access. "The company mattered, from everything Maren had seen of people
like this. The alone mattered more." Or cut the second sentence; the
observation of Maren adding wood to the fire without approaching is
already doing the work.

The "telling-you was a kindness. Vane did not talk about her body
unprompted, ever" passage is marginally stronger — Maren is reading
behavioral pattern from accumulated knowledge of Vane, which is
legitimate POV inference. Acceptable, but this is also where the author
is doing the most explanatory work on Vane's behalf. The explanation
slightly undercuts what the behavior was already demonstrating. Consider
cutting the explicit gloss and trusting the scene.

### Davan

**Assessment: Clean, with one notable technique.**

Davan's skin-color shift (blue-through-green) is handled well:
"a wash of blue-through-green that held and then settled to the low

The second skin-color moment (blue to green in response to the disc

> "Davan had felt the pulse as a weather he did not have a name for.

The phrase "Davan had felt the pulse as a weather he did not have a

presented as Maren's analysis from accumulated knowledge, not as narrator
omniscience. The construction is defensible; the skin-color is the
evidence, and the inference is marked as Maren's. On the line but passes.

The "She was not meant to see it. She had seen it anyway" sequence is
clean — Maren stating her own observational act, not Davan's intention.

## Aldric's "I have been counting" arrival sentence

**Assessment: Voice lands correctly.**

"I have been counting" as an opening sentence is formally constructed —
Aldric's present-perfect formulation, the kind of sentence that opens
a clause rather than a statement. Maren reads it as such: "formally,
with the first word doing more work than most people asked of a first
word."

The liturgical-scholarship conflict is not explicit in the sentence
itself (this is Maren's POV, not Aldric's; the conflict would be audible
in Aldric's own chapter), but it arrives through Maren's reading of his
cadence and the deliberate formality of his approach — walking his horse
to her pace before speaking, the two-hundred-yard silence before the
first word. This is a Keeper's approach pattern, not a scholar's or a
traveler's. The sentence lands in Aldric's register as Maren perceives
it.

The follow-on: "My counting has been a professional occupation of mine
for two months. I have been counting what my office taught me was not
countable." This is good: "my office" carries the institutional register
without requiring Maren to interpret Aldric's theological interior. The
formality is audible in Aldric's vocabulary even through Maren's ears.

## Davan's skin-light color shift (blue to green)

**Assessment: Clean visual rendering; one inference to watch.**

The shift is rendered visually and with calibrated restraint: "a wash
of blue-through-green that held and then settled to the low steady blue"
(boatyard) and "from the low steady blue to a low steady green" (disc
pulse response). Both are external description.

Maren does not interpret the green emotionally. She does not name what
it means to Davan. She files it: "The seeing went aside, with the thought
of her father, for later." This is Maren's cataloguing mode applied to
emotional data she does not have the framework to process yet. Correct.

Per the bible, blue=calm, green-gold=recognition/happiness. The chapter
shows "low steady green" — not full green-gold, but a movement toward it.
The reader who has read Ch. 19 will track this; Maren does not interpret
it. The restraint is the correct craft choice.

## POV discipline — Vane's private withdrawal

**Assessment: Pass with one fix recommended.**

The physical signs Maren reads: repacking behavior, deliberate-paced walk,
thumb on seam, right hand not entering pocket, proximity to fire,
fine tremor in cuff-fabric, jaw set. These are all external and
legitimate observational targets.

What Maren infers: that the shoulder-ache disclosure is a pre-log for
future reference, that Vane is claiming the second watch as a performance
of capability, that Vane's body is running "a small internal current."
These inferences are marked as Maren's and grounded in her knowledge of
Vane's behavior patterns. They pass.

What Maren does NOT do: name Vane's feelings, enter Vane's experience
of the withdrawal, explain the mechanism to the reader. The withdrawal
is shown through behavior only and Maren's reading of behavior.

**Recommended fix (as noted under POV discipline above):** The "the alone
mattered more" clause is the single place where the narrator is speaking
over Maren's shoulder about Vane's interiority without flagging it as
inference. One phrase anchors it.

## Closing image integrity

**"warmer-than-yesterday was not a sentence she had finished yet"**

**Assessment: Mostly in-register. One risk zone.**

The closing passage running from "She looked at them without counting"
through "She slept" is the chapter's most ambitious stretch of interior
prose and, with one exception, stays inside Maren's head.

The compressed, unresolved register is honored:
- "not a sentence she had finished yet" — Maren applying her dry
  analytical mode to her own emotional-epistemic state. This is the
  woodcutter's-shelter voice applied to Act 3 stakes. It lands.
- "All right," she thought. Not said. The two words to herself, the
  private register. / Not all right. Arranged. Put down." — This is
  the correct deployment of Maren's "All right" register, anchored
  firmly in self-awareness. "Not all right. Arranged. Put down." is
  the chapter's best single moment: compressed, analytical, honest
  about the gap between the word and what it holds. Canon.
- "the body was hers, and she did not get to hurry it" — in-voice.
  Maren's dry ownership of her own process.

**One drift into narrator lyricism:**

> "a country that did not know what they were"

This phrase concludes the moon-observation passage: "Three. At their
stations. Doing whatever they had been doing for ten thousand years
above a country that did not know what they were."

The phrase "that did not know what they were" contains a claim Maren
cannot make yet. She does not know what the moons are. She knows Aldric
has been counting them. She knows something the cooperage group believes
about pre-crash material. But "what they were" — ships at station,
orbital infrastructure — is the book's central secret, and Maren does
not hold that knowledge at this point in the narrative.

This is a subtle narrator-whisper: the phrase is structured so that it
sounds like Maren's thought but is actually the narrator marking the
irony of her ignorance for the reader. Compare to the Ch. 22 red-line
item in the voice-validation-ch21-22 report: "the three moons were over
the harbor outside, at their stations, doing what they had always done,
which — she did not know yet and would not know until the hill..." Same
structural intrusion, lighter version.

**Recommended fix:** "Three. At their stations. Doing whatever it was
moons did, above a country that had stopped asking." Maren can register
the moons as purposeful-seeming without the narrator confirming the
content of that purpose. "A country that had stopped asking" is also
more Maren in tone — dryer, less grand — than "a country that did not
know what they were."

The phrase as written does not break the chapter the way the Ch. 22
red-line item broke that chapter, because it is brief and immediately
followed by Maren returning to ground ("She looked at them without
counting"). But it is the chapter's one moment of narrator intrusion at
a symbolically loaded beat, which is exactly where the intrusion hurts
most.

## Verdict

**Green with two targeted fixes.**

Ch. 23 is the cleanest Maren-POV chapter in the manuscript run —
including Ch. 9, which held the previous high-water mark. Filter count
is 1 firm / 2 low-borderline, below every previous Maren chapter.
No non-smile-mouth tic. No "particular [noun]" instances. No "sit with
this." Zero verb contamination. The cataloguing reflex, "All right"
deployment, and analytical-self-observation mode are all operating at
canon specification. The disc-pulse interiority rendered in italicized
direct thought (not "she felt / she thought about") is the v2 revision
technique applied correctly for the first time in a Maren chapter.

**Fix 1 (red-line): "a country that did not know what they were."**
The narrator is speaking over Maren's shoulder about the moons' identity,
which Maren does not yet know. Revise to stay inside Maren's ignorance.
Suggested replacement above.

**Fix 2 (yellow): "The alone mattered more."** Stated about Vane's
interior without being flagged as Maren's inference. One phrase of
attribution ("from everything Maren had seen of people in this shape")
or cut the second sentence.

**One item to monitor in future chapters:** "filed" as Maren's
cognitive verb. Used once here; it is Vane's more heavily established
form. A second use in Ch. 24 or beyond would begin to read as
contamination.

The closing image ("warmer-than-yesterday was not a sentence she had
finished yet" / "Not all right. Arranged. Put down.") is fully in Maren's
register and is among the strongest closing-image prose in the
manuscript. The chapter earns it.
