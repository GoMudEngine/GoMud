## 1. Logline & Premise

Ten thousand years ago a four-ship colony mission reached Gaius. The
mission profile — as with any real colonization plan — assigned three
ships to stable orbit (as communications, survey, and support
infrastructure) and the fourth to descend with the colonists and
establish the ground colony. The three reached station. The lander's
descent failed; the fourth ship crashed in the eastern foothills. The
survivors encountered the Chrysalis — a native Gaian organism that
infected most of them, producing heritable mutations. A genetically
immune minority (the bloodline) kept a monopoly on pre-crash technology
and built a religion around their immunity. Their descendants have spent
ten millennia hunting any uninfected person born outside their line,
because an "unblooded" person with a crew ID chip is a key they cannot
control.

Maren is that person. Her father's disc is the key. The three moons are
the orbital infrastructure, still on station, still crewed by long-dead
descendants running mission protocols. Nobody alive on Gaius knows this
— the truth has been managed for ten thousand years, partly by
institutional suppression, partly by the simple erosion of memory.

Central dramatic question: what happens when the map turns out to be wrong
enough to send you off a cliff?

## 3. World & Setting

### Cosmology (The Truth) — Option C mission-profile framing (LOCKED)
The three visible moons are the colony mission's **orbital infrastructure**
— three ships that reached their planned stations ten thousand years ago
and have been holding them ever since. They are not "ships that happened
to make it." They are three-quarters of a designed system: communications,
survey, support. They are still crewed by long-dead descendants of those
who stayed on station per mission protocol. They are not merely *waiting*
— they are *maintaining position*, expecting contact from the lander or
colony below.

The fourth ship was always the **lander**: the planned descent vehicle
carrying the colonists and everything meant to be brought to the surface
(including the genetic archive — see Section 5 / four Confluence
containers). The descent failed. The fourth ship crashed in the eastern
foothills. Everything on Gaius now — every human, every container of
salvaged tech, the Chrysalis-infected population, the bloodline, the
theology — descends from that failed landing. The ships in orbit have
been awaiting confirmation that did not arrive.

The crew ID chip (Maren's disc) was designed to be recognized by the
ship's systems — not by bloodline genetics. When Maren presses it into
the surface recess at the crash site (Ch. 27), it sends the signal.
Under Option C framing, the signal does not merely alert the orbital
ships — it **re-establishes contact with mission infrastructure that
has been at its post the entire time**. Climactically richer: the three
moons have been *doing their jobs* for ten thousand years while the
humans below forgot what the jobs were.

Implications for drafters:
- The counting rhyme "the moons are waiting" is literally true but
  slightly reframed — they are waiting *at their stations*, not "waiting
  to come home."
- The symbol (Inner Orbit Symbol, Section 5) is the mission-profile
  diagram, pre-existing on all pre-crash tech. Accurate both before and
  after the crash — the three arcs are the orbital stations, the fourth
  is the descent trajectory.
- When Maren authenticates at the hill, the orbital ships' response can
  be framed as resumption-of-protocol rather than rescue. Gives the
  ending a different emotional color: infrastructure recognizing its
  long-lost ground team, not saviors arriving.

### Cosmology (What People Believe)
The Chrysalis is part of Gaian spiritual theology. The mutations are "the
gift." The bloodline are sacred servants with unique capacity to operate
Founding-era technology. The Restricted Collection contains material that
contradicts this. No one outside the bloodline/church knows the ships are
ships. The children's rhyme ("four fires in the sky, one came down to die")
preserves the truth as legend.

## 4. The Chrysalis / Bloom / Hollow System

### The Chrysalis
Native to Gaius. Entered colonists after landing through soil, air, water.
The weak died. The rest changed. Changes compound across generations into
extraordinary mutations. The organism also responds to belief and
self-concept — what you believe you are shapes what the Chrysalis makes you.
This is why the theology has partial truth: the story and the organism
reinforced each other over ten millennia.

Brennan's sophisticated-but-wrong scholarly conclusion (Ch. 13): the fourth
ship's people spread the Chrysalis. Inverts true causation (world entered
people, not people brought organism). Correction is Act Three revelation.

### Hollow / Uninfected
People born genetically immune outside the bloodline. Called "hollow" as

### The Four Smooth Containers (Confluence Chamber) — RESOLVED
- **Configuration**: 1 message container + 3 genetic-archive containers.
- **The message container**: Holds a recorded transmission from the fourth
  ship's crew (pre-crash). Becomes playable when Maren's disc authenticates
  against the ship's systems (Ch. 27+). The message plays at the climax —
  a voice from 10,000 years ago. This is the emotional payoff of the
  entire book. Drafters: do not over-write what the voice says; restraint
  lands harder than exposition here.
- **The three genetic-archive containers**: Hold preserved pre-Chrysalis
  human genetic material — the *original template* of humanity before Gaius
  rewrote it. Exact form (gametes, embryos, tissue) is drafter-discretionary;
  the important thing is that it is *unchanged humanity*.
- **Unopenability mechanism (LOCKED)**: The Chrysalis present in all
  currently-living humans is biologically incompatible with opening these
  containers safely. Opening them would require first undoing the
  Chrysalis — either from humanity or from the specific people attempting
  to open them. This collapses "should we open them" into "should we
  un-make ourselves." The mechanism is in-world consistent: even the
  ship's systems cannot bypass it, because the bio-lock was designed
  before Gaian contact and presumes uncontaminated human biology.
- **When the reader learns this**: Ch. 29, through Aldric reading the
  ship's records. By the final chapter, the characters and reader both
  know what's in the containers and why they can't be opened. The closing
  chapter is about the *choice*, not the *discovery*.
- **The closing image (LOCKED)**: The four POVs disagree on what to do.
  The book does not resolve the question — it ends with the disagreement
  held open, the containers still sealed, the species-level choice
  deferred to whoever comes next. Drafters: resist the temptation to let
  one character "win" the argument. Each POV brings their arc's weight
  to the position they hold; the disagreement should feel earned by the
  distinct journeys they've taken.
- **Thematic implications for Act 3 drafters**:

### Inner Orbit Symbol
- **Physical description (LOCKED, Ch. 9 manuscript):** four nested arcs
  with raised points along their paths; the fourth arc drifts off-true,
  breaking the nested-arc pattern at a tangent.
- **What it actually depicts (LOCKED per Option C cosmology):** the
  mission-profile diagram. Three arcs = the three orbital stations (the
  raised points = station positions on their respective orbits). Fourth
  arc = the lander's **descent trajectory**. The symbol is a schematic
  of how the colony was *planned* to work, not a narrative about the
  crash. It was engraved on pre-crash technology at manufacture and is
  identical on every surface that carries it — Maren's disc, the Temple
  of Confluence door, the crash-site hillside recess, Davan's obsessive
  carvings.
- **Ironic re-reading**: because the lander crashed, the fourth arc
  drifting off-true reads to modern eyes as "the one that came down" —
  a prophecy by coincidence. Davan (Ch. 6) is the first character in
  the book whose empathic sense reads the symbol accurately as orbital
  rather than spiral. Aldric (Ch. 24) counts what it describes.
- **Appearances:**
  - Maren's disc edge (original).
  - Above the Temple of Confluence door (likely salvaged and
    repositioned after the crash, but the symbol itself is pre-crash).
  - Davan's carvings (Ch. 6 onward) — his hands render the pre-crash
    shape before he can name it.
  - Crash site hillside recess — original surface of the lander's
    hull, marked at manufacture (Ch. 17, Reth).
  - On the disc: recognizable by Ch. 9 when Maren examines it closely.
- **Do NOT draft** any scene where a character discovers a "modified"
  or "retrofitted" version of the symbol. The symbol is singular. The
  crash did not change it; only the *meaning people assign to it* has
  shifted over ten millennia.

## 8. Style Notes (Author Voice)

*Populated from full manuscript read (Chs. 1-16) and draft chapters (17-20).*

### POV Voice Signatures (Manuscript-Confirmed)

**Maren**: Compressed, watchful, dry edge. Privately furious. Catalogues
exits and resources before people. Analytical about her own performance
("She had been good at this once. She was not being good at it now").
Catches herself in old habits (the coal pouch, the automatic scan for
exits, the rehearsed story). Short declarative sentences under stress.
Key tell: when she says "All right" (two words, Ch. 18 response to Vane),
it means something has settled at depth, not that she agrees or is fine.

**Davan**: Open-grained, observational. Processes by circling — approaches
a thing, leaves it, comes back with a slightly different angle. Connects
to people and places through noticing specific things (a smell, a shadow,
how someone holds their shoulders). Half-thoughts he doesn't force to
conclusion. When he does land on something ("It came down" — Ch. 19 to
Edvar), it lands hard because he's been orbiting it. Skin-light is a
second narrator visible to readers but not to him.

**Aldric**: Formal precision masking parallel inner life. Long careful
clauses with institutional vocabulary, followed by the simpler sentence
that is what he actually thinks. Self-aware about his own lying —
the narrative can feel him noting the lie even while executing it.
Feedback (FB-6) notes his voice blurs with Vane's "analytical/detached"
register; fix: lean more heavily into liturgical and theological language
in his internal monologue. "A man of the cloth realizing his god is a
shipwreck" — that guilt and contrast should be audible in his word choice.

**Vane**: Clipped noir register. Short sentences, declarative. World as
cost/yield/information. Interiority rationed near-zero — what leaks
through is in action and attention-allocation, not in stated feeling.
Professional categories keep things clean, or used to. Ch. 18 is notably
compressed even by her standards — shorter paragraphs, starker transitions
— whether this is intentional voice-under-pressure or draft compression
should be confirmed by author.

### Global Style Rules
- **"Careful" overused** across all POVs — flag for pass
- **"Not quite a smile" / "corner of Vane's mouth"** — used 3+ times in
  Chs. 10 and 14; flag for pass
- **Filter words** (FB-7): "She felt," "He noticed," "She realized" —
  remove and put the reader directly in the sensation. Standard fiction-
  workshop note; needs a systematic line-edit pass.
- **No Earth-specific language**: "the burning point at center," "the star
  of this system," not "the sun" used without grounding. "Solar" — avoid.
- **The face**: always "the face" — never "the panel," "the screen," or
  "the device"
- **The disc**: "the disc" — never "the chip" or "the token"
- **Inner orbit symbol**: appears on disc edge, above Confluence door,
  in Davan's carvings, on the crash site hillside surface. Should feel
  like a thread the reader tracks across POVs.

### Structural Patterns (Observed in Manuscript)
- Chapter endings tend to cut at the moment of commitment or
  incompletion — not resolution. Ch. 16 ends with the carving; Ch. 20
  ends with "The test had not yet begun." This is intentional.
- Multi-POV prologue (8 accounts of the First Coming) establishes the
  pattern: the same event looks different from each vantage, and none
  of the accounts are completely right.
- Chapters often begin mid-action or mid-observation, not at a scene
  opening beat.
- Dialogue is sparse and functional. Characters say less than they know.
  Silence and what is not said is load-bearing.
