# Chapter 28 — What Was Inside

## Chapter Twenty-Eight — *Davan*
### What Was Inside

---

The hillside was doing something.

He stood three paces up-slope of Maren's knee and did not move. His
hand had gone to the side of his coat without his hand asking him,
and his hand was flat against his ribs the way a hand went flat
against something inside a coat that wanted to get out. He did not
name the thing inside his ribs. He kept his hand where his hand was.

Under her thumb the small symbol on the back of the disc was lit in
the warmer color. The hillside was lit where the four arcs lay, in
the same warmer color but very faint, like coals under ash from a
night fire that had been banked for a long time and was, now, being
asked a question about whether it was still a fire.

The coals were saying yes.

Not in a rush. A slow spread along the arcs — not brighter exactly,
but *more present*, the arcs becoming something the hill was
instead of something in the hill — and where the arcs ran under the
overgrowth the overgrowth was not burning, was not dying, was only
*moving aside*. Vines the thickness of a wrist went slack at their
anchor-points, slid a hand's-breadth, re-seated. A clump of
bracken that had been wedged between two lines in the surface for
who knew how long sagged sideways and lay flat. A low thorn
branch that had grown in the crook of the easternmost arc for long
enough to have the arc in its grain came away at the root, not
torn, not pushed — *released*, the way a thing was released when
the surface under it decided it did not need to hold that thing
there any longer.

He watched it and his mouth was a little open.

Across a pace of hillside the growth was quietly unbuilding itself.

He thought about the way a tide went out. He thought about the way
a shore appeared along the length of a beach, not by the water
leaving but by the sand admitting that the sand had always been
under the water. The hillside was doing that. What was under the
growth had always been under the growth, and the growth was, with a
courtesy he did not know how to place, stepping aside.

Aldric was behind him. He had not heard Aldric come up. The older
man's breathing was small and even and did not draw attention.
Davan kept his eye on the hill.

---

The breach opened.

Not a door. There was no door. There was an unmaking of what had,
for a long time, been hillside, along a seam that had always been
the seam of the breach — the original wound the crash had cut into
the hull, over-grown and sealed by ten thousand seasons of the
country working on it. The growth retreated the length of a stride
on either side of the seam. A darkness showed in the slope between
two curving edges of the smooth material, narrow at the bottom and
widening at about chest-height and narrowing again at the top — a
passage shaped — the shape arrived to him without his naming it —
like a place where a thing had once been torn and where the growth had, in
the ten thousand seasons of its patience, filled the tear. What had
been a sealed wound was being asked to remember that it was a wound,
and was remembering.

The tear stood open.

Cool air came out of it.

Not cold. He had known cold on this hillside all morning — wind off
the dew-wet slope, the cold of a stone you put your palm on in
autumn. This was not that. This was the air of a closed room — no
weather in it, no wind in it, the kind of cool that sat in a vault
or a cellar or the back of a long unvisited barn. A held air. The
air of an indoors that had been indoors since before anyone in this
country had a word for indoors.

The cool air met his face and did not travel past his collar. The
way the stone at the gate-wall had handed warmth down into the
earth — that was old cold. This was inside cold. It was not trying
to do anything to him. It was only being what it had been being,
for a very long time, behind the hill.

He inhaled.

The air smelled of nothing. Not dust. Not stone. Not the wet green
of the outside hillside. A clean absence of smell that was, he
found, its own smell — the way a room smelled when no one had
cooked or coughed or wept in it for longer than any person
remembered rooms.

Aldric, at his elbow, said a word. He did not catch the word. He
thought it might have been a name.

---

Maren was still on her knee.

She did not lift her thumb from the symbol. Her head had not
turned. Her hair had come loose at the left temple in the breeze
she had climbed through earlier and the loose hair was against her
cheek and she did not tuck it back. Her eyes were on the disc.

The disc was lit warmer-leaf-through-sunlight under her thumb,
holding steady.

He understood — and understood was the wrong word; his body knew —
that the disc's stillness and the hillside's opening were the same
motion. The disc had stopped humming because the hillside had taken
up the hum. The signal was going somewhere past the hill. His body
read it as an outgoing direction, the way you could tell from the
set of a person's shoulders whether they were speaking to someone
in front of them or over their shoulder. The hill was speaking over
its shoulder, upward, at something far enough away that it took the
full register of the hill to say anything at all.

A resonance came up through his boot-soles.

Slow. Not the bone-sound Maren was carrying under her knee. A thing
above that, a long low note that was not a note, a carrying. The
rock underfoot was carrying a sending, the way the exposed slab by
the verge yesterday had carried the older register under his palm —
only that had been a whisper and this was a word.

The word was being said very quietly.

The word was being said very far.

He did not try to hear it. He was not the one it was being said to.
He was only in the room where the saying was being done.

---

"Davan."

Maren's voice. Low. She had not turned.

"Here."

"Go in."

He looked at her. Her eyes had stayed on the disc. Her thumb had
not moved. The set of her shoulder told him she had decided
something before she had spoken, and the decision was that his
being here at her knee was not what this moment was for. His being
inside was what this moment was for.

"Maren —"

"I have this," she said. "You have that."

His body said yes before his mouth did. He nodded. She did not see
the nod. She was busy holding a hillside open for him.

"Aldric comes," he said.

"Yes."

"Vane outside."

"Yes."

"Edvar at the cart."

"Yes."

The exchange was the exchange they had built on the road — small,
functional, each word a placed stone. He did not cross to her. She
did not need crossing-to. He turned and looked at Aldric, who was
already looking at the breach, and the two of them stepped forward
together.

His hand had come off his ribs. The coming-off had done itself
without him.

---

He went in first.

Aldric was half a pace behind him and did not argue. The passage
was narrow enough that a man had to turn his shoulder slightly to
enter and broad enough that he did not have to duck. The curving
edges where growth had been were smooth in the way the outer
hillside was smooth — the disc-smooth at the scale of a room,
cool-that-did-not-travel, not stone, felt like stone but not. He
did not touch them with his hands. His body did not want to touch
them yet. His body wanted to learn what the air was, first.

The air inside was the air that had come out, only more of it.

Three steps in, the outside sound stopped. Not faded. Stopped. Wind
had gone. The small creak of Edvar's cart-harness down-slope was
gone. The small river-talk far at the valley floor was gone. The
world had closed behind him, not as a trap but as a completion — a
space that was its own space now, whole inside its own boundary.

His breath arrived at the front of his ears.

Aldric's breath, a pace behind, came quicker than yesterday —
Aldric was not in his body at this moment the way the Aldric at
the outcrop yesterday had been in his body, steady; this Aldric
was in the body of a man approaching the thing he had travelled
thirty years to approach, and the body was doing what bodies did
when they arrived somewhere they had only thought about.

He did not turn to look at Aldric. He kept walking.

The floor had a give that was not the give of earth. A floor. It
had not been earth under his boot since two steps in. The change
had passed under his boot-heel and he had not marked it and now,
attending, he could feel the material through his sole — the same
cool density he had felt in Corvel's container at the cooperage
table, in Edvar's face the night of the green-gold, in the
hillside under Maren's knee. Floor of it. Walls of it. He was
inside a room made of the material the debris field had been
scattering pieces of across sixty miles of country for long enough
that no song still knew what the pieces were.

A piece of his mind, far back, said: *all of one thing*.

He did not chase the thought. He circled it and let it settle and
walked on.

---

The passage opened.

Not into a large room. Into a space the size of a winter byre —
low-ceilinged, roughly oblong, the air of it cool and held. The
walls were the dark material. The floor was the dark material.
There was light, and the light was coming from nowhere he could
name — not lamps, not windows. The light was in the walls in a way
he could not parse and did not try to. It was the cold-precise
light of the face, scaled up — light that had been *made* to the
specification of seeing-by, not the specification of warming.

The banks came to him first.

Along the two long walls, at about hip-height, ran low shelves.
Not shelves like the ones in Edvar's shop, made of wood and set
with pegs. Shelves as *part* of the wall — the wall deepened into
shelf at that register, the way stone in a country chapel
sometimes had a recess carved into it for a lamp. The shelves ran
the length of the room on both sides. On the shelves, row upon
row, sat objects.

He did not know the word for what they were.

Small. A man could have held one in two hands without strain. Flat
on one side and curved on the other, like a river-stone that had
been shaped by a hand that cared about its shape. Dark, the same
dark as the walls. A dozen and a dozen and a dozen of them, going
down the length of both walls in a pattern so regular that the
pattern was the first thing he read — not a row, a *system*. Each
object at the same distance from the next. Each at the same height.
Each oriented the same way.

He stopped at the first shelf and did not touch.

Aldric, a pace behind him, made a sound. It was not a word. It was
the sound a man made when a sentence he had been holding all his
life had just been shown the object it was about.

"Davan."

"Yes."

"Do you see."

"Yes."

They stood at the end of the first shelf and looked at the hundred
quiet objects on it, and the hundred quiet objects on the wall
opposite. Davan did not count. Counting was Aldric's work. His own
work was something else and he did not know it yet.

His shoulders opened.

He had not known his shoulders had been closed, on the road and at
the recess and in the passage. They had been. They opened now, by a
small margin, the way shoulders opened when a body discovered that
the room it had walked into contained nothing that wanted anything
from the body. The room did not want him. The room did not want
anyone. The room had been doing the work it had been set to do,
and the work it had been set to do was not a work that required
anyone.

This was, he understood slowly and without saying it, not a weapon
room. It was not a treasure room. It was not the room any song
about this hill had taught anyone to imagine.

It was a room full of kept things.

---

He walked to the nearest shelf.

He went slowly. Not out of fear. Out of a courtesy — the courtesy a
body found when it came into a room where a great deal of
attending had been done and the attending had been done by no one
for a very long time. He walked as he might have walked through a
chapel in a village where the chapel was not his, where the people
who had built it were under the earth, where the only thing left
of their work was the work itself and the work deserved not to be
walked on carelessly.

He stopped a hand's-breadth from the first object on the shelf.

He did not pick it up. He had no right to pick it up yet. Maybe he
never would. The picking up was Aldric's hand's work when Aldric's
hand arrived at it, and the hand had not.

He put his palm at the level of the object, close but not touching,
and he let his proximity-sense do its work.

The sense came up from his palm and from the underside of his
forearm and met the object. Not a heat. Not a cold. A *presence* —
the same presence he had read off the face in Edvar's back room,
off Corvel's container at the cooperage table, off the slab at the
verge yesterday, off the hillside under Maren's thumb. The same
quality. Only *different in kind*. The face had been an instrument.
The container had been sealed. The hill was an opening-outward.
This —

This was holding.

The object was holding something the way a barn held grain, or a
jar held seed, or a cellar held a winter's worth of roots.
Holding was what it was for. Whatever was in it had been put in it
to be there, and the putting had been done with the whole intent
of the being-there — the object was not a box that had happened to
be used for storage, it was a *storage*, made for storage, the
shape of its being was to hold.

His palm came lower. Not touching. The courtesy held.

Behind the holding was the quality he had been reading all morning
and did not have a name for. It came up at him from the whole
shelf — every object on the shelf at once, braided together so that
no single object was the source — a register his body read and
could not place.

It was not loneliness.

He tried the word and the word did not fit. The objects were not
lonely. Loneliness was a thing bodies felt when they were built
for company and were not in company. These objects had never been
built for company. They had been built for *being there*. The
difference was a whole language apart.

It was not patience, exactly, though patience was close. Patience
was what a person did when they were waiting and knew they were
waiting. The objects did not know they were waiting. The objects
were only *correct*, the way a well-set clock was correct in the
tick it was giving — not proud of the tick, not humble about the
tick, simply doing the tick — only without the clock's conceit of a
person checking it.

*Attending without attender*, a piece of his mind said.

He did not push the thought. He set it down at the back of the
shelf of his mind where the unfinished sentences lived.

The register pressed gently up at him and he let it press. He stood
in the full weight of it. His breath went out.

His skin began to light.

---

He did not see it. He had never seen his own skin-light the way
other people saw it. He read it by second-hand — the way a room's
walls took his color when he was lit, the way Maren sometimes
nodded at his collar, the way Lera on the barge had named her
colors for him. He knew it by how the shelf-material in front of
him changed tone under his palm.

The shelf-material changed tone.

The dark, at his palm-range, took on a warmth. Not the green-gold
of Edvar's back room. Not the held green of yesterday's road. Not
the blue of the shelter at Stillwater the disc had given Maren.
Something he did not have a word for.

It was, if he had to reach for the word — the color of honey held
up to a lamp, only cooler. The color of the edge of dawn a morning
nobody had seen, a morning the country was having for the first
time.

It was not green. It was not amber. It was not gold.

It was a color his skin had not produced before.

It was in him, at his wrists and the hollow of his throat and the
underside of his jaw, and he knew it was there because the walls
of the storage room knew it was there — the dark material held the
color for him, gave it back at his palm-range, carried it a hand's-
breadth into the material and then held it, *keeping* it, because
*keeping* was what the material did.

He did not pull his hand back. He did not step away.

He held where he was, and the storage room held where it was, and
for a beat the two of them were at the same register, lit in a
color that was neither fully his nor fully the material's but
something the two of them were making by standing this close to
one another.

Behind him, Aldric made a small sound.

---

"Davan."

Aldric's voice. Careful. Low.

"Here."

"Your skin."

"I know."

"I have not seen that color."

"No."

A pause. The pause was not awkward. The pause was the pause of a
man reading a new word on a page in a language he knew, taking the
moment the new word required, putting the word into the place in
his understanding that had been made for it.

"All right," Aldric said. The word was not Maren's word. In
Aldric's mouth it was a different instrument — an acknowledgment
of the page-edge, not a settling.

Davan lowered his palm to his thigh without turning. He took a
step back from the shelf. The color at his wrist did not fade. It
held, steady, at a register lower than the peak it had reached
while his palm was at the material, and the register it held at was
a register he had not held at before. The new register lived in
the tendons of his forearm. The skin there was, in the best-set
way a body could be, *working*.

Aldric crossed behind him to the long wall.

Davan did not watch. He listened. Aldric's steps were slow and
even and stopped at a point on the opposite shelf. A breath went
out of the older man that was the breath of a man lowering himself
to a bench. He was crouching. Davan heard the small sound of
knees, then the slower sound of hands — placed on thighs, palms
down, the gesture of a scholar sitting before a page he had not
yet earned the right to turn.

Aldric said one word.

He said it in the liturgical register.

Davan did not catch it. It was, he thought, the word for a place
in a liturgy — a fixed point, the point at which a reader laid
hands on the page before beginning — and Aldric had said it
because Aldric's body had reached the part of the room where that
word was the word his body had. He had not chosen the word. The
word had arrived because the room had asked for it.

Davan turned, slowly, at the shoulder.

Aldric was on both knees. His hands were flat on the tops of his
thighs. His back was straight. He was looking at the objects on
the shelf in front of him with the attention of a man whose whole
life had been instrument-building for this exact length of
looking. His lips moved once. Nothing came out. He closed his
mouth.

He was not reading. He was *seeing what was to be read*.

Davan recognized the quality in him. He had seen it in Edvar, in
the back room, across the lit face. He had seen it in himself. It
was the quality of a man at a threshold who knew that crossing
would require a different instrument than the one he had used to
get to the threshold, and who was, for a last held moment, being
the version of himself that had walked to the threshold, before
becoming the version of himself that would cross.

Aldric did not cross. Not yet.

Davan did not ask him to.

---

Behind them, the passage was open. The cool air was still moving
from the room outward and downward along the hillside. Davan could
feel the direction of it at his back — a breath from indoors going
out to meet the country — and the direction told him that the
storage was *venting*, that its air, held for as long as its air
had been held, was being given back to the hillside and the
hillside was giving the hillside's air in.

He walked a half-turn to the center of the room.

He stood there and he let his proximity-sense widen.

It did not feel like widening. It felt like *admitting*. The sense
had already been full — it had been reading the room since the
passage — and what widened was his willingness to let it read. He
stood at the center of the room and stopped fending.

The room came in.

Not at force. The room did not press. The room was *available*,
and his body took availability for what it was, and the register
that had been braided along the shelves became the register of
the space. He stood inside a held thing. He had been inside rooms
that had held feeling — a back bench, a barge cabin, the cooperage
basement — and every one of those rooms had held human warmth or
human cold, the leavings of bodies. This room held neither. This
room held the *shape* of waiting without any person in the shape.
It was a waiting that had gone on for so long that the waiting had
taken the shape of the material.

*What was inside*, a piece of his mind said.

He let the piece say it. He did not finish the sentence. The
sentence did not need finishing. What was inside was what was
inside. The body that was reading it was the body that was reading
it. The two facts sat in the middle of the room and did not ask
anything of him.

His skin was at the new color.

He knew it by the walls. The walls around him had taken a low
warmth from him. He was at the center of a room that was, in a
register small enough to miss and wide enough to fill the room,
the color he was making.

Somewhere above the hill — very far up, past the ceiling of the
chamber, past the crest of the hill, past the blue of the morning
the day was having outside — a resonance was happening that he
could not hear. The direction of it arrived in his back the way
the venting in his back had arrived. There was an outward. There
was a receiving. Somewhere far, very far, something was receiving
what the hill was sending, and was sending back. The sending-back
came in under the room as a pressure his inner ear caught and
dismissed before his inner ear would name it.

He did not name it.

He did not have the geography of it. He had only the register: a
saying and an answer, at a distance the word *distance* was not
built for.

He drew breath.

He stood.

---

Maren's voice arrived behind him, at the passage.

"Davan."

Small. Not stressed. Just placing him.

"Here," he said, over his shoulder.

"Aldric."

"Here," Aldric said, from the far shelf.

A pause. The pause was Maren at the outside of the passage,
reading the inside of the passage the way she read a room by
ear-and-light before stepping into it.

"Vane is on the slope," she said. "She has scent. West. One man.
Far yet."

"How far," Aldric said. Not a question he expected to get a number
on. A way of setting the fact in the room.

"Far enough to be a later thing. She wanted you to know."

"Yes," Aldric said.

"I am here," Maren said. "The disc is seated. I am not moving."

"Good," Davan said.

Another pause. Then:

"Is it a place."

"It is a place," Davan said.

"I thought it would be."

"It has —" he said, and let the word not finish because the word
that wanted to come was *holdings* and *holdings* was a word that
wanted to be Aldric's for a minute before it was his.

Maren did not push.

"All right," she said. Small. From the passage.

---

Aldric rose.

He rose slowly, as a man in his fifties rose from both knees when
the knees had been on a cool hard floor for longer than the knees
would have chosen. His hands pushed on his thighs and he came up
to a stand with the small involuntary sound a body made when it
straightened. He turned. He looked at Davan.

His face was the face he had worn at the camp at the edge of
Irongate country, on the other side of the small lamp. Only more
of it.

"They're records," he said.

"Yes," Davan said.

"All of them."

"Yes."

"Nothing else. No instruments. No —" his hand moved and stopped
and came back to his thigh. "No *control* of anything. No lever.
No dial. No — weapon, though I do not know why I expected a
weapon. No tool at all except these." He gestured at the shelves.
"The whole room is one thing."

"Yes."

"They are records."

He said it the third time the way a man said a thing when he was
making the saying itself a vessel, carrying the saying from the
place the saying had landed in his body to the place the saying
needed to go in order to become a thing he had said to another
person. Davan held the vessel with him. He did not interrupt it.
Aldric's hand had gone inside his robe, had found the cord of the
lamp he was not carrying, had not been there, had come back out
without the tic finishing. Davan registered this and did not say
it.

"It is," Aldric said, softer, "all of what was kept. Not what was
held to be used. What was held to be *kept.*"

"Yes."

"Oh," Aldric said.

It was, Davan thought, the smallest and most accurate sound a
priest had made in a long while.

---

Davan crossed back to the shelf where his palm had been.

He did not put his palm back against the material. He put it
near, again — the same hand's-breadth. The material did not give
his color back this time. It had given it back and the giving
was done and the color was in him, not in the wall. His skin
held steady at the register the wall had taught him, and the
wall held at its own register — quieter now, a little — and the
two of them were at separate registers and the separation was
correct.

He chose one of the objects on the shelf.

Not to pick up. To put a finger on. He chose the one nearest the
center of the row, because the center of a row was where a row's
attention was most evenly distributed and because he did not want
to be the person who chose an end. He extended one finger of his
right hand — his carving finger, the one that had known the
inner-orbit shape before his mind had — and he set the pad of it
against the flat side of the object. Very lightly.

The object was neither warm nor cold. It was at the same register
as the walls. It took his fingerprint's oil the way the wall would
have taken it, and gave nothing back, and was what it was.

His pad was on the object. He kept it there.

He did not lift it. He did not press harder. He kept the contact
at exactly the register at which two things could be, at once,
two things — the object still the object, his finger still his
finger, the material the material, the body the body.

A held point.

His skin at the wrist was at the new color and the new color was
not fading.

Aldric stood at the far shelf and did not cross to him and did
not speak. The room was the room.

Outside, through the length of the passage, through the breach,
through the morning where Maren's thumb was on the disc and Vane
was on the slope with a scent and Edvar was at the cart below with
the horses — the country was doing whatever the country did.
Davan did not go to find out.

The storage held.

He held with it.

And he held his pad on the object, and did not take it off, and
did not turn it over, and did not look to see if there was a
marking on the underside, and did not carry it back to the passage
for Aldric, and did not claim it, and did not pick it up.

He stayed at the shelf, with his finger on the one kept thing his
finger had chosen, and waited for Aldric to be ready to begin.
